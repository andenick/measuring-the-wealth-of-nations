## Tables Extracted

### Chapter 25: Labour Cost and Structure of Earnings

**Table 25.2 — Monthly average gross wage and annual average gross earnings by sex and educational attainment, 2006 and 2010**
- File: `chunk_013_table01_gross_wage_by_sex_education_2006_2010.csv`
- Coverage: Total, Males, Females × 5 education levels (ISCED 1–8)
- Columns: distribution %, monthly paid hours, hourly avg gross wage (TL), monthly avg gross wage (TL), annual avg gross earnings (TL)
- Key finding: Higher education workers earned ~2.7× more than primary-school-and-below workers in both years; females with higher education earned TL 2,380/month vs TL 2,842 for males in 2010

**Table 25.3 — Monthly average gross wage and annual average gross earnings by sex and age group, 2006 and 2010**
- File: `chunk_013_table02_gross_wage_by_sex_age_group_2006_2010.csv`
- Coverage: Total, Males, Females × 9 age groups (under 16 through 60+)
- Key finding: Wages rise steeply with age; 60+ workers earned ~2× the wage of 16-19 workers; females aged 60+ earned noticeably less than males

**Table 25.4 — Monthly average gross wage and annual average gross earnings by sex and managerial responsibility, 2006 and 2010**
- File: `chunk_013_table03_gross_wage_by_sex_managerial_responsibility_2006_2010.csv`
- Coverage: Total, Males, Females × with/without managerial responsibility
- Key finding: Managers earned ~2× non-managers; female managers earned slightly more than male managers in some years (e.g. TL 2,936 vs TL 2,783 monthly in 2010)

**Table 25.5 — Hourly labour cost index, 2008–2012**
- File: `chunk_013_table04_hourly_labour_cost_index_2008_2012.csv`
- Coverage: NACE Rev.2 sections B–N; base year 2008=100
- Key finding: Total index rose from 100.0 to 141.8 over 2008–2012 (~42%); Professional/scientific/technical (M) had highest growth at 173.1 by 2012; Water/sewerage (E) also high at 167.7

**Table 25.6 — Hourly earnings index, 2008–2012**
- File: `chunk_013_table05_hourly_earnings_index_2008_2012.csv`
- Coverage: NACE Rev.2 sections B–N; base year 2008=100
- Key finding: Total earnings index rose to 144.7 by 2012; Professional/scientific (M) highest at 178.4; consistently faster growth than labour cost index in most sectors

**Table 25.7 — Hourly labour cost excluding earnings index, 2008–2012**
- File: `chunk_013_table06_hourly_labour_cost_excl_earnings_index_2008_2012.csv`
- Coverage: NACE Rev.2 sections B–N; base year 2008=100
- Key finding: Non-earnings costs grew more slowly (total 130.3 in 2012); Mining (B) showed a dip in 2009-2010 before recovering; Information/communication (J) showed almost flat trend (114.8)

### Chapter 26: Science, Technology and Information Communication

**Table 26.1 — Gross domestic expenditure on R&D, 2007–2011**
- File: `chunk_013_table07_RD_expenditure_2007_2011.csv`
- Coverage: Total GERD in TL, PPP USD, USD; GERD/GDP ratio; per capita GERD; by sector (business, government, higher education)
- Key finding: GERD rose from TL 6,091M (2007) to TL 11,154M (2011); GERD/GDP rose from 7.1‰ to 8.6‰; higher education sector was largest throughout

**Table 26.2 — R&D personnel by sector and occupation, 2007–2011**
- File: `chunk_013_table08_RD_personnel_by_sector_occupation_2007_2011.csv`
- Coverage: Headcount and FTE × business/government/higher education × researcher/technician/other
- Key finding: Total headcount grew from 119,738 to 164,287 (2007–2011); business enterprise researchers grew fastest (17,055 → 35,191); higher education dominated headcount throughout

**Table 26.3 — GERD by type of expenditure and source of funds, 2010–2011**
- File: `chunk_013_table09_RD_expenditure_by_type_source_2010_2011.csv`
- Coverage: Current vs capital; labour vs other current; instruments vs land/buildings; domestic vs foreign funds by sector
- Key finding: Labour costs accounted for ~51% of total GERD in both years; domestic government funding was second largest source after own-sector business funding

**Table 26.4 — R&D personnel by sector and education level, 2010–2011**
- File: `chunk_013_table10_RD_personnel_by_sector_education_2010_2011.csv`
- Coverage: Headcount and FTE × business/government/higher education × 6 education levels
- Key finding: Higher education sector dominated PhD holders (46,972 of 49,625 in 2010); business enterprises concentrated at graduate level (23,560 headcount in 2010)

## Methodological Notes

- Labour cost and earnings data sourced from Labour Cost Survey (Frascati-standard methodology for R&D data)
- ISCED-1997 classification used for educational attainment
- NACE Rev.2 used for sectoral breakdowns
- Wage data in nominal Turkish Liras (TL)
- R&D data follow OECD Frascati Manual standards
- FTE (Full-Time Equivalent) methodology: person-years calculated proportional to time spent on R&D
