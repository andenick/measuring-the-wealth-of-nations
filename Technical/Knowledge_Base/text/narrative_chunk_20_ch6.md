## Summary

This chunk continues Section 6.2.2 with detailed analysis of aggregate money value estimates by LRA (1948), Eaton (1966), Mage (1963), and Shaikh (1978b). It includes three critical tables and three comparative figures showing different approaches to mapping Marxian categories to national accounts.

**Key contributions reviewed**:

1. **LRA (1948)**: Early discussion of general correspondence, but confused terminology
2. **Eaton (1966)**: Sophisticated theoretical approach, close to authors' revenue-side measures
3. **Mage (1963)**: Pioneering aggregate analysis, but restricts surplus value to property income only
4. **Shaikh (1978b)**: First systematic derivation, essentially same as authors' revenue-side approach

**Major findings**:
- Census-based sectoral estimates rise 66% faster than aggregate estimates (1948-1984)
- NIPA-based sectoral rate ~55% of aggregate rate (relatively stable)
- Mage's narrow definition produces rate of surplus value ~0.20-0.40 (flat)
- Shaikh (1978b) produces intermediate estimates ~1.40-1.80
- Shaikh & Tonak (this book) produces ~1.70-2.40 (rising)

## Significance for NSW Project

This chunk establishes the **methodological foundations** for the book's empirical work:

1. **Comprehensive surplus value definition** includes taxes, circulation costs - essential for NSW analysis
2. **Double-entry IO framework** prevents items from "disappearing" from accounts
3. **Proper treatment of government** sector distinguishes productive from unproductive activities
4. **Historical context** shows evolution of empirical Marxian economics

Our Phase 1 and Phase 2 work builds on Shaikh (1978b) foundation but adds:
- Full IO-based double-entry accounting
- Product-side as well as revenue-side
- Agriculture and government enterprises included
- Building rentals properly classified in trade
- Wage equivalent properly included in total wages
