## Main Topics:
1. National Accounting and Imputations
2. Investment and National Product
3. Nonproduction Activities and the Social Fabric

## Key Quantitative Data:

### Income Distribution Comparison (1966):
**Official GNP Accounts:**
- Labor income share: 82.6%
- Capital income share: 24.3%

**Jorgenson-Fraumeni Extended Accounts:**
- Labor income share: 93%
- Property income share: 7%

*Note: Extended accounts include imputations for household durable goods, household production, and leisure time, raising total household income over fivefold.*

## Theoretical Concepts:

### 1. National Accounting Extensions
- Extension of production measures to nonmarket activities requires corresponding imputations on income side
- Imputations significantly alter distribution of income between:
  - Capital and labor
  - Employed and unemployed
  - Other categories

### 2. Investment Definition Debates
**Items considered for inclusion in investment:**
- Net changes in consumer and government durables
- Changes in land value
- Expenditures for natural resource development and discovery
- Research and Development (R&D) expenditures
- Human capital investments (health, education, training, information)

**Haig-Simon-Hicks Definition:**
- Income = what can be consumed without changing real wealth
- Real income, savings, and investment must include net monetary revaluations in stocks

### 3. Classification of Public Services
**Activities Discussed:**
- Police protection
- Fire protection
- Guard services
- National defense

**Alternative Classifications:**
1. **Marxian View (Shaikh & Tonak):** Nonproduction activities, excluded from total product
2. **Kuznets View:** "Intermediate inputs" or "mere cost of maintaining the social fabric"
3. **Implication:** These are precondition for net product rather than net product itself
