## Summary

This chunk continues the critical analysis of empirical studies, focusing on Section 6.2.1 "Sectoral money value estimates." It reviews major studies from Varga (1928, 1935) through Shaikh (1992), examining their methodologies, findings, and limitations.

**Key studies reviewed**:
- **Varga (1928, 1935)**: Pioneer methodology - V* = production worker wages, S* = value added minus V*
- **Mandel (1975)**: Extended Varga with broader V* definitions
- **Perlo (1974)**: Incorporated tax adjustments, "real social wage cost"
- **Cuneo (1978, 1982)**: Canadian manufacturing application
- **Amsden (1981)**: International comparison across development levels
- **Shaikh (1992a)**: Long series 1899-1984
- **Gillman (1958)**: Property-type income focus
- **Labor Research Association (1948)**: Confused terminology issues

**Major findings**:
1. Sectoral estimates systematically understate aggregate rate (value transfers to trade sector)
2. Census data vs. NIPA data create significant discrepancies (19% GVA difference, 20% wage difference in 1970)
3. Combined effects: Census-based sectoral estimates have sharper upward trend than aggregate estimates
4. All studies show rising rate of surplus value in postwar period

## Significance for NSW Project

This chunk provides critical context for understanding:
1. How different data sources (Census vs. NIPA vs. IO tables) affect estimates
2. Why sectoral estimates differ from aggregate estimates (value transfers)
3. The evolution of empirical Marxian economics methodology
4. Validation that postwar rate of surplus value rose substantially (confirmed across multiple independent studies)

Our Phase 1 and Phase 2 work uses **aggregate NIPA-IO approach**, avoiding the pitfalls of sectoral Census-based estimates while maintaining the productive/unproductive labor distinction that these studies pioneered.
