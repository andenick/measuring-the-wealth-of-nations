## Section 5.3: Employment, Wages, and Variable Capital (Pages 108-111)

### Overview (Page 108)

**Transition from IO to NIPA data**:
- IO tables provide benchmark years only
- NIPA provides annual employment and wage data
- Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) provides production/nonproduction breakdowns

**Primary data sources**:
- **Employment (L)**: NIPA "persons engaged in production" (PEP)
  - Includes full-time equivalent employees (FEE) + self-employed persons (SEP)
- **Wages**: NIPA "employee compensation" (EC)
  - Includes wages, salaries, employer social security contributions
  - EC represents **total cost of labor power to capitalist**

**Key challenge**:
- NIPA does not distinguish production vs. nonproduction workers
- BLS provides this breakdown by sector
- **Solution**: Use BLS ratios applied to NIPA totals

### Methodology: Total Labor and Productive Labor (Pages 108-109)

**Definition**:
> "Productive labor is the production labor employed in capitalist production sectors: agriculture, mining, construction, transportation and public utilities, manufacturing, and productive services (defined as all services except business services, legal services, and private households, as in Table E.1)." (p. 108-109)

**What is excluded**:
1. Nonproduction labor (sales, etc.) in production sectors
2. All labor in nonproduction sectors (trade, finance)

**Total unproductive labor** = nonproduction workers in production sectors + all workers in nonproduction sectors

**Notation** (Page 109):

Production sectors: j = 1, ..., k
Nonproduction sectors: j = k+1, ..., n

**Calculation Formulas**:

```
Lj = total employment in jth sector (from NIPA)
   = "persons engaged in production" (PEP)
   = full-time equivalent employees (FEE) + self-employed persons (SEP)

L = ΣLj = total labor

(Lp/L)j = ratio of production/total workers in jth production sector, j = 1,...,k (BLS)

(Lp)j = (Lp/L)j · (Lj) = estimated production worker employment in jth production sector

Lp = Σ(Lp)j = total productive labor

Lu = L - Lp = total unproductive labor
```

**Footnote 7** (Page 109):
Notes asymmetry between production and nonproduction sectors:
- Production sectors always have nonproduction activities (buying, selling, finance)
- Nonproduction sectors have no systematic reason to incorporate production activities
- Data limitations prevent further breakdown of nonproduction sectors

**Footnote 8** (Page 109):
- Productive service sectors estimated by ratio method
- Ratio of GNP(productive services)/GNP(total services) × total employment in services
- See Tables E.1 and F.1

**Footnote 9** (Page 109):
- PEP is standard NIPA series
- Inclusion of SEP alongside FEE suggests self-employed are largely full-time

### Methodology: Total Wages and Variable Capital (Page 111, beginning)

**Objective**:
- Measure total cost of hiring labor power
- Must include supplements (social security, pension contributions)
- NIPA "employee compensation" (EC) is appropriate base

**Two adjustments needed**:

1. **Self-employed persons adjustment**:
   - EC covers only employees
   - L includes employees + self-employed (SEP)
   - Need wage equivalent estimate for SEP

2. **Corporate officers adjustment** (Footnote 10, page 111):
   - EC includes salaries of corporate officers (COS) and supplements
   - Following Mage (1963): these are capitalist income, not wages
   - Must be excluded (see Appendix G)

**Integration of BLS and NIPA data**:
- BLS: production/nonproduction ratios by sector
- NIPA: total employment and wage totals (tied to value-added estimates)
- Combination retains NIPA totals while using BLS distributional information

**Calculation approach** (outlined on page 108):

1. Calculate (Lp/L)j ratios from BLS for each production sector
2. Apply ratios to NIPA employment Lj to split into components
3. Estimate wage per production worker using BLS+NIPA
4. Apply to Lp estimate to derive total wage bill of productive labor (V*)
5. Derive Wu = W - V* (unproductive wage bill) as residual

**Result**:
- Retains NIPA total employment and wage estimates
- Incorporates BLS production/nonproduction distinction
- Maintains consistency with value-added and final-demand estimates

### Key Results Referenced (Page 108)

**Figure 5.5 vs. Figure 5.9 contrast**:
- Figure 5.5: GOp/TV* and GOtt/TV* shares **stable** over postwar period

- Evidence of rising productivity of productive workers

### Data Sources Summary

**NIPA**:
- Persons engaged in production (PEP) = L
- Employee compensation (EC) = base wage measure
- Tied to GVA and final demand estimates

**BLS**:
- Production vs. total employment ratios by sector
- Source: *Employment and Training Report of the President* (BLS 1981)
- Two employment types per sector:
  1. Total employment
  2. Production and nonsupervisory workers
- In production sectors, (2) ≈ production workers (Footnote 6, page 108)
