# Page 140 - Comparison of Key Marxian and Orthodox Measures

## Key Findings from Table 5.14:

### Variable Definitions:
- TP*/GP: Total Product (Marxian) vs Gross Product
- TP*/GNP: Total Product (Marxian) vs GNP
- Lp/L: Productive labor ratio
- V*/W: Variable capital (Marxian) vs Wages
- S*/P: Surplus value (Marxian) vs Profits
- C*/V*: Value composition of capital (Marxian)
- M/EC: Intermediate inputs vs Employee Compensation
- S*/V*: Rate of surplus value (Marxian)
- P+/EC: Profits vs Employee Compensation
- q*/y: Productivity measures

### Key Observations:
1. **Profit Trends**:
   - Profit fell steadily until 1980
   - Reagan-Bush policies accelerated surplus value growth
   - Reversed postwar profit trend (though still lower than beginning)

2. **Labor Composition**:
   - Lu/Lp (unproductive to productive labor ratio) rose dramatically
   - This affected surplus value absorption by unproductive expenses

3. **Resulting Effects**:
   - NIPA-based profit rate declined more rapidly than Marxian rate
   - Corporate profit rate also declined more rapidly
   - Conventional productivity measure rises much slower than Marxian
   - Explains "productivity growth slowdown"

### Central Conclusion:
"The theoretical difference between Marxian and orthodox economic analysis is reflected in a fundamentally different empirical picture of capitalist reality."
