# Page 130 - Figure 5.7: Total Labor and Productive Labor Trends

## Chart Description:
Figure 5.7 shows trends in total labor (L) and productive labor (Lp) from 1948 to 1988

### Data Visualization:
- **X-axis**: Years (1948-1988)
- **Y-axis**: Number of individuals in thousands (20,000 to 120,000)
- **Two lines**:
  1. L (Total labor) - solid line
  2. Lp (Productive labor) - dashed line

### Key Trends:
1. **Total Labor (L)**:
   - 1948: ~58,000
   - 1988: >110,000
   - Significant upward trend

2. **Productive Labor (Lp)**:
   - 1948: ~33,000
   - 1988: ~41,000
   - Much slower, stable growth

### Ratio Analysis:
- **Lp/L ratio**: Declined by more than 37% over postwar period (Figure 5.9)
- **Lu/Lp ratio**: Rose sharply by almost 138% over same period (Figure 5.11)

## Wage Measurement Discussion:
### Employee Compensation (EC):
- NIPA measure of employee compensation is starting point
- Must adjust for self-employed wage equivalents
- Includes supplements to wages (social security, pension funds)

### Note on Corporate Officer Salaries (COS):
- Employee compensation includes COS and supplements
- Mage (1963) excludes COS as capitalist income, not wages
- Authors make similar adjustment (see Appendix G)
