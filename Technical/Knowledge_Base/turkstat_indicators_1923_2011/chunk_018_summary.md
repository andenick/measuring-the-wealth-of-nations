## Content Overview

This chunk covers the debt-side of Chapter 19, presenting domestic debt stock composition indicators and comprehensive gross external debt data for Turkey across multiple decades. Two chart-only pages (pp. 650, 683) with no extractable numerical data beyond reference to other tables are noted but not extracted.

## Tables

### Table 01: Domestic Debt Stock Indicators — Old Series (1980–2006)
`chunk_018_table01_domestic_debt_stock_indicators_old_series.csv`
- Shares of domestic debt stock by instrument: government bonds, treasury bills, central bank advances, consolidated debt, FX differences
- Key shift: central bank advances fell from 27% (1980) to 0% (1998+); treasury bills dominated mid-1990s; government bonds dominated from 1999 onward (74–79%)

### Table 02: Domestic Debt Stock Indicators — New Series (1998–2011)
`chunk_018_table02_domestic_debt_stock_indicators_new_series.csv`
- Total domestic debt stock/GDP: peaked 50.9% (2001 crisis) → fell to 28.4% (2011)
- Government bonds/GDP and treasury bills/GDP decomposition
- By 2011: domestic debt was 100% government bonds, 0% treasury bills

### Table 03: External Debt Indicators (1965–1996)
`chunk_018_table03_external_debt_indicators.csv`
- Debt service/exports, debt service/GNP, external debt stock/exports, external debt stock/GNP
- Short-term debt as % of external debt
- Annual rates of change (total, short-term, medium-term)
- Notable: external debt/GNP peaked ~48% (1994), debt service/exports peaked 95% (1980)

### Table 04: Gross External Debt Profile (1989–2011)
`chunk_018_table04_gross_external_debt_profile.csv`
- Total gross external debt by sector: public, CBRT, private (short + long term)
- Total (Million USD): 43,911 (1989) → 306,704 (2011)
- Gross external debt/GDP: peaked 57.7% (2001) → 39.6% (2011)
- Private sector surpassed public as dominant debtor from mid-2000s onward

### Table 05: External Debt by Borrower — 1989–1994
`chunk_018_table05_external_debt_by_borrower_1989_1994.csv`
- Disaggregated by borrower type: central government, local administrations, SOEs, public banks, CBRT, private banks, private non-financial
- Central government long-term debt: 23,056 (1989) → 33,404 (1994) Mn USD

### Table 06: External Debt by Borrower — 2007–2011
`chunk_018_table06_external_debt_by_borrower_2007_2011.csv`
- Same disaggregation for 2007–2011
- Private sector dominant: 161,086 → 202,724 Mn USD (2007–2011)
- Private non-financial corporates: 92,999 → 107,802 Mn USD

### Table 07: External Debt by Lender — 1989–1994
`chunk_018_table07_external_debt_by_lender_1989_1994.csv`
- Creditor breakdown: official vs. private; monetary vs. non-monetary; multilateral organizations
- IBRD exposure: 5,691 (1989) → 4,987 (1994) Mn USD
- Bond issuances: 2,560 (1989) → 11,946 (1994) Mn USD

### Table 08: External Debt by Lender — 2006–2011
`chunk_018_table08_external_debt_by_lender_2006_2011.csv`
- Long-term private creditors dominated: 99,448 → 130,245 Mn USD (2006–2011)
- IMF exposure declined: 10,759 → 4,361 Mn USD (2006–2011)
- IBRD grew: 6,855 → 12,224 Mn USD
- Bonds grew: 36,347 → 50,531 Mn USD

## Relevance to ST2 / Labor Share

- **Low direct relevance** to labor share or compensation of employees. No "ücret," "istihdam," "GSYH" (GDP level), or national accounts data in this chunk.
- The domestic debt/GDP series (Table 02) and external debt/GDP ratios are useful **macroeconomic context** for understanding debt burdens that compressed wage shares in the 1990s–2000s.
- The domestic debt stock composition shift (away from monetized central bank advances toward market instruments) documents the financialization trajectory relevant to functional income distribution analysis.
