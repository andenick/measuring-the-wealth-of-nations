# Figure 1: Productive Labour for Capital

## Description
A hierarchical flowchart diagram illustrating the classification of economic activities as productive and unproductive labour for capital. The diagram shows the decision tree for determining what constitutes productive labour under capitalism.

## Structure

**Top Level: Labour**
- Branches into two main categories

**Second Level:**
1. **Production of Use-Values** (self-subsistence farming, housework, etc.)
2. **Production of Commodities**

**Third Level (under Production of Commodities):**
1. **Petty Commodity Production** (artisans, smallholding peasants, etc.)
2. **Production based on Wage-Labour**

**Fourth Level (under Production based on Wage-Labour):**
1. **Wage-Labour paid out of revenue** (domestic servants, cooks, gardeners, chauffeurs, bodyguards, etc.)
2. **Wage-Labour employed by capital**

**Fifth Level (under Wage-Labour employed by capital):**
1. **Workers in Circulation** (employees of banks, insurance and mortgage companies, commercial workers)
2. **Workers in production and transportation** - **Productive Labour for Capital**

## Purpose
This diagram was laid out in Savran and Tonak (1999) to classify economic activities and clarify what should be regarded as labour within social reproduction, distinguishing productive from unproductive labour based on specific criteria.

## Key Criteria for Productive Labour
The diagram illustrates three necessary criteria for productive labour to exist under capitalism:
1. The labour must be involved in a productive activity
2. The labour must produce commodities
3. The labour power must enter into exchange with capital for surplus-value production
