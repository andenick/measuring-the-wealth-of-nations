# Figure 8: The Rate of Profit of the G20, 1950-2019

## Source Information
- **Location**: Page 366
- **Author**: Roberts, M. (2022)
- **Data Source**: Penn World Tables 10.0
- **Figure Type**: Line graph

## Visual Description

### Chart Characteristics
- **Chart Type**: Time series line graph
- **Time Period**: 1950-2019 (69 years)
- **Y-Axis**: Rate of profit (percentage)
- **Y-Axis Range**: Approximately 6.0% to 10.5%
- **X-Axis**: Years (1950-2019)

### Key Visual Features
- Continuous line showing profit rate trajectory over time
- Multiple labeled periods marked on the graph
- Dashed vertical lines separating distinct economic periods

## Identified Historical Periods

### 1. Golden Age (1950-early 1960s)
- **Starting Rate**: Approximately 9.5%
- **Peak**: 10.3% (early 1960s)
- **Characteristics**: High and relatively stable profit rates

### 2. Profitability Crisis (mid-1960s to early 1980s)
- **Trend**: Sharp decline in profit rates
- **Low Point**: 6.6% (early 1980s)
- **Characteristics**: Sustained downward trajectory

### 3. Neoliberal Period (1980s-2007)
- **Trend**: Recovery and fluctuation
- **Peak**: 7.8% (late 1990s/early 2000s)
- **Characteristics**: Partial recovery but not to Golden Age levels

### 4. Long Depression (2008-2019)
- **Starting Point**: Approximately 7.4% (2007)
- **Low Point**: 6.4% (around 2013)
- **End Point**: Approximately 6.8% (2019)
- **Characteristics**: Lower average profit rates, sluggish recovery

## Key Data Points
- **1950s (Golden Age)**: ~9.5-10%
- **Early 1960s (Peak)**: 10.3%
- **Early 1980s (Trough)**: 6.6%
- **Late 1990s/Early 2000s**: 7.8%
- **2007 (Pre-crisis)**: 7.4%
- **2013 (Post-2008 low)**: 6.4%
- **2019 (End of series)**: ~6.8%

## Trends and Observations

### Long-Term Trend
- **Overall Pattern**: Declining rate of profit from 1950 to 2019
- **Total Decline**: From ~10.3% (peak) to ~6.8% (2019) = approximately 34% reduction
- **Average Decline**: Sustained downward pressure despite cyclical recoveries

### Cyclical Patterns
1. **Golden Age Stability**: Relatively high and stable profit rates
2. **Crisis and Decline**: Sharp fall during profitability crisis (1960s-1980s)
3. **Partial Recovery**: Neoliberal period shows recovery but fails to reach Golden Age levels
4. **New Depression**: Post-2008 shows renewed decline and stagnation

### Period Comparisons
- **Golden Age average**: ~9.5-10.3%
- **Neoliberal period average**: ~7-7.8%
- **Long Depression average**: ~6.4-7.4%

## Theoretical Significance

### Supporting Marx's Law
This graph provides empirical evidence for Marx's law of the tendency of the profit rate to fall, showing:
1. **Long-term decline**: Overall downward trajectory over 69 years
2. **Counteracting tendencies**: Temporary recoveries (neoliberal period)
3. **Reassertion of tendency**: Despite recoveries, profit rate continues structural decline

### Crisis Periods
The graph visually correlates declining profit rates with major crisis periods:
- **1970s**: Sharp decline coinciding with oil crises and stagflation
- **2008**: Beginning of Third Great Depression
- **Post-2008**: Persistent low profit rates despite massive stimulus

## Methodological Notes

### Data Source Limitations
As noted by Roberts (2022):
- Penn World Tables 10.0 database used
- **No distinction** between productive and unproductive activities
- Uses "internal rate of return on capital stock (IRR)" as proxy
- IRR serves as "a good proxy for the Marxian rate of profit"

### Implications of Methodology
- Not a pure Marxian calculation (doesn't separate productive/unproductive labor)
- Nevertheless shows clear tendential fall consistent with Marxian theory
- Conservative estimate (actual Marxian rate might show steeper decline)
