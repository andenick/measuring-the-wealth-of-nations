## Conceptual Mathematical Relationships

### 1. Rate of Profit (p. 363)

**Conceptual Definition**:
> "The rate of profit is the ratio of surplus-value to overall capital."

**Mathematical Expression** (not in text, but implied):
```
Rate of Profit = Surplus-Value / Total Capital
```

or in Marxian notation:
```
r = s / (c + v)
```

Where:
- r = rate of profit
- s = surplus-value
- c = constant capital
- v = variable capital (wages)

**Context**: This is the central variable under discussion throughout the chapter. The entire analysis concerns why this ratio tends to fall over time.

---

### 2. Organic Composition of Capital (p. 363)

**Conceptual Definition**:
> "What Marx calls the technical composition of capital and the organic composition of capital (the difference need not detain us here), i.e., the ratio of constant capital to living labour"

**Mathematical Expression** (not in text, but implied):
```
Organic Composition = Constant Capital / Variable Capital
```

or:
```
OCC = c / v
```

**Context**: Rising organic composition is the key mechanism driving the falling rate of profit. As machinery (c) increases relative to labor (v), the rate of profit falls because only labor produces surplus-value.

---

### 3. Mechanics of the Falling Rate of Profit (p. 363)

**Conceptual Description**:
> "Because constant capital (machinery, etc.) increases faster than living labour and because any given amount of living labour can only create a corresponding amount of value, the denominator increases faster than the numerator, thus bringing down the rate of profit."

**Mathematical Logic**:
```
If c increases faster than v,
and s is proportional to v,
then (c + v) increases faster than s,
therefore r = s/(c + v) falls
```

**Counteracting Tendency**:
> "Of course, since the entire operation was started to increase the relative surplus-value, the numerator will not remain constant but increase as well."

```
Rate of exploitation = s/v increases
But OCC = c/v increases faster
Therefore r = s/(c + v) still tends to fall
```

---

### 4. Production of Absolute Surplus-Value (p. 362)

**Conceptual Description**:
> "The production of absolute surplus-value does not require any changes in the techniques or methods of production; it is based simply on lengthening the workday. The longer the workday, the more labour the worker will have to expend, and, given the wage rate, the more surplus-value will be produced as a result."

**Implied Relationship**:
```
Surplus-Value ∝ Length of Workday (holding wage rate constant)
```

---

### 5. Production of Relative Surplus-Value (p. 362-363)

**Conceptual Description**:
> "As consumption goods cheapen, the worker will have to spend less of his or her time to reproduce an amount of value equivalent to his or her wages and thus will spend a longer part of the (unchanged) workday in the production of surplus-value."

**Implied Relationship**:
```
If productivity ↑ → cost of wage goods ↓
→ necessary labor time ↓
→ surplus labor time ↑ (workday constant)
→ surplus-value ↑
```

---

### 6. Competition and Individual Value (p. 364)

**Conceptual Description**:
> "In order to outbid its competitors, a firm will invent a new technique or method of production that increases the productivity of its labour force. This means that the individual value of its goods (or services) will cost a lower amount of labour than the comparable goods (or services) of its competitors"

**Implied Relationship**:
```
Individual Value = Labor Time Required by Individual Firm
Social Value = Average Labor Time Required

If Individual Value < Social Value:
→ Firm can lower price and still make profit
→ Competitive advantage
```

## Temporal Dynamics Described (No Equations)

### Speed of Growth Comparison (p. 363)

**Critical Relationship**:
> "Thus, the outcome will be decided by which grows faster: the productivity of labour or the organic composition of capital."

**Logic**:
- If productivity growth > OCC growth → rate of profit may rise
- If OCC growth > productivity growth → rate of profit falls
- "But as the investment that must be made in order to extract additional surplus-value increases with more and more advanced technology, at a certain stage organic composition will overcome the counteracting tendency and the rate of profit will start to fall."

---

## Theoretical Framework (Qualitative)

### The Fundamental Contradiction (p. 363)

**Statement**:
> "As capital strives to increase the amount of surplus-value, it is thereby ejecting from the production process the very source of value, i.e., labour."

**Logical Structure**:
1. Only living labor creates value
2. Capital accumulation increases c/v ratio
3. Therefore, source of value (labor) shrinks relative to capital
4. Therefore, rate of profit (s/[c+v]) falls
