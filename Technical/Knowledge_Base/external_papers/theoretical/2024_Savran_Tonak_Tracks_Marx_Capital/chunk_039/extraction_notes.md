### Law of Falling Rate of Profit (continued from chunk 038, pages 361-366)

**Marx's Characterization** (Page 362):
> "Marx characterises it as the 'most important law of modern political economy'" (Marx, 1993)

**Tendential Law** (Page 362):
- Constantly subject to: modification, attenuation, even arrestation
- Impact of counteracting tendencies
- Whether asserts predominant tendency → depends on mutual impact
- **Force of predominant tendency**: "So powerful that, sooner or later, it will assert itself unstoppably"

**Two Levels of Explanation** (Page 362):

1. **Volume One of Capital**: Relationship between capital/wage labour in isolation
2. **Volume Three of Capital**: Sphere of competition between capitals

**Relative Surplus-Value Mechanism** (Pages 362-363):

**Two Methods of Surplus-Value Production**:

1. **Absolute Surplus-Value**:
   - No changes in techniques/methods
   - Based on lengthening workday
   - Longer workday → more labour expended → more surplus-value

2. **Relative Surplus-Value**:
   - Changes in technical basis/methods of production
   - Rise in productive power of labour from:
     - New scientific research
     - Discovery of new materials
     - Application of science to technology
     - New production methods
   - Spread economy-wide → smaller labour amounts for each good
   - Includes worker consumption goods → consumption cheapens
   - Worker spends less time reproducing wage value
   - → Longer workday portion for surplus-value production

**Communist Manifesto Basis** (Page 363):
> "Owners of capital 'cannot exist without constantly revolutionising the instruments of production'" (Marx & Engels, 2010a)

**The Contradiction** (Page 363):

**Capital's Search**:
- Constantly develops new techniques/materials/methods
- Increase labour productivity
- Higher surplus-value (profit)

**Problem Created**:
- Advancement usually requires new machines/expensive materials
- **Constant capital increases** (plant, machinery, equipment, energy, etc.)
- Increases relative to living labour

**Key Concepts**:
- **Technical composition of capital**: Ratio constant capital to living labour
- **Organic composition of capital**: Same (difference not relevant here)
- Both increase as capital develops

**The Falling Rate Dynamic** (Page 363):

**Basic Marxist Proposition**: Source of all value (and surplus-value) = **living labour**

**Paradox**:
- Capital strives to increase surplus-value
- Thereby **ejecting from production the very source of value** (labour)

**Rate of Profit Formula**: Surplus-value / Overall capital

**Mechanism**:
- Constant capital (machinery, etc.) increases faster than living labour
- Given living labour amount → can only create corresponding value amount
- **Denominator increases faster than numerator**
- → Rate of profit falls

**Counteracting Tendency** (Page 363):
- Numerator (surplus-value) also increases (entire operation's goal)
- This is THE important counteracting tendency

**Outcome Determination**:
- Which grows faster: **Productivity of labour** OR **Organic composition of capital**?
- As investment for additional surplus-value increases with advanced technology
- → At certain stage: Organic composition overcomes counteracting tendency
- → Rate of profit starts to fall

**Competition Explanation** (Pages 363-364):

**Same Process, Different Lens**:
- Not capital-labour production relations
- **Dynamics of competition between capitals**

**Price Competition via Technical Change** (Page 364):
- Most important factor in long run
- Firm invents new technique/method → increases labour force productivity
- Individual value of goods < competitors' goods
- → Firm can lower price
- Competitors' quandary:
  1. Keep same price → lose customers (exodus to cheaper competitor)
  2. Lower prices similarly → suffer losses vs competitor
- **Long-run solution**: Adopt same (or better) techniques to survive
- All firms bring prices to same level

**Consequences** (Page 364):
- Increases outlay on capital goods for all firms
- Leaves profits behind vs incremental modernization costs
- **Long-run outcome**: Drop in rate of profit
- When predominant tendency overcomes counteracting ones → profit rates fall

**Crisis of Overaccumulation** (Page 364):

**Capitalist Aim**: Highest rate of profit possible from given capital magnitude

**Fall in Profit Rate Impact**:
- Makes capitalists less willing to invest new capital
- Accumulate at slower pace
- Not enough surplus-value for expanded reproduction to continue

**Marxist Crisis Theory Conclusion**:
> "Neither one of overproduction nor of underconsumption, but a crisis of overaccumulation."

**Empirical Validation** (Pages 364-366):

**Historical Status** (Page 364):
> "Until half a century ago, the law of the tendency of the profit rate to fall was only a theory, albeit a very powerful one... It had never been tested empirically."

**Measurement Challenge** (Page 365):

**Explaining Periodic Recurrence** (Page 365):

**Unlike Underconsumption Theory**: This theory CAN explain periodic crises

**Mechanism**:
- Capital accumulation slows/stops
- Capitalist class + government take measures to raise profit rate
- Sometimes drastic economic policy changes
- Example: **Neoliberalism** = "strategy of atomising the global working class in order to raise the rate of surplus-value and, concomitantly, the rate of profit"

**State Form Changes** (Page 365):
> "Capitalists have no qualms about replacing the existing state form with one that can enact measures that are better positioned to restore the rate of profit."

**Repression Correlation**: "The more powerful the contradictions between the working and capitalist classes, the more repressive the regimes that result"

**Historical Example**: Hitler's Nazi regime post-1929 crash → brought world to "brink of total collapse" (except Soviet Union)

**Finance's Role** (Page 366):
- Can postpone worst outcomes

- Temporarily relieve economic units
- Prolong "day of reckoning when the bubbles burst"
- **2008 crisis**: "Excellent example of such reckonings"

**Conclusion** (Page 366):
> "The outward manifestation of a crisis is hardly ever directly indicative of the true causes of that crisis. A superficial interpretation misleads those who act to change the tide in favour of the working masses, the oppressed nations, and the wretched of the earth, leading to policies that are of little help to get us out of the doldrums."

### SECTION 5: Great Depressions as the Birth Pangs of a New Society (Pages 367-371)

**Opening Questions** (Pages 366-367):
- Why depressions (vs ordinary business cycles)?
- Why have depressions become most salient/destructive form over last 150 years?

**Answer**: "Marx's grand vision of historical change, of how humanity moves from one mode of production, one socioeconomic formation, to another"

**Historical Timeline** (Page 367):

**Capitalism Duration**:
- Europe: 250 years minimum
- More comprehensive: ~5 centuries (16th century, English capitalist agriculture)

**Great Depressions**: Last century and a half phenomenon

**Three Great Depressions** (Page 367):
1. **Long Depression**: 1873-1896
2. **Great Depression**: 1929-1948
3. **Third Great Depression**: Authors' assessment: Started 2008, "still in full swing"

**Business Cycles vs Great Depressions** (Page 367):

**Business Cycles**:
- Endemic to capitalism
- Short recession period
- Market forces + government policy → new expansion cycle

**Great Depressions**:
- Much longer (at least decade, often several)
- Necessarily international
- Require deep restructuring: economic, political, ideological, military spheres
- Resolve contradictions causing profound disruptions

**Key Insight** (Page 367):
> "As capitalism progresses, economic crises become ever-more severe and longer lasting."

**Historical Stage** (Page 367):
- Great depressions NOT throughout entire capitalist history
- Product of **certain historical stage** of capitalist mode development

**Marx & Engels' Foresight** (Pages 368-369):

**Communist Manifesto (1848) Passage**:

> "Modern bourgeois society with its relations of production, of exchange and of property, a society that has conjured up such gigantic means of production and of exchange, is like the sorcerer who is no longer able to control the powers of the nether world whom he has called up by his spells."

> "It is enough to mention the commercial crises that by their periodic return put the existence of the entire bourgeois society on trial, **each time more threateningly** [emphasis added]."

> "The productive forces at the disposal of society no longer tend to further the development of the conditions of bourgeois property; on the contrary, they have become too powerful for these conditions, by which they are fettered, and no sooner do they overcome these fetters than they bring disorder into the whole of bourgeois society, endanger the existence of bourgeois property. The conditions of bourgeois society are too narrow to comprise the wealth created by them." (Marx & Engels, 2010a, 489-490)

**Authors' Emphasis on "Each Time More Threateningly"** (Page 368):
- Marx conscious even at early stage
- Economic crises become "ever-more severe and disruptive with the maturing of the capitalist mode of production"

**Explanation** (Page 368):
- Productive forces developed by capitalism
- Come into contradiction with mode of production that gave them life
- **More developed productive forces** → **greater contradiction** → **more severe crisis**

**Marx vs Schumpeter** (Pages 368-369):

**Economic Crisis's Unique Position in Marx**:
- Distinct from all rival theoretical treatments
- Even Schumpeter's "creative destruction" cannot match

**Difference**:
- **Schumpeter**: "Creative" = creation of new productive forces in vacuum
- **Marx**: "Creation" = creation of **new society**

**Great Depression Role** (Page 369):
> "Having gained the stature of 'great depression', crisis now takes on the part of a midwife that guides the transition from a system based on capitalist private property and the market to one based on public (or commonly held) property of the means of production and central planning."

**Dialectical Reversal** (Page 369):
> "As an ultimate dialectical reversal, the wasteland created by crisis is expected to give birth to a civilisation that is at once dynamic, constructive and fair."

**1859 Preface to *A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy*** (Page 369):

> "In the social production of their existence, men inevitably enter into definite relations, which are independent of their will, namely relations of production appropriate to a given stage in the development of their material forces of production. The totality of these relations of production constitutes the economic structure of society, the real foundation, on which arises a legal and political superstructure and to which correspond definite forms of social consciousness."

> "At a certain stage of development, the material productive forces of society come into conflict with the existing relations of production or – this merely expresses the same thing in legal terms – with the property relations within the framework of which they have operated hitherto. From forms of development of the productive forces these relations turn into their fetters. **Then begins an era of social revolution.**" (Marx, 2010b, 263)

**Feudalism to Capitalism Transition** (Page 369):

**Feudal Fetters**:
- Lord-serf relationship (countryside)
- Master-apprentice relationship (medieval corporation stifling rules)
- Became fetters on productive forces potential

**Contradiction**:
- Rising bourgeoisie + free cities
- vs Classes of feudal society with vested interests

**Result**:
- Struggle → head-on clash
- Forces of new society vs absolutist state of late feudal society
- → "Era of social revolution"

**Democratic Revolutions** (Pages 369-370):
- English Revolution (1640)
- US Revolution (late 18th century)
- French Revolution (late 18th century)
- Latin American revolutions (early 19th century)
- **Result**: New capitalist-bourgeois society gradually replaced old feudal structure
- 1848 revolutions: Capitalism expansion across Europe
- Later: Rest of world (requires separate treatment)

**Same Contradiction Now** (Page 370):
> "It is this same type of contradiction between the productive forces and the social relations of capitalist society, in particular the relations of production between the capitalist class and the proletariat, that is now 'each time more threateningly' putting capitalism in jeopardy."

> "Here, crisis acts as the birth pangs of the new society."

**Marx's *Capital* Vol. I, Chapter 32** (Page 370):

**"The Historical Tendency of Capitalist Accumulation"**:

**Process Described**:
1. Capitalism destroyed historical unity: Direct producer + means of production
2. Triggered dynamics of new mode of production
3. Cooperation → technical division of labour → large-scale industry (machinery)
4. → Production transformed into **collective process**
5. Large-scale production: Ever-more efficient, productive, profitable
6. More powerful capitalists destroyed independent direct producers
7. Now: **Capitalists expropriate other capitalists**

**Marx's Analysis** (Pages 370-371):

> "This expropriation is accomplished by the action of the immanent laws of capitalistic production itself, by the centralisation of capital. One capitalist always kills many. Hand in hand with this centralisation, or this expropriation of many capitalists by few, develop, on an ever-extending scale, the cooperative form of the labour-process, the conscious technical application of science, the methodical cultivation of the soil, the transformation of the instruments of labour into instruments of labour only usable in common, the economising of all means of production by their use as the means of production of combined, socialised labour, the entanglement of all peoples in the net of the world-market, and with this, the international character of the capitalist regime..."

> "[B]ut with this too grows the revolt of the working class, a class always increasing in numbers, and disciplined, united, organised by the very mechanism of the process of capitalist production itself. The monopoly of capital becomes a fetter upon the mode of production, which has sprung up and flourished with, and under it. Centralisation of the means of production and socialisation of labour at last reach a point where they become incompatible with their capitalist integument. This integument is burst asunder. **The knell of capitalist private property sounds. The expropriators are expropriated.**" (Marx, 1976, 929)

**Authors' Interpretation** (Page 371):

**How Capitalism Destroys Own Bases**:
- Collective, socialised, large-scale, internationalised production
- Centralises production on own dynamics
- Elimination of smaller capitalists each day
- Socialisation of labour: From isolation in small units → integrated mode
- Worker → "collective worker"
- Production decisions on scale of vast units (corporations)
- → Production planned on extraordinarily large scale, international level

**Marx's Terms**:
- **Centralisation of the means of production**
- **Socialisation of labour**

**Fundamental Contradiction** (Page 371):
- Centralisation/socialisation processes
- Enter contradiction with logic of capital (private appropriation + market)
- **Socialised production requires planning**
- **Capitalist property = barrier to wholesale planning**

**Current Reality** (Page 371):
- Multinational companies DO conduct large-scale planning
- BUT: "Each is an island unto itself in a sea of market relations"
- Planning within corporations + anarchy between capitalists/world economy coexist
