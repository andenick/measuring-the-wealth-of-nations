## Historical Figures Mentioned (with dates)

### Economists and Theorists

1. **Jean-Baptiste Say** (1767–1832)
   - French economist
   - Creator of Say's Law ("law of outlets")

2. **David Ricardo** (1772–1823)
   - Greatest figure in classical political economy (alongside Smith)
   - Adopted Say's Law
   - Theory of land rent

3. **Adam Smith** (1723–1790)
   - Classical political economy
   - Mentioned alongside Ricardo

4. **Jean Charles Léonard de Sismondi** (1773–1842)
   - Social critic in Switzerland
   - Early critic of Say's Law

5. **Thomas Malthus** (1766–1834)
   - Conservative British economist
   - Friend of Ricardo, critic of Say's Law
   - Underconsumptionist

6. **William Stanley Jevons** (1835–1882)
   - Prominent representative of marginalist school
   - Exceptional figure in crisis theory

7. **John Maynard Keynes** (1883–1946)
   - British public intellectual and economist
   - Major representative of "realist school"
   - Author of General Theory (1936)

8. **Karl Marx** (1818–1883)
   - Author of Capital and Communist Manifesto
   - Critic of bourgeois economics
   - Theory of capitalist crisis

9. **Joseph Alois Schumpeter** (1883–1950)
   - Austrian economist
   - Theory of "creative destruction"
   - Author of Business Cycles (1939)

10. **Robert J. Shiller**
    - Nobel laureate in economics
    - Expert on stock markets
    - Warning about 21st century "roaring 20s"

## Concepts and Schools of Thought Referenced

1. **Say's Law** ("law of outlets")
   - Early 19th century
   - Total supply = total demand

2. **Classical School of Political Economy**
   - Adam Smith, David Ricardo

3. **Neoclassical Theory**
   - Orthodoxy in mainstream economics
   - Marginalist economics (1870s)
   - General equilibrium theory (post-WWII)
   - Rational expectations theory (late 20th century)

4. **Efficient Market Hypothesis**
   - Modern version of market clearing argument

5. **Business Cycles Literature**
   - Specialized study of economic fluctuations

6. **The Denialist School**
   - Mainstream economics denying systemic crises

7. **The Realist School**
   - Led by Keynes
   - Acknowledges possibility of crises

8. **Underconsumptionism**
   - Crisis theory based on lack of consumption

9. **Keynesianism**
   - Fine-tuning policies
   - Monetary and fiscal intervention

10. **Schumpeterian Theory**
    - Creative destruction
    - Innovation-driven cycles
