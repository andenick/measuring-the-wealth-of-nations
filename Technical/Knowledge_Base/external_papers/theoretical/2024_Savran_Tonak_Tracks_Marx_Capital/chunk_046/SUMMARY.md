## Key Theoretical Arguments (Chapter 19 Conclusion)

### Central Thesis
Savran concludes his defense against critics by asserting that Sraffa's economic theory contains a fundamental inconsistency between production and distribution, while Marx's theory remains internally consistent.

### Main Arguments

#### 1. **The Rupture in Sraffa's System**
- Sraffa creates a **rupture between production and distribution**
- He suppresses one pole of the dialectical unity of capitalist production
- The surplus magnitude is determined independently of class relationships
- Distribution of surplus is inconsistent with its production
- The wage-annual product inconsistency is a surface manifestation of this deeper problem

#### 2. **Comparison to Ricardo**
Savran quotes Marx's critique of Ricardo (from *Grundrisse*, p. 553):
- Ricardo treats a "definite amount of objective labour time" and asks how it is distributed
- The real question is the specific nature of the capital-labor relation
- Capital appropriates ready-made value rather than creating surplus-value
- Sraffa's procedure mirrors Ricardo's methodological error

#### 3. **Defense Against Neoclassical Accusations**
- Savran explicitly rejects Eatwell's claim that his critique leads toward neoclassical theory
- His criticism of Sraffa is NOT a condemnation of Marx's surplus-value theory
- The argument provides "further evidence that Marx's theory is the only consistent and meaningful theory of the capitalist mode of production"

#### 4. **Walras's Distortion of Ricardo** (Footnote 13)
- Walras claimed Ricardo founded the "English theory" where "the price of productive services determines the price of products"
- Savran calls this "probably the greatest single distortion of a rival theoretical stance in the history of economic ideas"
- Ricardo actually argued value is regulated by "the quantity of labour bestowed on [commodity] production"
- Productive services don't even exist in Ricardian theory
- Critique of Eatwell's characterization of Savran's argument

### 5. **Final Conclusions**

**On Sraffa's Relationship to Neoclassical Economics:**
- The impression that Sraffa is close to neoclassical economics (requiring simultaneous determination) is "somewhat misleading"
- Fixing one distribution variable independently defines Sraffa's specificity
- The issue is not affinity to neoclassical economics, but **theoretical consistency of Sraffa's structure**

**On the Success of Defense:**
- Defenders of Sraffa have been "unsuccessful" in salvaging the theory from inconsistency charges
- Savran's earlier arguments are "logically sound"
- Sraffa's theory is:
  - Not only deficient
  - Not susceptible to rigorous reformulation
- **Radical divergence** exists between Sraffa and Marx
- Marx's theory is "immune to the criticism formulated against Sraffa"
