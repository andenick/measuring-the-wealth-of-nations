# SUMMARY - Chunk 045 (Pages 441-450)

- **Chapter**: 19 - "Confusions Concerning Sraffa (and Marx): Reply…"
- **Author**: S. Savran

## Overview

This chunk contains S. Savran's reply to critics (primarily Steedman, Eatwell, and McLachlan et al.) regarding his earlier critique of Sraffa's economic theory. The text presents a detailed defense of Savran's position that Sraffa's theory is internally inconsistent, while arguing that Marx's theory of surplus-value is immune to similar criticisms.

## Main Sections

### 1. The Standard Commodity (Pages 424-425)
**Key Argument**: The standard commodity is indispensable for Sraffa's theory, particularly when dealing with joint production, fixed capital, and rent.

**Main Points**:
- Without the standard commodity as the measure of prices, the same real wage rate may correspond to different rates of profit
- Sraffa's chronological development (standard commodity worked out in 1930s-40s) does not contradict its logical necessity
- The "particular aspects" (standard commodity, joint products, fixed capital) are necessary for completion of the argument

### 2. The Wage as a Price (Pages 425-426)
**Key Argument**: Steedman's defense of fixing wages as a price fails on multiple grounds.

**Main Points**:
- Steedman's first case (necessary as basic in another capacity) is irrelevant to Savran's criticism
- Steedman's second case simply reiterates Sraffa without addressing Savran's logical argument
- A minimum wage rate cannot be determined in the abstract before prices are known
- In joint product systems, the price of a commodity may fall faster than the wage, making it impossible to specify a "minimum viable wage rate"

### 3. The Rate of Profit (Pages 426-427)
**Key Argument**: The method of fixing the rate of profit independently is inadmissible.

**Main Points**:
- No definable minimum wage means no corresponding maximum rate of profit
- No conceivable mechanism could set a ceiling on the rate of interest
- Nothing guarantees consistency of an independently determined rate of profit with the system of production, "even if the wage were reduced to zero"

### 4. The So-Called "Fourth Alternative" (Pages 427-429)
**Key Argument**: Separating necessary and surplus wage components does not resolve Sraffa's inconsistencies.

**Main Points**:
- In Sraffa's presentation, this is merely a variant of the first method (wage as aggregate of commodities)
- If surplus commodities are non-basics, they influence profit rate (contradicting Sraffa)
- If surplus commodities are basics, the input structure changes with variable surplus component
- Changing input structure makes it impossible to construct a standard commodity
- Alternative versions (Roncaglia's, or with independently given profit rate) also fail

### 5. The Question of Reformulation (Pages 429-430)
**Key Argument**: Sraffa's theory cannot be reformulated to achieve internal consistency.

**Main Points**:
- McLachlan et al. (MOS) avoid some of Steedman's pitfalls
- MOS agree that Sraffa's theory has shortcomings but claim it's merely "underspecified"
- However, MOS's own reformulation fails because it assumes an abstract price can buy subsistence commodities
- Sraffa's theory is both inconsistent in original formulation and not susceptible to rigorous reformulation

### 6. Marx in Defence of Sraffa (Pages 430-433)
**Key Argument**: Eatwell incorrectly claims that Savran's critique of Sraffa applies equally to Marx's theory.

**Main Points**:

**Three Propositions**:
1. **Sraffa's inconsistency**: Results from mutually independent determination of wage and annual social product, which are actually interdependent
2. **Marx's immunity**: Marx's theory is premised upon the relationship between these magnitudes, not their independence
3. **Methodological difference**: The divergence derives from fundamental differences in scientific method

**Marx vs. Sraffa on Independent Determination**:

**Sraffa**:
- Starts with given technological matrix
- Annual social product magnitude independent of wage size
- Wage determined independently of annual social product
- Mutual independence creates logical problem: no a priori reason for one to be greater than the other

**Marx**:
- Value of labour-power is given (for a given country/period)
- BUT: Annual product (newly created value) is NOT independently determined
- Capitalist can force worker to perform surplus labour beyond reproducing value of labour-power
- Process of valorization guarantees newly created total value is NECESSARILY greater than value of labour-power
- These two magnitudes are NOT mutually independent

**Methodological Difference**:
- **Marx**: Investigates relations of production prior to relations of distribution; analyzes how surplus-value is produced; grasps dialectical unity of labour process and valorization process
- **Sraffa**: Takes social forms as granted; cannot grasp specificity of capitalist production; treats it as simple labour process without social/historical determination

## Key Theoretical Contributions

1. **Internal Consistency Argument**: Detailed demonstration that Sraffa's theory is internally inconsistent across all proposed methods of fixing independent variables

2. **Standard Commodity Necessity**: Shows that the standard commodity is logically (not just chronologically) necessary for Sraffa's analysis of joint production and fixed capital

3. **Marx-Sraffa Distinction**: Clarifies fundamental difference between Marx's theory of surplus-value and Sraffa's approach to distribution

4. **Methodological Critique**: Identifies the root of Sraffa's problems in his scientific method (starting from technological matrix rather than social relations of production)

## Critical Debates Engaged

1. **With Steedman**: On viability of wage-as-price and rate-of-profit methods
2. **With McLachlan et al. (MOS)**: On possibility of reformulating Sraffa's theory
3. **With Eatwell**: On relationship between Savran's critique and Marx's theory

## Terminology Clarifications

- **Annual social product**: What Eatwell (following Sraffa) calls "net social product"
- **Value-product**: Marx's term for newly created value
- **Distributional parameters**: Eatwell's term (Savran notes this is not correct for Marx)
- **Technologically basic**: Roncaglia's term for basic products in capacity as means of production

## Theoretical Stakes

This chunk addresses fundamental questions about:
- The logical coherence of Sraffian price theory
- The relationship between wage determination and surplus distribution
- The distinction between Marx's value theory and neo-Ricardian approaches
- The role of social relations of production in economic theory
- The limits of reformulating theories with methodological problems

## Conclusion (Implicit)

Savran establishes that:
1. Sraffa's theory cannot be made internally consistent through any of the proposed methods
2. The inconsistency is not merely technical but methodological
3. Marx's theory is fundamentally different and immune to these critiques
4. The difference lies in Marx's focus on production relations rather than distribution relations
