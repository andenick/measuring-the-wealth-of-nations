## Content Summary

### Chapter/Section Context
This chunk continues **Chapter 5: "Productive and Unproductive Labour: An Attempt..."** covering pages 95-104. The content is still in the theoretical/conceptual portion of the work, establishing the Marxist framework for distinguishing productive from unproductive labour (PUPL).

### Main Topics Covered

1. **Outsourcing and Labour Forms** (p. 95)
   - Putting-out systems and home-based production (not productive)
   - Subcontracting (productive or unproductive depending on activity)

2. **Exchange Against Capital vs. Exchange Against Revenue** (pp. 95-97)
   - Circuit of capital: M - C ... P ... C' - M'
   - Labour-power exchanged against capital produces surplus-value
   - Labour-power exchanged against revenue (domestic servants) is unproductive
   - Historical context: Adam Smith, Ricardo, and classical political economy
   - Domestic servants in early capitalism vs. modern contexts

3. **Production vs. Circulation Spheres** (pp. 97-102)
   - Metamorphoses of capital: money capital → productive capital → commodity capital
   - Only productive capital phase creates surplus-value
   - Circulation activities are unproductive by definition
   - Specialization of capital functions:
     - Interest-bearing capital (banks, insurance, etc.)
     - Commercial capital (wholesale, retail, etc.)
   - Workers in circulation sphere are unproductive
   - Critique of "necessity" argument for productivity
   - Circulation as faux frais (false costs) of production

4. **Transportation and Storage** (pp. 100-101)
   - Transportation as part of production process (productive)
   - Storage as final stage of production (productive)
   - Exception: circulation-motivated transport/storage (unproductive)
   - Just-in-time techniques and productivity

5. **Summary Framework** (p. 102)
   - Figure 2: Classification diagram of labour under capitalism
   - Four key distinctions:
     1. Use-value production vs. commodity production
     2. Exchange of commodities vs. exchange of labour-power
     3. Exchange against revenue vs. exchange against capital
     4. Exchange against circulation capital vs. production capital

6. **The Problem of Services** (pp. 102-104)
   - Introduction to service sector ambiguity
   - Adam Smith's two definitions of productive labour:
     - First (correct): based on capitalist social relations
     - Second (erroneous): based on material fixation of labour
   - Soviet national accounts treatment of services (footnote 21)

- **Location**: Page 95 (book page)
- **LaTeX**: `M - C \ldots P \ldots C' - M'`
- **Description**: General formula for the circuit of capital representing the process of self-expansion (valorization) of capital
- **Components**:

  - C = Commodities (labour-power and means of production)
  - P = Production process
  - C' = Commodities of higher value
  - M' = Expanded money capital (M + surplus-value)

## Key Findings/Arguments

1. **Necessary Conditions for Productive Labour** (Sequential/Hierarchical):
   - Commodity production (not use-value production)
   - Sale of labour-power (not independent production)
   - Exchange against capital (not against revenue)
   - Employment in production sphere (not circulation)

2. **Circulation Activities Are Unproductive**:
   - Despite being "necessary" for capitalism
   - Do not create value or surplus-value
   - Only change forms (commodities ↔ money)
   - Constitute additional social costs (faux frais)
   - Workers in banks, retail, wholesale = unproductive

3. **Transportation/Storage Exception**:
   - Part of production process (productive)
   - Necessary final stage under any social system
   - Exception: circulation-motivated activities (speculation, etc.)

4. **Historical Context**:
   - Classical political economy used PUPL distinction politically
   - Attack on landed interests (unproductive domestic servants)
   - Modern diminution of domestic service in developed countries
   - Recent resurgence with inequality

5. **Methodological Clarity**:
   - Avoid conflating criteria of different orders
   - Surplus-value production = absolute guiding principle
   - Other criteria (revenue/capital, circulation/production) = partial conditions
   - Figure 2 provides comprehensive classification framework
