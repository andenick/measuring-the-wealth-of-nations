# Figure 2: Classification of Labour Under Capitalism

**Location**: Page 103 (book page), page 9 (PDF page)

**Type**: Hierarchical flowchart/decision tree diagram

**Purpose**: Visual classification of different forms of labour under capitalism to distinguish productive from unproductive labour

## Complete Figure Structure

### Level 0 (Root)
**Labour** (top-level category)

### Level 1: First Bifurcation
Labour splits into two main branches:

#### Branch 1.1: Production of Use-Values
- **Examples**: self-subsistence farming, housework, etc.
- **Status**: UNPRODUCTIVE (terminal branch)
- **Reason**: Not commodity production

#### Branch 1.2: Production of Commodities
- **Continues to**: Level 2 (further subdivision)

### Level 2: Second Bifurcation
Production of Commodities splits into:

#### Branch 2.1: Petty Commodity Production
- **Examples**: artisans, smallholding peasants, etc.
- **Status**: UNPRODUCTIVE (terminal branch)
- **Reason**: Not based on wage-labour/sale of labour-power

#### Branch 2.2: Production based on Wage-Labour
- **Continues to**: Level 3 (further subdivision)

### Level 3: Third Bifurcation
Production based on Wage-Labour splits into:

#### Branch 3.1: Wage-Labour paid out of revenue
- **Examples**: domestic servants, cooks, gardeners, chauffeurs, body-guards, etc.
- **Status**: UNPRODUCTIVE (terminal branch)
- **Reason**: Exchanged against revenue, not capital; does not produce surplus-value

#### Branch 3.2: Wage-Labour employed by capital
- **Continues to**: Level 4 (further subdivision)

### Level 4: Fourth Bifurcation (Final)
Wage-Labour employed by capital splits into:

#### Branch 4.1: Workers in Circulation
- **Examples**: employees of banks, insurance and mortgage companies, commercial workers
- **Status**: UNPRODUCTIVE (terminal branch)
- **Reason**: Circulation activities do not create surplus-value

#### Branch 4.2: Workers in production and transportation
- **Status**: **PRODUCTIVE LABOUR FOR CAPITAL** (terminal branch)
- **Reason**: Only labour that produces surplus-value for capital

## Visual Characteristics

### Diagram Elements
1. **Boxes**: Each category appears in a rectangular box
2. **Arrows**: Downward arrows showing flow from general to specific
3. **Branching**: Each level shows binary or multiple splits
4. **Examples**: Italicized text in parentheses providing concrete examples
5. **Emphasis**: Final productive category appears in bold or emphasized text

### Flow Direction
- **Top to bottom**: From most general (Labour) to most specific classifications
- **Left to right**: Generally, unproductive categories appear on left branches, with the productive category as the final right-most terminus

## Analytical Framework Encoded in Figure

### Four Key Distinctions (Sequential Tests)
The figure visually represents four sequential criteria that must ALL be met for labour to be productive:

1. **Commodity Production Test** (Level 1)
   - Question: Is this production of use-values or commodities?
   - Must be: Commodity production
   - Eliminates: Self-subsistence farming, housework

2. **Wage-Labour Test** (Level 2)
   - Question: Is this petty commodity production or wage-labour based?
   - Must be: Based on wage-labour (sale of labour-power)
   - Eliminates: Artisans, smallholding peasants

3. **Capital vs. Revenue Test** (Level 3)
   - Question: Is labour-power exchanged against revenue or capital?
   - Must be: Exchanged against capital
   - Eliminates: Domestic servants, personal service workers

4. **Production vs. Circulation Test** (Level 4)
   - Question: Is capital employed in circulation or production?
   - Must be: Production (including transportation as final production stage)
   - Eliminates: Bank employees, retail workers, insurance workers

### Logical Structure
```
PRODUCTIVE LABOUR =
  Commodity Production
  AND Wage-Labour
  AND Exchanged Against Capital
  AND Employed in Production Sphere
```

## Theoretical Significance

This figure synthesizes the entire argument of Chapter 5 up to page 102. It shows:

1. **Hierarchy of Conditions**: Each level adds a necessary condition for productive labour
2. **Elimination Process**: Most labour categories are unproductive; only one specific category is productive
3. **Narrow Definition**: Productive labour is a small subset of total social labour
4. **Conceptual Clarity**: Visual representation prevents conflation of different criteria

## Connection to Empirical Analysis

This classification framework will be essential when the book transitions to empirical analysis (anticipated in Part II):
- National income accounts must distinguish these categories
- GDP calculations affected by PUPL distinction
- Surplus-value estimation requires identifying productive labour
- Class analysis depends on this classification

The figure provides the conceptual map for coding actual economic activities in empirical data.
