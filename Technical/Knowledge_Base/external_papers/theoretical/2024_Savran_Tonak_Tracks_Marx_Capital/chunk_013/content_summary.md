# Content Summary - Chunk 013

## Overview
**Chunk 013** (book pages 95-104, PDF pages 121-130) continues the theoretical development of the productive/unproductive labour (PUPL) distinction in Chapter 5. This chunk systematically builds the complete framework for classifying labour under capitalism.

## Main Sections

### 1. Outsourcing and Forms of Labour Organization (p. 95)
The chapter begins by addressing modern forms of labour organization:
- **Putting-out systems** (home-based production): NOT productive even when capitalist provides tools/materials - not subsumed under capitalist form
- **Subcontracting**: Can be productive or unproductive depending on specific activity (usually involves capitalist firms)

### 2. Exchange Against Capital vs. Exchange Against Revenue (pp. 95-97)

#### The Circuit of Capital
Introduces the fundamental formula: **M - C ... P ... C' - M'**
- M = Money capital
- C = Commodities (labour-power + means of production)
- P = Production process
- C' = Commodities with higher value
- M' = Expanded money capital

#### Two Types of Labour-Power Exchange
1. **Against Capital**: Labour-power purchased as part of M (money functioning as capital)
   - Produces equivalent of its value PLUS surplus-value
   - This is PRODUCTIVE labour

2. **Against Revenue**: Labour-power purchased from capitalist's consumption fund
   - Used for personal service (domestic servants, cooks, drivers, gardeners)
   - Product consumed as use-value, not sold as commodity
   - Does NOT produce surplus-value
   - This is UNPRODUCTIVE labour

#### Historical Context
- Central debate in classical political economy (18th-19th centuries)
- Adam Smith and Ricardo used this distinction
- Particularly relevant when domestic servants were common
- Before WWI: 1/6 of Britain's workforce were domestic servants
- Declined in developed countries, now resurging with inequality
- Still structural feature in underdeveloped countries

### 3. Production vs. Circulation: The Core Distinction (pp. 97-102)

#### Capital's Metamorphoses
Capital must go through different functional forms:
1. **Money Capital** (M): Ready to purchase inputs
2. **Productive Capital** (P): In production process - **ONLY HERE IS SURPLUS-VALUE CREATED**
3. **Commodity Capital** (C'): Products ready for sale
4. Back to **Money Capital** (M'): Capital expanded by surplus-value

#### Key Insight
**Circulation activities change forms but create no value**
- M → C: Buying (exchange of money for commodities)
- C' → M': Selling (exchange of commodities for money)
- No new value or surplus-value produced in these phases
- These are "unproductive circulation tasks by definition"

#### Specialization of Capital Functions
With capitalist development, different capitals specialize:

**Interest-Bearing Capital** (money capital functions):
- Commercial banks
- Investment banks
- Brokerage firms
- Mortgage companies
- Insurance and reinsurance companies

**Commercial Capital** (commodity capital functions):
- Wholesale merchants
- Department stores
- Large retail outlets
- Other retailers

#### Workers in Circulation Sphere = Unproductive
Even though:
- They sell their labour-power
- They're employed by capital
- They're necessary for reproduction of total capital
- They enable their employers to earn profits

**They are unproductive because:**
- Engaged in circulation tasks, not production
- Do not produce surplus-value
- Their labour is faux frais (false/unproductive costs) of production
- Their wages are paid from surplus-value produced elsewhere
- Profits of circulation capital come from redistribution of surplus-value produced in production sphere

#### The "Necessity" Argument Refuted
Text argues against claiming circulation workers are productive because they're "necessary":
- Policemen are "necessary" for capitalist state but not productive
- Circulation is only necessary under capitalism, not for production in general
- Generalized commodity production is historically specific to capitalism
- Circulation is wasteful from standpoint of social production
- It's an additional social cost, a drain on social surplus

#### Marx Quote on Circulation Workers
"(A buying and selling) agent expends his labour-power and labour-time in the operations of C – M and M – C. And he makes his living in that way, just as another does by spinning or by making pills. He performs a necessary function, because the process of reproduction itself includes unproductive functions. He works as well as the next man but intrinsically his labour creates neither value nor product. He belongs himself to the faux frais of production."

#### Rapid Turnover and Double Counting
Text addresses argument that circulation speeds up capital turnover, increasing production:
- Yes, circulation can enable more rapid turnover
- This allows MORE capital to be available for production
- Additional surplus-value created is produced by PRODUCTION workers
- Attributing this to circulation workers would be double counting
- Circulation workers' contribution: releasing capital from unproductive tasks
- This releases money to function as capital, but doesn't directly create surplus-value
- Would be "attributing magical power of productivity to objectified labour"

### 4. Transportation and Storage: Special Cases (pp. 100-101)

#### NOT Circulation Activities
Despite involving movement/holding of commodities:

**Transportation**:
- Necessary final stage of production under any social system
- Use-value only realized when commodity reaches destination
- Can't consume a commodity produced elsewhere unless transported
- Marx: "use-value of things is materialized only in their consumption and may necessitate a change of location"
- Transportation adds value (transfers value from means of transport + adds value through labour)
- Therefore: **PRODUCTIVE** (when employed by capital)

**Storage**:
- Necessary part of production process
- Indispensable element of any advanced division of labour
- Just-in-time techniques that reduce storage simply raise labour productivity
- Therefore: **PRODUCTIVE** (when employed by capital)

**EXCEPTION**: Transportation/storage for purely circulation motives:
- Speculation
- Re-exportation due to government regulation
- Other circulation-specific reasons
- These activities are UNPRODUCTIVE

### 5. Complete Definition of Productive Labour (pp. 101-102)

#### Sufficient Conditions Achieved
"All labour that is exchanged against capital employed in the sphere of production is productive for capital."

#### The Four Sequential Criteria
Through the chapter's analysis, we've established:

1. **Commodity production** (vs. use-value production)
   - Eliminates: self-subsistence farming, housework

2. **Sale of labour-power** (vs. independent production)
   - Eliminates: artisans, smallholding peasants

3. **Exchange against capital** (vs. exchange against revenue)
   - Eliminates: domestic servants, personal service workers

4. **Production sphere** (vs. circulation sphere)
   - Eliminates: bank workers, retail workers, commercial workers

#### Only the Final Category is Productive
Workers in production and transportation employed by capital

### 7. Introduction to Services Problem (pp. 102-104)

#### Two Major Categories Still to Discuss
1. Workers in service sector
2. Wage labourers employed by state

#### Services: A Contentious Area
- "Most contentious area" in PUPL discussion
- Even authors agreeing on other aspects disagree here
- Confusion due to ambiguity of concept "service" itself
- Term designates "two very different types of things"

#### Adam Smith's Two Definitions of Productive Labour

**First Definition** (correct - based on social relations):
- Manufacturer adds value of maintenance + master's profit
- Menial servant "adds to the value of nothing"
- Manufacturer's wages restored with profit
- Servant's maintenance "never restored"
- "A man grows rich by employing a multitude of manufacturers: he grows poor, by maintaining a multitude of menial servants"
- Marx: "Adam Smith was essentially correct with his productive and unproductive labour, correct from the standpoint of bourgeois economy"

**Second Definition** (erroneous - based on material fixation):
- Manufacturer's labour "fixes or realizes itself in some particular subject or vendible commodity, which lasts for some time"
- Labour "stocked and stored up to be employed, if necessary, upon some other occasion"
- Menial servant's services "generally perish in the very instance of their performance"
- "seldom leave any trace or value behind them"

#### Marx's Critique (Anticipated)
"The material aspect of the product of labour or the concrete content of the labour itself has nothing to do with the definition of productive..."
