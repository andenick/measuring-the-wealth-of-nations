### Chapter 3 Conclusion (Pages 53-55)

**Section 7: The Laws of Motion of Capital**

The chapter concludes with a discussion of Marx's statement about "the economic law of motion of modern society." Key points:

- Marx identifies multiple laws of motion in Capital (tendency to commoditize all products, proletarianization, concentration and centralization of capital, reserve army of labor, tendency of rate of profit to fall, periodical crises)
- The fundamental "law of motion" is capitalism's tendency to create contradictions that make it superfluous and create the basis for a new society
- Quotes Marx's famous passage from Chapter 32 of Volume I on "Historical Tendency of Capitalist Accumulation"
- Concludes that Capital is "as much a book about communism as it is about capitalism"

### Chapter 4: Critique of Political Economy (Pages 57-63)

**Author**: Sungur Savran

**Introduction**: Examines why Marx titled his major works as "critique of political economy" rather than simply political economy.

**Section 1: Classical Political Economy (Pages 59-62)**

Detailed analysis of:
- Adam Smith's theoretical framework
  - Division of labor and exchange as human nature
  - Value theory (labor embodied vs. labor commanded, ultimately natural price theory)
  - Wealth of nations and capital accumulation
  - Relationship between distribution and growth

- David Ricardo's contributions
  - Takes Smith's framework as starting point
  - Central problematic: how accumulation affects distribution
  - Famous quote on distribution as "the principal problem in Political Economy"
  - Labor theory of value based on labor time necessary for production
  - Superiority over vulgar economics but with a critical flaw: relationship between labor and value is presupposed, not investigated

**Section 2: Introducing Marx's Critique (Pages 63)**

Begins examining:
- Continuity between classical political economy and Marx
- Common misunderstanding that Marx merely "corrected" classical economics
- Marx's specific criticisms (money, rent, labor vs. labor power, value vs. price of production, surplus-value vs. profit)
