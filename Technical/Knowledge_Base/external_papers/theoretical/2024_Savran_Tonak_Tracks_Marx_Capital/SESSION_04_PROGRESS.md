## CHUNK 031 (Pages 301-310 / Book pages 276-285)

### Chapter 13 Section 2: Fordism = Taylorism + Moving Assembly Line (Conclusion)

**Core Argument**: "Fordism" is a vacuous concept. What Ford introduced was simply **Taylorism reinforced by the moving assembly line**.

**Moving Assembly Line Characteristics**:
- NOT invented by Ford; first used in slaughterhouses ("disassembly line")
- Spread via car industry application
- **Key advantage for capital**: Management controls pace of entire factory (not just individual machines)
- **Prerequisite**: Capital must already possess production knowledge (Taylorism prerequisite)

**"Fordism" Critique**:
- Lacks sufficiently specified characteristics as distinct labor process
- Other elements (intensive accumulation regime, mass consumption thesis) unconvincing
- Should be "buried" as vacuous concept
- **Taylorism** should reclaim proper place as management style/labor control method

---

### Chapter 13 Section 3: Lean Production - Capital Fighting "Idle Time"

**Definition**: "Lean production" = most accurate term (vs. post-Fordism, flexible production, Japanese system, Toyotism)
- **Lean** = Production cleansed of all resource use beyond strictly necessary
- Like "lean meat" - robust, dynamic, energetic

**Two Dimensions**:
1. **Labour Process Management** (relations between capital and labor inside enterprise)
2. **Workplace Fragmentation** (constellation of supplier firms around main corporation)

**Aim**: NOT to "humanize" production, but to:
- Consolidate capital's control over labor
- Increase labor productivity and intensity
- Save on constant capital (reduce costs)

---

#### The Contradictions of the Assembly Line

Lean production = search for solutions to contradictions of moving assembly line system.

**Problem 1: IDLE TIME**

**Definition**: Sum of moments when worker not performing useful act (from capital's viewpoint)

**NOT from worker "laziness"**, but from **"balancing of the line"** problem:
- Incompatibilities/disharmony between work stations
- Workers downstream depend on upstream pace
- Imbalances create unused labor surplus at some stations

**Lean Production Solution**: Visualisation system (andon) + JIT elimination of inventory
- **US Transportation Dept report goal**: "Making each worker work 60 minutes every hour"
- **Taiichi Ohno** (inventor of lean production): "Present capacity = work + waste. True efficiency = bring percentage of work to 100%."

**Most Important Aspect**: Systematic elimination of idle time in favor of capital.

---

**Problem 2: SLOWING DOWN THE LINE**

When demand falls → line must slow → idle time arises at every station

**Classical System Problem**: Each worker specialized on narrow tasks → hard to substitute/redistribute

**Lean Production Solution**: Worker replaceability through task mobility

---

**Problem 3: IMPOSSIBILITY OF USING WORKER EXPERIENCE/KNOWLEDGE**

**Taylorism Achievement**: Expropriated worker production knowledge → all design/knowledge to management

**Remaining Problem**: Worker's experience-based knowledge still exists → potential for more rational organization squandered if management remains alien to it

**Lean Production Solution**: Quality circles and suggestion schemes extract this knowledge

---

**Problem 4: WASTE**

**Classical System (JIC - Just-In-Case)**:
- Work stations maintain reserve stock of pieces/inputs
- Guards against upstream glitches stopping downstream work
- **Cost**: Capital lies idle most of time
- **Risk**: Quality control done AFTER production → defective production may continue unnoticed

**Lean Production Solution**: JIT + continuous quality control eliminates both wastes

---

#### JIT (Just-In-Time) System

**Common (Limited) Definition**: Production without inventory → saves inventory costs

**Three Cost Savings** (acknowledged but SECONDARY):
1. Eliminates warehouse storage costs
2. Avoids idle stocks of capital within factory
3. Workers no longer waste time looking for storage/hunting for parts (Ohno quote)

**REAL FUNCTION**: **INTENSIFICATION OF LABOUR** through identification and elimination of idle time

**How JIT Identifies Idle Time**:
- Without inventory, fast-running line forces hard-pressed workers/stations to reveal capacity limits
- **Visualisation System (Andon)**: Green/amber/red lights at each station
  - Green = normal operation
  - Amber = partial problem
  - Red = cannot cope → line stops

**Paradox**: "Green is no good!"
- Constant green → management deduces **superfluous labor surplus** exists
- More work heaped on worker OR worker drawn away to another station
- Continues until worker/station reaches limit (pushes yellow button)
- **Result**: Line balanced with respect to tempo/pace

**Essence**: **"Management by stress or pressure"** (Parker & Slaughter, 1988)

**Ohno's Boat Analogy** (p. 289):
> "8 rowers, 4 left + 4 right. If one rows twice as hard, boat zigzags. Best way = everyone distribute force equally, rowing evenly at same depth."

**Real objective**: "Balancing of the line"

**Kaizen (Continuous Improvement)**:
- Pressure forever increased to increase work intensity
- NO endpoint; always "superfluous surplus" to eliminate
- In Japan: Workers come early, use coffee breaks/rest time to prevent future failures
- **Result**: Lean production creates NEW "work time" beyond stipulated contract ("elasticity of workday")

**Summary Quote (p. 289)**:
> "JIT is NOT a technical matter, but a method directly related to the management of labour in the name of the capitalist with a view to extracting ever more labour."

## CHUNK 032 (Pages 311-320 / Book pages 286-295)

### Chapter 13 Section 3 (Continued): Task Mobility and Interchangeability

**Purpose**: Enable line balancing + layoffs when line slows down

**How Achieved**:
- Workers must be able to perform MORE than single task
- Interchangeability between workers essential

**RADICAL CHANGE: Modular System**

**Classical System**:
- Work station = group of workers + same machine + same task

**Lean Production System**:
- **U-form work station** with several different machines
- Individual worker works at different machines
- Worker performs sub-assembly chores in "idle time" from central task
- Performs SEVERAL motions... endlessly (vs. one motion endlessly)
- **Additional Functions Transferred to Production Worker**:
  - Maintenance (previously separate workers)
  - Quality control (previously separate function)

**Result**: Further increase in labor intensity through idle time reduction

**Researcher Comment** (Windolf, 1984):
> "Joining together previously separated individual areas of responsibility in team opens possibilities for reducing sum of time lost. Or: achieve highest possible ratio of time worked within working time of individual employee."

---

### Team Work and Quality Circles

**Similarity: Ohno ↔ Taylor**
- Both paid attention to shop floor knowledge
- Ohno (like Taylor): Rose from production floor, remained connected to "plant-first principle"
- Ohno quote (p. 291): "Production plant = manufacturing's major source of information... most vital information about management = time spent in plant, not vice president's office."

**Four Functions for Capital**:

**1. EXTRACTION OF WORKER KNOWLEDGE**

- Suggestion schemes extract experience-based knowledge from ordinary workers
- Team leaders/lower-level managers = go-betweens transmitting knowledge to top management
- **Once suggestion made**: Knowledge no longer belongs solely to worker → becomes management knowledge
- Extends to **time and motion studies** suggestions
- **Bitter Irony**: Workers suggest work pace increases (historically their fundamental struggle AGAINST this!)

**Savings on Variable Capital**:
- Quality supervision previously by special operatives → now by production workers
- Combined with JIT → **"zero defect" system**
- Downstream workers detect defective pieces from upstream (no inventory buffer)
- Every worker conducts quality control with great attention

---

**2. TQM (Total Quality Management) IDEOLOGY**

**"Quality" Penetrated Popular Culture** (late 20th century):
- Consumer rights aspect (admitted as rightful)
- BUT capital shaped "consumer satisfaction" mentality through TQM training/propaganda

**Key Clarification**: "Quality" in TQM ≠ only finished products
- **Also**: Industrial inputs at all production stages
- **"Customer"** = not only final buyer, but **"internal customer"** (downstream department/work station)

**"Customer Satisfaction" Reinterpreted**:
- = Department/work station satisfaction at subsequent level
- Social significance TOTALLY DIFFERENT
- **Example**: If eliminating defects brings extreme burden to worker → quality realized AT EXPENSE of worker

**Working Class Response**: Attitude/struggle should ensure cost of quality SHOULDERED BY CAPITAL, not worker

---

**3. CORPORATE ALLEGIANCE**

- Suggestion system → illusion worker's ideas esteemed → worker has power to impact production
- "Quality" hype, awards, glitter at meetings, ISO documents → pride in corporation
- **Result**: Perception of capitalist enterprise + workers = **"family"**
- Competition with OTHER company workers sharpens
- Contradictions within OWN company between classes recede to background
- Reinforced by: "Democratic" atmosphere, "company anthem," ideological instruments

---

**4. PEER PRESSURE ON PRODUCTIVITY/DISCIPLINE**

**Absenteeism Example** (most striking):
- Absent worker NOT substituted (lean production = production without substitution/buffers)
- No reduction in production quota despite team reduction
- **Entire burden** of absent worker falls on teammates
- **Result**: Pressure on every worker to reduce absenteeism to avoid coworker ire
- Japan: Absenteeism much less common than Western countries

**General Peer Pressure Reversal**:
- **Past** (pre-Taylorist + Taylorist periods): Workers collectively pressured colleagues to KEEP WORK PACE LOW
- Those outside this sentiment = "darling of boss" or asocial personality
- **Lean Production**: Peer pressure works in OPPOSITE direction (towards raising labor intensity)

---

**5. MOST INSIDIOUS ASPECT: CONQUEST OF WORKER'S MIND**

**Class Difference: Worker vs. Petty-Bourgeois**

**Worker** (under normal conditions):
- Working time clearly defined (result of class struggle gains)
- After work: Bears marks of workplace, may share anger about overseer at pub
- Does NOT reflect during leisure on: How work could be done better, machines kept cleaner, company saved
- Perhaps OPPOSITE: Reflects on how to beat back boss/management on contentious issues

**Petty-Bourgeois**:
- Extends working hours extremely long (exploits own labor per Marx)
- Mind constantly drifts to work, especially if not going smoothly
- Examples: Convenience store owner (7am-late night), artisanal carpenter (weekend work for deadline)

**Fundamental Reason for Worker Difference**:
- Labor power = commodity sold for TEMPORARY time span
- Time span limited by work contract + law
- Capitalist can use worker's bodily/mental capacity ONLY within official workday

**Lean Production Change via Quality Circles**:
- Suggestion schemes → worker reflects during LEISURE on how to perform production better
- **Result**: Working hours become **theoretically unlimited** (except sleeping)
- **Transforms leisure time into time spent for capitalist**
- Worker identifies interests with capitalist
- Devotes **in voluntary manner** leisure time to increasing labor productivity/intensity

**Conclusion** (p. 295):
> "This is the most dangerous aspect of TQM: it becomes **an instrument for the conquest of the mind of the worker**."

### Chapter 13 Section 4: The Fragmentation of the Work Force

**Core + Periphery Division**:
- **Core**: Relatively higher job security, better economic conditions
- **Periphery**: No job security, term contracts, part-time, temps, subcontractor-employed, much worse economic conditions

**General Principle**: Every division within working class harms ENTIRE class (including those temporarily at top)

---

#### Economic Basis of Core Layer Allegiance

**Beyond** ideological/psychological/cultural factors ("enterprise culture"):

**1. JOB SECURITY (Ironic "Pride of Place")**

**Japanese Nengko System** (lifelong employment):
- **Starts from first rung** of career hierarchy (hiring young workers)
- If worker resigns to look elsewhere → risk of NOT finding job OR falling from core to periphery
- **Result**: System **PUSHES worker** to embrace enterprise completely

**2. DEPENDENCE ON ENTERPRISE (Social Services)**

**Japan + United States** (less so continental Europe):
- Social security (health care, pensions) provided BY ENTERPRISES (not state)
- When laid off/quit → NO guarantee of healthcare/retirement contributions
- Some companies: Provide housing, educational facilities for workers' children

**3. INDIVIDUALIZED WAGE SYSTEM**

- Part of wage dependent on superior's assessment of worker performance
- Assessment based on **SUBJECTIVE factors** (e.g., "positive attitude")
- Worker forced to "declare allegiance out loud" to receive decent wage
- "Negative attitude" → deemed fit for lower wage
- **Economic coercion** into allegiance

**4. JAPANESE UNION SYSTEM**

- Unions organized at WORKPLACE level (not industry-wide)
- Union future + bureaucracy tied to enterprise attitude/health

### Chapter 13 Section 5: The Fragmentation of the Workplace

**Objective**: Prevent confrontation between great masses of workers + single boss → break organizing power of large workers' collectives

**Two Forms**:
1. **Within Same Spatial Location**: Core/periphery division (different bosses for worker subsets)
2. **Division of Physical Production Space**: Inter-capitalist relationship (BUT intimately tied to class relations)

---

#### Company Strategy Change During Crisis

**Pre-1970s Crisis** (long post-war boom):
- **Vertical Integration**: Monopoly capital brought major input production under own control
- Part of "finance capital" formation (amalgam across productive + unproductive activities)

**Post-1970s Crisis - Two Contradictory Tendencies**:

**Tendency 1**: Mergers & acquisitions spree (1980s on)
- Sometimes: Carries vertical integration to new heights

**Tendency 2**: Focus on "Basic Competencies"
- Specialize + outsource many previously internal functions
- **Supply Chain Model**: Concatenation between capitalist producers, then internationalized
- Previously peculiar to automotive industry (major brands + components industry)
- Now: Many industries adopted globally
- First-rank suppliers → relations with other less powerful companies worldwide

**Franchising System** (different):
- Services, trade, media, tourism (McDonald's, Benetton, Starbucks, CNN)
- International chains DOWNSTREAM (merchandising, marketing, catering) not upstream (inputs)

---

#### Consequences for Capital-Labor Relations

**Labour Cost Reduction**:
- Suppliers/franchisees = small/medium-scale operations
- Environments where capital more successful preventing unionization
- Wages + miscellaneous labor costs LOWER

**Japan Data**:
- Wage level difference: Main companies vs. suppliers = **33-44%** (AGAINST supplier workers)
- **Implication**: If inputs produced within main company → total wage bill higher, surplus-value extracted smaller
- **This structure** = great advantage for capital (higher surplus-value appropriation)
- Additional cost from loss of economies of scale DWARFED by wage structure advantages

**Extreme Case: Putting-Out / Cottage Industry / Sweatshops**:
- Industries: Textiles, ready-made garments, footwear, leather products
- Return to pre-industrial capitalism practices
- Same principle as components industry relationship BUT MORE LUCRATIVE:
  - Workers NOT unionized
  - Usually UNREGISTERED (no social security)
  - Long hours, starvation wages
  - Many = irregular migrant workers (most precarious working class section, 21st century)

### Chapter 13 Section 6: "Lean and Mean" - Class Struggle Under Lean Production

**Standard "Post-Fordism" Explanation**: Rise of international competition (1970s on)
- Japanese companies + newly industrialized countries threatened US/European companies → forced turn to lean production

**Critique 1: West-Centrism**:
- Perhaps explains Western adoption (though problematic even here)
- BUT: System FIRST implemented by JAPANESE companies
- **Question**: What forced Japanese companies to adopt lean production? Competition from Japanese companies?!
- Those providing/accepting this explanation "not even aware of what they are talking about"

**Critique 2 (More Important): Abstracts From Class Struggle**:
- Takes up change with indelible marks on class struggle IN ABSTRACTION from class struggle itself
- Tries to capture within framework of **competition of capitals**
- Competition = sphere where general laws of capital imposed on individual capitals

**Real Aim of Lean Production Methods**:
> "None other than bringing working class under FURTHER CONTROL by capital so as to INCREASE amount of surplus-value and RAISE rate of surplus-value."

## KEY THEMES (CHUNKS 031-032)

### 1. Fordism Debunked
- Moving assembly line = Taylorism + mechanical pacing device
- "Fordism" = vacuous concept lacking specified characteristics
- Should be buried; Taylorism should reclaim proper conceptual place

### 2. Idle Time = Central Target
- Lean production's obsessive focus: Eliminate ALL non-productive moments
- Goal: "60 minutes work every hour" (US Transportation Dept)
- Ohno: "Present capacity = work + waste; bring work to 100%"

### 3. JIT ≠ Inventory Savings (Primary Function)
- Cost savings acknowledged but SECONDARY
- **Real function**: Identification + elimination of idle time
- **Method**: Visualisation system reveals labor surpluses
- **Essence**: Management by stress/pressure
- Kaizen = continuous pressure increase (never-ending)

### 4. Task Mobility = Intensification
- Modular system (U-form stations, multiple machines per worker)
- Production worker absorbs maintenance + quality control functions
- Further idle time reduction

### 5. Quality Circles = Five-Fold Weapon
1. Extract worker knowledge (including time/motion study suggestions!)
2. TQM ideology ("customer satisfaction" reinterpreted for internal customers)
3. Corporate allegiance ("family" perception)
4. Peer pressure reversal (towards raising intensity, not lowering)
5. **Conquest of mind** (leisure time becomes thinking-for-capitalist time)

### 6. Core/Periphery Fragmentation
- **Economic basis** of core allegiance (beyond cultural factors):
  - Job security system (nengko) = economic coercion
  - Enterprise-provided social services = dependence
  - Individualized wages = subjective loyalty assessment
  - Workplace-level unions = tied to enterprise fate

### 7. Workplace Physical Fragmentation
- Supply chain model + franchising
- **Japan data**: 33-44% wage differential (suppliers vs. main companies)
- Extreme: Sweatshops/putting-out (unregistered, migrant, starvation wages)

### 8. Class Struggle (Not Competition) Explanation
- "International competition" theory = West-centric nonsense
- Abstracts from class struggle itself
- **Real aim**: Increase surplus-value extraction by bringing working class under further control
