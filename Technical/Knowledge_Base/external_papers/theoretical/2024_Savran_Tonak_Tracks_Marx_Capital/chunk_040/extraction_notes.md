### Section 5 Continuation: Great Depressions as Birth Pangs (Page 372)

**Completion of Marx's *Capital* Vol. I Chapter 32 Analysis**:

**The Proletariat as Universal Class** (p. 372):

**Dispossession process**:
- Small-holder peasants → proletariat
- Petty merchants → proletariat
- City artisans → proletariat
- Smaller/weaker capitalists → dispossessed by competition vortex

**Proletariat's Unique Historical Character**:
> "The proletariat, this specific product of the capitalist mode of production, distinguishes itself from all the preceding classes of direct producers in history by the fact that it does not in the least enjoy a share in the means of production."

**Definition**:
- Completely deprived of means of production
- Completely separated from owning means of production

**Political Implication**:
- Interests lie in defending **owners of labour power** (NOT property)
- Hence: **"universal class"** (Marx's term)
- Most adequate agent to abolish private property
- Abolishing private property = precondition for overcoming market fragmentation

**Laws of Capitalist Development** (p. 372):

**Marx's Emphasis**:
> "The entire process of capital accumulation advances through 'the immanent laws of capitalistic production itself'."

**Necessity vs Contingency**:
- Capitalism **necessarily** proceeds toward own demise based on own laws
- Through its laws, capitalism generates forces that will destroy it
- Effects may be: mitigated, temporarily arrested, reversed for certain periods
- BUT: As long as capitalism exists, at certain stage it will undermine own existence

**Conclusion**: Capitalism creates historical conditions for own demise

**Communism - Superior Civilization** (pp. 372-373):

**Inevitable Preparation**:
> "Capitalism inevitably prepares the conditions for the emergence of a civilisation that is superior to itself."

**Communism Defined**:
1. Will submit productive forces to **planning**
2. Will eliminate private property in means of production
3. Will eliminate **classes** (products of private property)
4. Will eliminate **the state** (product of private property)
5. "Promises to take human society out of prehistoric times to begin the true history of humankind"

**Current Crisis Character** (p. 373):
> "That is why the crisis that we are in the midst of today is not only a capitalist crisis, but a crisis of capitalism as a mode of production and of society."

#### Point 1: "The cooperative form of the labour-process" (pp. 373-374)

**Marx's Analysis in *Capital***:
- Traces development: cooperative labour → manufacturing → machine-based production
- Social productive power mystified as "power of capital"
- Reality: Power arises from **cooperation of workers themselves**
- Acts as **free gift to capital**

**The Collective Worker Concept** (p. 374):

**Artisan vs Modern Worker**:
- **Feudal artisan**: "This table is my product, wholly the result of my work"
- **Modern worker**: Cannot say "This airplane (or chip) is my product"
- Factory workers = **collective worker** (Marx's term)

**Need for Central Control**:
- Pre-production planning required
- Execution during production requires coordination
- Examples: Football team, philharmonic orchestra
- Control can be: **Autocratic** (capitalism) or **Democratic** (socialism)

**1970s Transformation - Supply Chains** (p. 374):

**Further Entanglement**:
- Division of production stages → **supply chains**
- Productive power of social labour → **global workforce**
- = **Globalisation**

**Enabling Technologies**:
1. **Information**: Digitalisation
2. **Communication**: Satellite, fiber optic, cellular networks
3. **Transportation**: **Containerisation** (key innovation)

**Reference**: Manuel Castells, *The Rise of the Network Society* (1996:92)

**Internationalization of Production** (p. 374):

**Beyond World Trade**:
- NOT just transit of goods/services
- NOW: **Internationalization of production**

**Marc Levinson on Containerization** (2016:2):
> "The container is at the core of a highly automated system for moving goods from anywhere to anywhere, with a minimum of cost and complication on the way. The container made shipping cheap, and by doing so changed the shape of the world economy."

**Disarticulation of Production** (p. 375):

**Tricontinental: Institute for Social Research** (*In the Ruins of the Present*, 2018):
> "Firms could break up factories and set them up in several countries at the same time—a process known as the disarticulation of production."

**Modern Commodity Production**:
- Individual parts: produced in different country
- Assembly: yet another country
- Marketing: yet another country
- Consumption: across the planet

**COVID-19 Reminder**:
> "When global supply chains were disrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic, the world was reminded of how dependent we all are on the simultaneous labour of the workers of the world."

**Marx's Assertion - *Capital* Vol. I Chapter 32** (p. 375):
> "The monopoly of capital becomes a fetter upon the mode of production, which has sprung up and flourished along with, and under it. Centralisation of the means of production and socialisation of labour at last reach a point where they become incompatible with their capitalist integument. This integument is burst asunder. The knell of capitalist private property sounds. The expropriators are expropriated." (Marx 1976:929)

#### Point 4: "The centralisation of capital" (pp. 375-376)

**Marx's Terminology** (different from mainstream):

**Concentration**:
- Growth in size of individual capitalist firms
- Through capital accumulation process

**Centralisation**:
- Bigger firms swallowing smaller firms (**acquisitions**)
- Two comparable firms joining forces (**mergers**)
- Credit system plays increasingly important role

**Empirical Evidence - Foster & McChesney (2012:32)**:

**Top 500 Global Corporations (2004-2008)**:
- Total annual revenue = **~40% of total global income**
- 1960: Only ~20% (nearly doubled!)

**Merger & Acquisition Data**:
- **1999**: $3.4 trillion (= 34% of value of all US industrial capital)
- **2007**: $4.38 trillion (up 21% from 2006, pre-crisis record)

**Foster & McChesney (2012:74)**:
> "In the end, finance has been – as it invariably is – a force for monopoly... The long-term result of this process is a ratcheting up of the concentration and centralization of capital on a world scale."

**Consequence** (p. 376):
> "The process of the concentration and centralisation of capital, all other things remaining equal, results in the continuous growth of the size of firms and enterprises. This means that, over time, the labours of ever-greater numbers of workers will be increasingly articulated and enmeshed with each other."

**Rationality of Planning** (p. 378):

It allows for the increased organisation of the production process within the division of labour between units of production, whether within the same enterprise or between distinct ones."

### Section 7: CONCLUSION (Pages 378-381)

**Summary of Chapter 15 Journey** (pp. 378-379):

**Section 1**: Established world in great depression since 2008

**Mainstream Economics Review**:
- **Denialist school**: "Simply useless"
- **Keynes (realist school)**: Relatively superior BUT unsatisfactory
  - Does NOT fully explain investment dynamics
  - Neglects production process, favors circulation
  - Cannot explain precise rhythm of periodic crises

**Marxist Theories Review**:
- **Profit-squeeze**: Unsatisfactory
- **Underconsumption**: Unsatisfactory
- **Falling rate of profit**: "Most sound theory and only one that explains periodic recurrence"

**Great Depressions - Qualitative Change** (p. 379):

**Three Great Depressions**:
1. Late 1800s (Long Depression)
2. 1930s
3. Current (2008-present)

**Character**: "Incomparably more profound and more threatening than crises of 18th and 19th centuries"

**Explanation**: Marx's theory of historical change

---

**Marx's Historical Materialism Applied** (pp. 379-380):

**Dialectical Contradiction**:
> "The catalyst is the same: the dialectical contradiction between the productive forces and the relations of production."

**Universal Pattern**:
- Applies to: Feudalism → Capitalism transition
- Applies to: Capitalism → Socialism → Communism transition
- At certain stage: Relations of production form **fetter** on productive forces

**Current Reality**:
> "The same is now happening to capitalism on a world scale."

**Central Contradiction** (p. 380):

**Socialisation vs Private Property**:
- Productive forces: Highly socialised, mutually dependent
- Workers: Collectively functioning productive unit (same/different workplaces)
- **Requires**: Planning at societal level, even world level
- Production: Immensely internationalised

**But**:
> "Capital is inimical to central planning, which requires property in common, communal property under its different forms, in order to flourish. This is the contradiction that rings the death knell of capitalism."

---

**Nothing Automatic About Transition** (p. 380):

**Requirements for Revolution**:
1. **Revolutionary break** from old world
2. **Social force**: Proletariat (no innate interest in private property)
3. Capital accumulation produces:
   - This class (proletariat)
   - New productive forces (cannot flourish in old relations)

**Marx & Engels - *Communist Manifesto***:
> "An uninterrupted, now hidden, now open fight, a fight that each time ended, either in a revolutionary reconstitution of society at large, or in the common ruin of the contending classes." (Marx & Engels 2010a:482)

**Rosa Luxemburg's Battle Cry**:
> "Socialism or barbarism"

---

**Great Depressions as Minefields** (pp. 380-381):

**First Great Depression (Late 19th Century)**:
- Overcome through: Rise of imperialism
- Led to: Forcible colonisation, enslavement of world's peoples
- Result: **First World War bloodbath**

**Second Great Depression (1930s)** (Note: appears TWICE on pages 380-381, likely editing issue in original):
- Led to: Rise of fascism and Nazism
- Result: **World War II** (minimum 60 million dead)
- Horror: **The Holocaust**

**Barbarism Defined**:
> "Colonisation, universal war, fascism: this is the barbarism Rosa Luxemburg was talking about."

---

**Great Depressions as Hotbed of Revolutions** (p. 381):

**Revolutionary Responses**:
- **Russian Revolution (1917)**
- **Chinese Revolution (1949)**
- Lesser revolutions in Europe and Asia

**Born of**: Circumstances of capitalist barbarism

**Political Lesson**:
> "The real political lesson of a study of economic crises is to organise in order to overcome the barbaric tendencies born of those capitalist crises that are called depressions."

**How Wars Ended**:
> "It should never be forgotten that both world wars came to an end not through peace negotiations, nor the preaching of peace by the good-hearted and well-intentioned, but through revolution and the power thus gained by the exploited and the downtrodden."

---

**Final Conclusion - Internationalist Revolution** (p. 381):

**Capitalism Creates Conditions for Revolution**:
1. Globalised capitalism exploits workers worldwide
2. Draws them together in cooperative work (global supply chains)
3. Develops means of production to ever-greater productivity
4. Develops **own grave diggers** = global working class

**Working Class**:
- Cooperative labour = source of all value
- Together with mother earth = source of all wealth

**Internationalization of Production** (p. 381):
> "Capital has internationalised production; it has tied the workers of all countries together; it has made one single collective work force out of what was once a multitude of nations, races, ethnicities distant and alien to each other."

**Revolution Must Be Internationalist**:
- Can only **begin** at national level
- Each national section takes on own bourgeoisie
- Fight to take over national state
- BUT: Revolution/counter-revolution always grows to regional → continental dimensions

**Strategic Conclusion**:
> "Hence, we should prepare for wresting away from the international bourgeoisie as large parts of the world economy as the course of revolution permits."
