# Quick Reference: The Four Grand Levels of Abstraction in Marx's Capital

**Source**: Savran (2024), Chapter 3, Section 5

---

## Level 1: PRODUCTION IN GENERAL

| **What** | Universal attributes of production across ALL modes of production |
| **Where** | Outside *Capital* proper - foundational substratum |
| **Basis** | Materialist conception of history (*German Ideology*, *Contribution* Preface) |
| **Function** | Foundation - necessary but insufficient for understanding capitalism |
| **Analogy** | "Foundation of building - part of edifice but outside habitable space" |
| **Present in capitalism?** | Yes - but provides no clues to capitalism's specific nature |

---

## Level 2: EXCHANGE IN GENERAL

| **What** | Analysis of commodity, value, and money |
| **Where** | Volume I, Part I ("Commodities and Money") |
| **Abstracts from** | Capital-wage labour relationship |
| **Studies** | Society with advanced division of labour + private property |
| **Key insight** | Labour can only be socially validated on market → value relation |
| **Foundation for** | Law of value (labour theory of value) + theory of money |
| **Why first?** | Labour power IS a commodity → must understand commodity first |

**Key concepts**:
- Use-value ↔ Value
- Concrete labour ↔ Abstract labour
- Commodity ↔ Money as universal equivalent

---

## Level 3: CAPITAL IN GENERAL

| **What** | Capital-wage labour relationship in isolation |
| **Where** | Volume I (Parts III+) and Volume II |
| **Abstracts from** | **Competition between capitals** (key point!) |
| **Studies** | "Laboratory conditions" - two main classes in isolation |
| **Frozen/held constant** | Relations between capital fractions; relations between units |
| **NOT assumed** | Identical organic compositions (common misconception!) |
| **Focus** | How capital exploits wage labour to produce surplus-value |

**Volume I**: Production of capital (surplus-value creation)
**Volume II**: Circulation of capital (turnover, reproduction)

**Result**: Understanding capital-wage labour as such, before competition complicates picture

---

## Level 4: MANY CAPITALS

| **What** | Competition between different fractions and units of capital |
| **Where** | Volume III ("The Process of Capitalist Production as a Whole") |
| **Abstracts from** | NOTHING - most concrete level |
| **Introduces** | Impact of competition between capitals |
| **New phenomena** | Industrial vs. commercial vs. financial vs. landed property capital |
| **Interaction** | Class struggle + inter-capital competition = concrete functioning |
| **Why "as a whole"?** | Only now complete picture of capitalist production emerges |

**Essential forms** (Vol I) → **Phenomenal forms** (Vol III):
- Value → Price of production
- Surplus-value → Profit of enterprise, commercial profit, interest, ground rent

---

## Critical Relationships Between Levels

### Moving Forward: Progressive Concretization
1. **Production in General** → foundation
2. **Exchange in General** → commodity/money (Part I)
3. **Capital in General** → capital-labour (Vols I-II)
4. **Many Capitals** → competition added (Vol III)

### Looking Backward: Incorporation Not Replacement

**CRUCIAL**: Each level **incorporates** previous levels, doesn't replace them!

- Volume III phenomenal forms = **modified** Volume I essential forms
- **Metamorphosis**, not abandonment
- **Aufhebung**: supersession AND conservation
- One reality at different levels of abstraction, NOT two separate systems

---

## Common Misunderstandings - AVOID THESE!

### ✗ WRONG: "Value system" vs. "Price system"
Two different realities made of different substances

### ✓ CORRECT: Essential forms vs. Phenomenal forms
Same reality at different levels of abstraction; phenomenal forms are transformed essential forms

### ✗ WRONG: Volume I outdated once you reach Volume III
Values no longer relevant after introducing prices of production

### ✓ CORRECT: Volume I essential, Volume III builds on it
Prices of production are value transformed by competition; values remain operative underneath

### ✗ WRONG: Volumes I-II assume identical organic compositions
All capitals have same ratio of machines to living labour

### ✓ CORRECT: Volumes I-II abstract from IMPACT of competition
Different compositions exist but their competitive effects are frozen/held constant

### ✗ WRONG: Marx discovered transformation problem after Volume I
Had to add Volume III to fix the problem

### ✓ CORRECT: Marx wrote Volume III (1864-65) before publishing Volume I (1867)
Knew about value-price divergence all along; it's capitalist reality, not theoretical difficulty

## Quick Diagnostic: Which Level Am I On?

**Ask yourself**:

1. **Are we discussing specific mode of production?**
   - No → Production in General
   - Yes → Continue...

2. **Is capital-wage labour relationship in the picture?**
   - No → Exchange in General (just commodity/money)
   - Yes → Continue...

3. **Is competition between capitals being considered?**
   - No → Capital in General (Vols I-II)
   - Yes → Many Capitals (Vol III)

---

## Why This Matters

### For Understanding *Capital*:
- Prevents confusion when moving between volumes
- Explains why different volumes have different concepts
- Shows how argument builds systematically

### For Avoiding Errors:
- Don't expect prices in Volume I (wrong level!)
- Don't ignore values in Volume III (they're still there, transformed!)
- Don't treat volumes as contradicting each other (they're one argument!)

### For Political Economy:
- Shows Marx's scientific rigor
- Reveals how capitalism mystifies itself (essence ≠ appearance)
- Demonstrates necessity of theoretical analysis (can't just look at surface)

---

## Marx's Method in One Sentence

> Move from abstract (isolate determining relations) to concrete (add back complexities) while incorporating each level into the next through transformation (*aufhebung*), never replacement.

---

## The Famous Quote

> "All science would be superfluous if the form of appearance of things directly coincided with their essence!"
> — Marx, *Capital* Vol. III, p. 956

**Meaning**: The very fact that Volume III (appearance) differs from Volume I (essence) is not a problem with the theory - it's the contradictory structure of capitalist reality itself. Science is needed precisely to show the connection.
