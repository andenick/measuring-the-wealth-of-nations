## Key Concepts and Findings

### 1. Importance of PUPL Distinction

**Theoretical Importance:**
- Essential for calculating rate of surplus-value (s/v ratio)
- Cannot use conventional national income categories (profits/wages) directly
- Impacts rate of profit calculations
- Critical for understanding capitalist crises within falling rate of profit framework

**Empirical Example Given:**
- US 1948-89: Rate of surplus-value (with PUPL distinction) increased ~44%
- Same period: Conventional profit-wage ratio declined 22%
- Reference: Shaikh & Ahmet Tonak (1994, 151)

### 3. Productive Labour in General

**Definition:** Labour engaged in **production** - the transformation and appropriation of nature to create use-values.

**Basic Set of Social Activities:**
1. Production
2. Circulation
3. Distribution of the product (income distribution)
4. Personal and social consumption
5. Reproduction of the social order

**Activities NOT Involving Labour:**
- Consumption (common to all living species, not specifically human labour)
- Distribution of the product (social relationship mediated by product, not labour itself)

**Activities Involving Unproductive Labour (in general):**
- Circulation (metamorphoses between commodity and money forms)
- Reproduction of social order (state apparatus, religious institutions)

**Examples of Unproductive Labour in ALL Social Formations:**
- Priests and religious officials
- Kings, presidents, politicians
- Public administrative employees
- Financial officers of state
- Judges, lawyers, juridical professionals
- Generals, soldiers
- Policemen, prison wardens

**Key Distinction:**
- Production mediates the **relationship of society to nature**
- Circulation/reproduction activities mediate **relations among human beings**
- Only production provides material elements indispensable for social reproduction

### 4. Productive Labour for Capital

**Basic Definition:** Labour that produces **surplus-value**

**Double Determination:**
1. Must be productive in general (transform nature, produce use-values)
2. Must produce surplus-value (specific to capitalist mode)

**Key Principle:**
- Productive labour for capital is a **subset** of productive labour in general
- Being productive in general is **necessary but not sufficient**
- Sufficient condition: production of surplus-value

**Capitalist Specificity:**
- Production not simply for use-value, but for exchange-value and surplus-value
- Production process is valorization process (self-expansion of capital)
- Labour productive only if "directly consumed in the course of production for the valorization of capital" (Marx, 1976a, 1938)

**Immediately Excluded from Productive Labour for Capital:**

1. **Labour producing only use-values** (not commodities):
   - Self-sufficient peasant households
   - Housework under capitalism
   - (Not productive for capital, though productive in general sense)

2. **Petty commodity producers:**
   - Own their means of production
   - Exchange products, not labour-power
   - No surplus-value production (requires sale of labour-power)

### 5. Important Theoretical Points

**Marx's Critique:**
- "Bourgeois narrow-mindedness" confuses productive labour from standpoint of capital with productive labour in general
- Cannot assume all labour producing "anything at all" is productive for capital

**Concept Origins:**
- 'Productive labour in general' belongs to sphere of 'production-in-general'
- Defines characteristics common to all modes of production
- Starting point for analyzing capitalist mode specifically

**Methodological Note:**
- PUPL distinction is NOT necessary for establishing distinction between production and circulation
- Production/circulation distinction is conceptually independent and PRIOR to PUPL
- PUPL is predicated upon solid understanding of production/circulation difference

**Housework Clarification (Footnote 9):**
- Housework IS production activity (not consumption)
- Necessary element for consumption goods to acquire final consumable form
- Includes socially created forms of consumption preparation

**Capitalist as Worker (Footnote 10):**
- To extent capitalist works as manager, acts as worker
- Income from managerial labour belongs to same category as other managers' salaries
- Marx considers director of labour process as productive worker
- Decisive criterion: type of activity, not position in social hierarchy
