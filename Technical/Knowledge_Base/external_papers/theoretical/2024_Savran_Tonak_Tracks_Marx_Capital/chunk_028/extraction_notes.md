### Chapter 11 Conclusion (Pages 249-251)
This section concludes the comparative input-output network analysis of the United States, Germany, and Spain. The conclusion synthesizes findings about structural vulnerabilities preceding the global financial crisis, emphasizing the Marxian distinction between productive and unproductive sectors. Key findings:

- Germany's central sectors (Motor Vehicles, Health, Food, Machinery) were productive and tradeable, providing resilience
- Spain's central sectors (Construction, Trade, Hotels-Restaurant) were non-tradeable and collapsed during the crisis
- United States showed vulnerabilities with predominantly unproductive top-ranking sectors

### Chapter 12 Introduction (Pages 255-260)
This new chapter begins a theoretical and empirical investigation of surplus-value production in the digital economy, using Facebook as a case study. Key theoretical contributions:

- Distinguishes two sources of profit: (1) Profit on Transfers ("buy cheap, sell dear"), and (2) Profit from Surplus-Value (extraction in production)
- Analyzes productive vs. unproductive labor in the digital economy
- Establishes that Facebook workers are productive laborers who produce surplus-value
- Argues that Facebook users are "petty commodity producers" who produce value but not surplus-value
- Provides detailed analysis of Facebook's production process using Marxist categories (constant capital, variable capital)

## Key Concepts and Terms

### Marxist Economic Theory
1. **Surplus-value** - Unpaid labor time extracted in production
2. **Productive labor** - Labor that produces surplus-value in production process
3. **Unproductive labor** - Labor in circulation/trade that doesn't produce surplus-value but facilitates its transfer
4. **Constant capital** - Means of production (machinery, materials, infrastructure)
5. **Variable capital** - Labor power purchased by capitalists (wages)
6. **Money capital** - Initial capital used to purchase means of production and labor power
7. **Rate of exploitation** - Ratio of surplus labor time to necessary labor time
8. **Necessary labor time** - Time needed to reproduce worker's labor power
9. **Surplus labor time** - Time worked beyond necessary labor time

### Digital Economy Concepts
10. **Petty commodity producers** - Individual producers who own means of production and work for themselves
11. **Profit on transfers** - Profit from "buy cheap, sell dear" or appropriation from productive sectors
12. **Profit from surplus-value** - Profit from extraction in production process
13. **Digital labor** - Labor involved in creating digital platforms and environments
14. **Marketable environment** - Social interaction space sold as commodity to advertisers

### Network Analysis Concepts (from Chapter 11)
15. **Betweenness centrality** - Network measure of sectoral importance
16. **Sectoral interdependencies** - Extent of connections between economic sectors
17. **Tradeable sectors** - Sectors producing goods/services for export
18. **Non-tradeable sectors** - Sectors serving domestic markets only

### Specific Empirical Terms
19. **Input-Output (I-O) tables** - Economic accounting framework showing inter-sectoral flows
20. **Network analysis** - Mathematical analysis of economic sector connections
