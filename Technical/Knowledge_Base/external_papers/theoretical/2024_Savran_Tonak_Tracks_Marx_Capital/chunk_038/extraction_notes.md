**Schumpeter Critique Conclusion** (Page 352):

**Authors' Two-Fold Critique**:

1. **Absolution of Devastation**:
> "By making crisis a category that serves the progress of humanity, Schumpeter is absolving one of the most undesirable aspects of capitalism by providing a justification for all the devastation and misery caused by this phenomenon."

> "This vision leads to a one-sided understanding of crises because it limits the destructive force of crises under capitalism to the means of production, whereas it is perfectly possible that in and through such profound crises, society may move towards the destruction of the relations, and thereby the mode, of production."

**Conclusion**:
> "The idea of creative destruction thus is so formulated as to preclude the more radical destruction that can be caused by crisis, that of revolution, capable of creating not only new means of production but a new society."

**Marx's Critique of Say's Law** (Page 353):

**Say's Reasoning**:
- Commodity producers engage in production to sell → purchase other commodities
- "Whoever sells buys"
- Aggregate: Demand created by supply

**Marx's Correction**:
- Supply creates **capacity** to purchase (not purchase itself)
- Sale and purchase are **separate transactions**
- Mediated through: Commodity form → Money form → Commodity form
- **Sellers may withhold money** (hoarding)
- If sufficiently large number hoard → shortcoming in demand → crisis possibility

**Say's error**: "Reduced the unity between sale and purchase that is mediated by money into an unmediated or direct relationship between supply and demand"

**Crisis as Problem AND Solution** (Pages 353-354):

**Dual nature**:

**As PROBLEM**:
- Reveals contradictions within capital accumulation
- Concealed during times of "heady profit-making"
- Accumulation slowdown → shortcomings surface
- Means of production undergo "moral depreciation"
- Can no longer serve competitive effort

**As SOLUTION**:
Two requirements per Marx:
1. Raise general rate of profit
2. Eliminate obsolete means of production → make way for new machinery/equipment/methods

**Devalorisation (Entwertung)** (Page 354):

**Terminology**:
- **Valorisation (Verwertung)**: Production adds new value through living labour
- **Devalorisation**: Loss in value (can reach total loss)
- Capital embodied in plant/machinery/equipment loses value quality → becomes worthless
- No longer serves as productive equipment

**Agreement with Schumpeter**: Elimination of old/obsolete/insufficiently productive machinery fundamental in both analyses

---

**Triggering Factors vs Crisis Itself** (Page 354):

**Fundamental Marxist Distinction**:
- **Triggering factors**: Cause disturbance → may set off chain reaction
- **Crisis itself**: Always different from triggering element

**Example 1 - 1973-1974 Oil Crisis**:
- **Trigger**: Rise in oil price
- **Real cause**: Fall in profit rate over extended period
- **Mechanism**: Progressive replacement of living labour by machinery/automation
- **Result**: Long depressive period of slow accumulation

**Example 2 - 2007-2009 Subprime Mortgage Crisis**:
- **Trigger**: Subprime mortgage crisis (US, 2007)
- **Real cause**: Financial system expansion "well beyond the productive base of the real global economy"
- **Mechanism**: Detonator of devastating financial collapse
- **Spread**: 2008-2009 globally

**Bourgeois Economics Problem**: Attributes root cause to triggering factor → "great confusion among the public about the nature of crises"

**Confusion**: Some deduce from unsold goods that crises are "in their essence, crises of overproduction"

**The Contradictory Part Played by Finance** (Pages 355-356)

**Core Thesis** (Page 355):
> "Marx situates the real dynamic of crisis under capitalism in the unfolding of the productive sphere of the economy, within the process of the production and accumulation of capital. The sphere of finance is not the hotbed out of which crisis grows. And yet finance plays a fundamentally important role in the subsequent unfolding of the crisis."

**Two Methods of Capital Amassment**:
1. **Bank credit**: Deposits create new tranches of credit surpassing original sum
2. **Stock market**: Duplication of value in means of production, independent of production sphere

**Fictitious Capital** (Page 356):
- Marx's term for stock market capital
- "Separates from the original capital it represents"
- "Can very well take flight towards magnitudes of value incommensurate with the original productive capital it represents"

**Finance's Role in Crisis** (Page 356):

**Self-Propagation Capacity**:
- Crisis → lack of demand, fall in investment/consumption, lack of means of payment
- Additional finance "blows new life" into struggling economic units
- Sustains economic activity
- Prevents bankruptcies (sometimes sizeable ones)

**"Over-Credit"** (Marx's term):
- Credit/finance carry economic activity beyond "confines of what is rationally possible"
- Given production system capacity at specific moment
- Confines NOT static: Extra credit/loan capital → revive economy → limits advance proportionally
- Economic units find "way out of the morosity"
- **Cost**: Rising debt

**Accumulation Dynamic**:
> "The more finance oils the machinery of a moribund economy, the more debt accumulates. In the end, financial flows reach a magnitude that is disproportionate when compared to the productive base that has given birth to all the credit and market capitalisation that have been amassed."

**Conclusion**: "Thus, the later the crash, the worse the outcome."

**2008 Application**: "This is what happened in 2008"

**Future Prediction**: "Will, in all probability, repeat itself again in the short- or medium-term"

### SECTION 4: The Profit-Squeeze Theory (Pages 357-358)

**Core Thesis** (Page 357):
> "The profit-squeeze theory is very simple: it contends that the crisis is caused by the strengthening of labour and the consequent rise in wages, thus leading to a squeeze in the profit rate."

**Historical Status**:
- Very popular end of post-war boom (mid-1940s to mid-1970s)
- Now "largely out of favour"

**Common Ground with Falling Rate of Profit Theory**:
- Both attribute crises to fall in profits
- Comparison possible

**Key Difference**:
- **Profit-squeeze**: Fall due to division of product between capitalists/workers (income distribution)
- **Falling rate of profit**: Fall due to changing composition of capital

**Mid-1970s Plausibility** (Page 357):

**Empirical Context**:
- Shrinking reserve army of labour (falling unemployment) in imperialist countries
- → Strengthened working class
- → Labor unions empowered
- → Wages rose

**Pre-crisis pattern**: "Not uncommon" - overheated economy → wages increase as economy exits strong growth

**Theoretical & Empirical**: "May very well seem flawless"

**Critical Problem** (Page 357):

**Not with**: Recognition of profit rate fall

**Problem with**: Reasons offered for the fall

**Theoretical Critique**:
- Change in income distribution between classes "can be rectified by the functioning of the capitalist economy in a rather short period"
- (NOT the case with law of falling profit rate)

**Rectification Mechanism** (Page 358):
- Capital resorts to more machinery-intensive techniques
- Sheds labour
- Increases unemployment
- → Greater competition among workers/unemployed
- → Brings down wages

**Marx's Explanation** (Footnote 2, Page 358):
> "Capital works on both sides at the same time. If its accumulation, on the one hand, increases the demand for labour, it increases on the other the supply of labourers by the 'setting free' of them, whilst at the same time the pressure of the unemployed compels those that are employed to furnish more labour, and therefore makes the supply of labour, to a certain extent, independent of the supply of labourers. The action of the law of supply and demand of labour on this basis completes the despotism of capital." (Marx, 1976)

**Marx's Insight**: Capital controls BOTH demand for AND supply of labour force → impossible to force wages above value of labour power for "meaningfully long period"

**Empirical Refutation** (Page 358):

**If theory true**: Cannot explain "crisis of major proportions that lasts for years on end"

**Proven by subsequent developments**:
- Crisis created large reserve army of labour
- → Weakened working class
- → Subsequent decrease in wages

**2008 Crisis**: Profit-squeeze theory NOT put forward
- Wages NOT high enough to squeeze profits
- Had "stagnated for decades"

**Conclusion**: "The profit-squeeze theory was, to a certain extent, influential, but its influence has not lasted."

### The Theory of Underconsumption (Pages 358-361)

**Significance** (Page 358):
> "The next trend that we will take up in Marxist crisis theory, underconsumption as the cause of all crises, is one that has had, both historically and at the present time, a great impact on Marxist thinking, an impact that has not been confined to crisis theory but has extended to attempt to explain the overall functioning of the capitalist economic system."

**Holistic Approach**:
- Consumption/demand = main drivers of capitalist mode of production
- **Striking departure** from Marx's *Capital*
- Marx: Production of surplus-value (profit) = driving force of accumulation
- Underconsumption "almost become common-sense thinking for the public"

**Diverse Schools** (Page 359):

**Representative Figures/Schools** (otherwise little in common theoretically/politically):

1. **Rosa Luxemburg** (1871-1919):
   - Developed theory of imperialism
   - Criticized Marx's *Capital* (especially Volume 2 reproduction schemes)
   - Alleged Marx "misunderstood the entire mechanism of capitalist accumulation"

2. **Monthly Review School**:
   - Paul Sweezy
   - Paul Baran

3. **French Regulation School**:
   - Michel Aglietta
   - Robert Boyer
   - Alain Lipietz

4. **Social Structure of Accumulation School**:
   - David Gordon
   - Michael Reich
   - Richard Edwards
   - Thomas Weisskopf
   - Sam Bowles

5. **Other Influential Figures**:
   - David Harvey (most famous)

**Composite Underconsumptionist Position** (Page 359):

**Core Question**: "Where does the demand for the goods that correspond to surplus-value come from?"

**Logic**:
- Surplus-value exists → value produced > (capital used up + worker consumption)
- Capitalist class won't consume entire surplus product (not why produced)
- **Who buys the goods/services embedding that value?**

**Variant Solutions** (Pages 359-360):

1. **Rosa Luxemburg**: Must be middle strata consumers (reminiscent of Malthus)

2. **Baran & Sweezy**: State expenditure + unproductive expenditures (advertising, etc.) fill gap

3. **Permanent Arms Economy**: Popular 1960s-70s

4. **Regulation/Social Structure Schools**: Welfare state as element of "Fordist regime"

5. **Harvey**: "All manner of subsidiary factors"

6. **Both Schools**: Emphasize institutions + macroeconomic policy shifts across periods → provide additional demand

**Crisis Definition**: "Result of the system's failure to provide this additional demand"

---

**What Underconsumptionists Overlook** (Page 360):

**Authors' Answer**:
> "What all schools of underconsumptionism overlook is that capitalist accumulation itself provides the answer to the question 'where does the demand for the goods that correspond to surplus-value come from?'."

**Marx's Distinction**:

1. **Simple Reproduction**:
   - Capitalist class consumes entire surplus-value as revenue
   - Unrealistic
   - Heuristic device to demonstrate expanded reproduction

2. **Expanded Reproduction** (= Capital Accumulation):
   - "Corresponds to what happens in the real world of capitalism"
   - Capital expands production scale by:
     - Using surplus-value to purchase new means of production
     - Hiring new workers
   - New workers paid wages → consume additional consumption goods
   - → Excess consumption beyond existing workers

**Simple Conclusion**:
> "If there is an accumulation of capital (or expanded reproduction), this will create, under normal conditions, sufficient demand to consume the surplus-value produced."

**Critical Error**: "Underconsumption theory rules out expanded production"

**Bukharin's Critique of Luxemburg** (Page 360):
- Nikolai Bukharin: Prominent Bolshevik Party theoretician
- Criticism: If exclude expanded reproduction at outset → "no wonder that expanded reproduction will prove impossible to explain at a later stage"
- **Reference**: Luxemburg & Bukharin, 1972

---

**Periodic Rhythm Problem** (Page 360):

**Even if correct** about lack of demand: "Cannot explain the recurrence of crises at a periodic rhythm"

**Monthly Review School Example** (Page 360):
> "It is difficult to understand how a conscious strategy of constant pump-priming fails to work at periodic intervals."

**Underconsumptionist Framework Problem** (Page 361):
- Whatever state capitalist economy is in (stagnation OR vigorous accumulation)
- "Will continue forever, or at least until there is a discrete change in the circumstances"
- **Incumbent on theory**: Explain why change comes periodically
- **Reality**: "None of the schools we have discussed have even recognised the problem, let alone provided an answer"

---

**Political Consequences** (Page 361):

**General Orientation**: "Most underconsumption theory tends to propose wage increases as a solution to capitalist crises"

**Political Importance**:
- Opens avenue → reformist policy
- Try to convince capitalists wage increases serve best interests
- Pull economy out of stagnation + help working-class families
- **Result**: "Necessarily create the illusion of common interests between the main classes"
- **Reality**: "Severe economic crisis always deepens the contradictions between capitalists and working people"
- **Effect**: "Plays the part of a sedative, blunting the working class' will to fight"

**Marx's Attack** (*Capital*, Page 361):
> "If the attempt is made to give this tautology the semblance of greater profundity, by the statement that the working class receives too small a portion of its own product, and that the evil would be remedied if it received a bigger share, i.e. if its wages rose, we need only note that crises are always prepared by a period in which wages generally rise, and the working class actually does receive a greater share in the part of the annual product destined for consumption. From the standpoint of these advocates of sound and 'simple' (!) common sense, such periods should rather avert the crisis." (Marx, 1992, 486-487)

**Conclusion**: "For these and other, more secondary, reasons, underconsumptionism simply does not stand scrutiny."
