# Extracted Quotes - Pages 136-145 (Book pages 161-170)

## Quote 1: Marx (1970, 20-21) - From "Contribution to a Critique of Political Economy" (1859)

"In the social production of their existence, men inevitably enter into definite relations, which are independent of their will, namely relations of production appropriate to a given stage in the development of their material forces of production. The totality of these relations of production constitutes the economic structure of society, the real foundation, on which arises a legal and political superstructure and to which correspond definite forms of social consciousness... At a certain stage of development, the material productive forces of society come into conflict with the existing relations of production... From forms of development of the productive forces these relations turn into their fetters. Then begins an era of social revolution."

**Context**: Used to demonstrate how Capital verifies Marx's materialist conception of history through the contradiction between productive forces and relations of production.

---

## Quote 2: Marx & Engels (1998, 11) - From "Communist Manifesto" (1848)

"Modern bourgeois society with its relations of production, of exchange and of property, a society that has conjured up such gigantic means of production and of exchange, is like the sorcerer, who is no longer able to control the powers of the nether world whom he has called up by his spells. For many a decade past the history of industry and commerce is but the history of the revolt of modern productive forces against modern conditions of production, against the property relations that are the conditions of the existence of the bourgeoisie and its rule."

**Context**: Shows how Capital provides scientific proof for theses put forward in the Communist Manifesto two decades earlier.

---

## Quote 3: Marx & Engels (1998, 24) - From "Communist Manifesto" (1848)

"The advance of industry, whose involuntary promoter is the bourgeoisie, replaces the isolation of the labourers, due to competition, by their involuntary combination, due to association. The development of Modern Industry therefore cuts from under its feet the very foundation on which the bourgeoisie produces and appropriates products. What the bourgeoisie produces, above all, are its own grave-diggers."

**Context**: Demonstrates that capital produces both the productive forces that undermine capitalism and the class (proletariat) that will bring down the social order.

---

## Quote 4: Marx & Engels (1976, 49) - From "German Ideology" (1845)

"Communism is for us not a state of affairs which is to be established, an ideal to which reality [will] have to adjust itself. We call communism the real movement which abolishes the present state of things. The conditions of this movement result from the now existing premise."

**Context**: Distinguishes scientific socialism from utopian socialism; Capital confirms that communism emerges from the real movement of society, not from ideals.

---

## Quote 5: Marx (1968, 763) - From "Capital" Volume I

"It is the negation of the negation. This does not re-establish private property for the producer, but gives him individual property based on the acquisitions of the capitalist era: i.e. on co-operation and the possession in common of the land and of the means of production."

**Context**: From the chapter "Historical Tendency of Capitalist Accumulation" - describes the dialectical nature of the transition to communism as "individual property" based on common ownership of means of production.

**Key phrase emphasized**: "individual property"

**Significance**: Marx characterizes communist property form as "individual property" but one that rises on the basis of common property in the means of production - a dialectical synthesis (Aufhebung).

## Key Conceptual Phrases

1. **"the transformation of the instruments of labour into instruments of labour only usable in common"**
   - The key phrase anticipating humanity's transition to a new epoch

2. **"collective interest has become the highest mediation of individual interest"**
   - The central theoretical proposition explaining why communism is historically necessary

3. **"the material bases of the struggle over interests within and between human societies will be disappearing"**
   - Marx's anticipation of a fundamentally new stage in human history

4. **"based on the acquisitions of the capitalist era"**
   - The return to individual property conserves capitalism's achievements (Aufhebung - supersession and conservation)

5. **"Capital is as revolutionary a text as the Communist Manifesto!"**
   - Author's thesis about the unity of Marx's theoretical and political work
