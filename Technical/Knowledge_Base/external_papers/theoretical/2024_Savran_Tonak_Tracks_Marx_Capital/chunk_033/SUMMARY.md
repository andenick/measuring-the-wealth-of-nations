# Executive Summary: Chunk 033 (Pages 301-310)

## Chapter 13: Lean Production and Flexibility (Continuation)

### Core Argument

Lean production represents capital's most successful post-1980s strategy for intensifying exploitation through both absolute and relative surplus-value extraction, while flexibility operates as a one-sided class weapon that atomizes the working class.

### Historical Genesis

**Japanese Origins (1945-1975)**
- Japanese capital faced 1:9 productivity gap with US (via 1:3 German ratio)
- Ohno's Toyota Production System emerged from competitive necessity to "eliminate waste"
- "Waste" elimination = forcing workers to work more by reducing idle time
- Competition drove compliance with capital's fundamental law: increase surplus-value

**Global Spread (1975-1980s)**
- Mid-1970s economic crisis tightened world market competition
- US/European capital adopted Japanese techniques after falling behind
- Lean production became component of broader neoliberal assault alongside: free market policy, deregulation, privatization, de-unionization, commodification of social services

### Marxist Analysis: Surplus-Value Mechanisms

**Two Forms of Surplus-Value Production**

1. **Absolute Surplus-Value** (extending/intensifying labor)
   - Extending working time (workday, workweek, workyear)
   - Raising labor intensity (faster pace = more labor in same chronological time)

2. **Relative Surplus-Value** (reducing variable capital value)
   - Pushing real wages down (limited by subsistence requirements)
   - Increasing labor productivity (cheaper commodities = lower variable capital value)

**How Lean Production Increases Both**

*Absolute*:
- Unpaid overtime, working through breaks/vacations
- Working times systematically longer than non-lean companies
- Supply chain workers in sweatshops/putting-out systems work even longer (unorganized, legally unprotected)

*Relative*:
- Divided work collective = lower overall wage levels
- "Atypical" contracts (temp, part-time, term) = systematically lower wages
- Subcontractor/supply chain wages incomparably lower than monopoly core

**Labor Productivity vs. Labor Intensity Distinction**

*Productivity increases* (same pace, more usable output):
- JIT eliminates inventory clutter management time
- Worker-performed quality control eliminates defect rectification waste

*Labor intensity increases* (THE PRIMARY MECHANISM):
- **Constantly pushing pace to limits of human possibility**
- Stress/line balancing through pressure eliminates every idle moment
- Kaizen (continuous improvement) captures smallest particles of labor
- Task mobility (multi-machine operation + maintenance + quality control) gobbles unreachable moments
- **Production without inventory: fulfill quota with minimum workers at maximum intensity**

### Flexibility Taxonomy

**Production-Related (workplace struggles)**
1. Functional: task mobility + interchangeability for reallocating surplus labor
2. Time: worktime/leisure boundary erosion via JIT, TQM, HRM
3. Supply: monopoly capital's maneuverability over scattered suppliers (offshoring, supply chain management)

**Circulation-Related (societal struggles)**
1. Flexible employment: non-standard contracts now dominant (contingent work heyday)
2. Numerical: peripheral temp belt enables layoffs; core workers lack job security (even Japan legally permits employer termination)
3. Labour contract: subcontracting divides collective worker (security, cleaning, food, etc.)
4. Wage: periodic reassessment by dedication/allegiance criteria; high bonuses (Japan: 40% remuneration); highest wage flexibility = Japan
5. Social services/rights: welfare state truncation + recommodification; company provision divides class by enterprise; "cafeteria system" individualizes needs → **atomization goal**

### Ideological Critique: "Is Flexibility Positive?"

**Fundamental Question**: Flexibility for whom?

**Answer**: Overwhelmingly one-way relationship—**capitalist flexibility = worker rigidity/ruthlessness**

*Numerical flexibility example*:
- Never about restricting hiring when demand rises (socially irrational)
- Always about layoff freedom when demand falls
- Worker perspective: gross ruthlessness, not flexibility
- Positive view = capitalist class perspective

*Time flexibility (annualization) example*:
- Short hours: worker doesn't choose timing = no flexibility
- Long hours: health-deleterious + eliminates overtime pay
- Doubly negative for workers

### Conclusion

Lean production succeeds for bourgeoisie in general economic crisis context by controlling business management and labor process to maximize surplus-value extraction. The flexibility discourse masks class assault aimed at atomizing working class and reversing post-war gains.
