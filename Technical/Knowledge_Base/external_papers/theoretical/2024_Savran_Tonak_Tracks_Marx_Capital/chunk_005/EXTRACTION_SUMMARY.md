### 2. Grundrisse Historical Analysis (Ch. 2)

**Central Thesis**: Without Grundrisse (1857-58), neither *A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy* (1859) nor *Capital* (1867) could have been written.

**Writing Context:**
- Winter 1857-1858, less than one year
- Motivated by Great Crisis of 1857
- 824 pages in 7 notebooks
- Never intended for publication (self-clarification)
- Marx never returned to it; Engels never mentioned it

**Publication Odyssey:**
- 1939-1941: First edition in Moscow (3,000 copies each volume)
- Only 3-4 copies reached West initially
- 1953: Dietz Verlag East Berlin edition (basis for all translations)
- 1973: First complete English translation (Nicolaus)
- 1986-1987: Marx-Engels Collected Works vols. 28-29 (35,000 copies)
- **Total**: 22 languages, 500,000+ copies

**1968 Watershed:**
- Rosdolsky: *The Making of Marx's Capital*
- Nicolaus: "The Unknown Marx" (NLR article, Isaac Deutscher award)
- Brought Grundrisse to New Left attention

**Structure Comparison:**

## 4. Notable Historical Details

### Marx's December 8, 1857 Letter to Engels
Reveals state of mind during Grundrisse writing:
- Family economic troubles
- Daily crisis tracking in *The Economist*
- Banking Act of 1844 prediction vindicated
- Embarrassment of *New York Times* (after calling Marx "absurd")
- Famous quote: *"I am working like mad all night and every night collating my economic studies so that I at least get the outlines (die Grundrisse) clear before the déluge"*

### 1857 Crisis Parallels with 2008
- **1857**: Ohio Life Insurance & Trust Company collapse → European contagion
- **2008**: Lehman Brothers collapse → global crisis
- Marx wrote 12+ articles for *New York Tribune* analyzing crisis (Nov 1857 - March 1858)

### Kautsky's Early Role
- Custodian of Marx manuscripts (via German SPD)
- Published *Theories of Surplus-Value* (1905-1910)
- First published Grundrisse "Introduction" as appendix to *A Contribution*
- This choice repeated in translations, enabling method debates before full Grundrisse available

### Cold War Knowledge Geography
- Manuscripts went to USSR (Marx-Engels-Lenin Institute)
- Wartime publication (1939-1941) limited circulation
- Western libraries: 2 copies British Library, 1 Library of Congress
- East Berlin 1953 edition broke through barrier
- Japanese (1958-65) and Chinese (1962-78) translated before English (1973)
