**Section 1: The Making of the Grundrisse (pp. 19-20)**

Publication history:
- Written in German, winter 1857-1858
- Never returned to by Marx, never mentioned by Engels
- Manuscripts held by German SPD (Kautsky)
- Volume 1: 1939 Moscow (3,000 copies)
- Volume 2: 1941 Moscow (3,000 copies)
- Only 3-4 copies reached Western world (2 British Library, 1 Library of Congress)
- 1953 Dietz Verlag East Berlin single volume edition (basis for all translations)

Translation sequence:
1. Japanese (1958-1965)
2. Chinese (1962-1978)
3. Russian, Italian, French
4. English - Nicolaus (1973), Marx-Engels Collected Works Vols 28-29 (1986-1987, 35,000 copies)
5. 22 languages total, 500,000+ copies

Key texts bringing Grundrisse to prominence (both 1968):
- Roman Rosdolsky: *The Making of Marx's Capital*
- Martin Nicolaus: "The Unknown Marx" (New Left Review, Isaac Deutscher award winner)

## 6. KEY CONCEPTS AND FINDINGS

### Methodological Concepts
1. **Reverse transformation from prices to values**: Converting national accounts (compiled in mainstream economic terms, market prices) back to Marxist value categories for empirical testing
2. **Operationalization of Marxist categories**: Making theoretical concepts measurable (productive/unproductive labour, surplus-value, organic composition)
3. **Net social wage**: Key variable for analyzing welfare state realities
4. **State expenditure functional forms**: Different categories for analyzing government spending

### Historical-Theoretical Insights
1. **Grundrisse as prerequisite**: Tonak's central argument that without Grundrisse, neither A Contribution nor Capital would exist
2. **Crisis-driven theorization**: The 1857 global crisis as primary motivation for Marx's intensive theoretical work
3. **Evolution of theoretical structure**: How weight of topics (money, commodity, capital) shifted dramatically across texts written 1857-1867
4. **Publication archaeology**: Detailed reconstruction of how Grundrisse remained nearly unknown for century, then became widely influential after 1968-1973

### Contemporary Applications (Part III Preview)
1. **Digital economy**: "Immaterial labour" claim refuted - Facebook analyzable through Capital's categories
2. **Flexibility/lean production**: Analyzed via labour process/valorisation dialectic
3. **Modern imperialism**: Economic analysis (value transfers) vs purely political analysis
4. **Third Great Depression**: Current crisis as expression of historic decline of capitalism due to productive forces/relations contradiction

### Intellectual History
1. **Kautsky's role**: Early custodian of manuscripts, published Theories of Surplus-Value (1905-1910), first published Grundrisse Introduction as appendix
2. **Cold War knowledge geography**: Texts trapped in USSR/East Germany, limited Western access
3. **Translation politics**: Japanese and Chinese translations preceded English by 15+ years
4. **1968 as watershed**: Rosdolsky and Nicolaus bringing text to New Left attention

### Biographical Details
1. **Nail Satlıgan**: Friend and comrade mentioned in footnote 8, made authoritative German-to-Turkish translation of Capital Vol. I, died at age 63 before completing all three volumes
2. **E. Ahmet Tonak**: Identified as pioneer in empirical Marxist economics (1970s onward), author of PhD dissertation on state revenues/expenditures (1984), co-author with Anwar Shaikh of *Measuring the Wealth of Nations* (1994)

### Crisis Parallels (Footnote 1)
- **1857 crisis**: Ohio Life Insurance and Trust Company collapse → spread to Europe
- **2008 crisis**: Lehman Brothers bankruptcy → global contagion
- Marx's articles for New York Tribune (Nov 1857 - March 1858): dozen+ articles analyzing crisis
