## Key Concepts and Findings

### From Foreword (Boratav):

1. **Operationalizing Marx's Capital**: The core methodological challenge of translating abstract value theory into empirical measurement
   - Transformation problem: values → prices
   - S/V (rate of exploitation) → π/(W+π) (profit share)

2. **Decomposition of Surplus-Value**:
   - Financial capital's share
   - Rents from asset appreciation vs. surplus-value
   - Realized capital gains vs. surplus-value
   - Hidden profits (executive compensation) vs. labor wages
   - Unproductive vs. productive worker wages
   - State appropriation (welfare vs. repressive functions)

3. **Book Structure** (Four Parts):
   - Part I: Marx's theory and method
   - Part II: Measuring exploitation empirically
   - Part III: Contemporary capitalism (productive forces, imperialism, crises)
   - Part IV: Value theory vs. neo-Ricardian approach

4. **Historical Context**:
   - Post-golden age capitalism (post-1970s)
   - Neoliberalism's failures
   - 2008-09 global financial crisis as turning point
   - Current phase: "moribund and aggressive" imperialism
   - Ongoing capitalist depression

5. **Neo-Ricardian Debate**:
   - Authors reject integration of neo-Ricardian school with Marxism
   - Key difference: absence of surplus-value analysis in neo-Ricardian framework
   - Sraffa's contribution valid as critique of neoclassical economics, but incompatible with Marxist political economy
