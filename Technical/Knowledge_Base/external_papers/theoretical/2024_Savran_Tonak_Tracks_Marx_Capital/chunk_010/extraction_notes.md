### Main Theoretical Arguments

**Pages 64-66 (91-93)**: The Historical vs. Natural Forms
- Marx's critique begins with the historicity of capitalist relations
- Political economy treats capitalist categories as "natural forms" rather than historically specific "social forms"
- Smith's four stages of history (hunting, herding, agriculture, trade) and the misconception that this represents historical materialism
- The key error: political economy sees capitalist relations as "adequate to human nature" and therefore eternal

**Pages 66-67 (93-94)**: Marx's Critique Clarified
- Marx quotes himself from "The Poverty of Philosophy": economists see feudal institutions as "artificial" and bourgeois institutions as "natural"
- The famous quote: "Thus there has been history, but there is no longer any"
- Smith's eternalisation of capitalism through exchange and division of labour
- Marx's counter-argument: division of labour can exist without commodity exchange

**Pages 67-69 (94-96)**: The Meaning of Critique of Political Economy
- Section 3 begins: "Critique of Political Economy"
- What political economy takes as an assumption becomes the problem itself for Marx
- Engels quote: "where others had seen a solution, [Marx] saw nothing but a problem"
- The shift in object of investigation: from studying quantitative laws to studying the conditions of existence and reproduction of capitalist relations
- Long footnote (7) on Marx's 1844 Economic and Philosophical Manuscripts establishing the basis for critique

**Pages 69-70 (96-97)**: Consequences of the Shift
- Marx's problematic is broader: production and reproduction of relations of production
- Understanding the "laws of motion of capitalism as a historical totality"
- Marx's work is not merely "economic theory" but a "general science of social relations"
- Capital studies the production and reproduction of commodity producer relations (value theory) and capitalist-worker relations (theory of capital)
- Footnote (8) on "Results of the Immediate Process of Production" (unpublished sixth chapter)

**Pages 70-73 (97-100)**: Section 4 - Case Study on Value
- Section 4: "A Case Study: Value as a Relation of Production"
- The necessity of viewing capitalism from "outside in" - critical distance
- Marx's materialist conception of history as the foundation
- "Production in general" vs. historically specific modes of production
- The concept of "mode of production" as unity of social forms with productive forces level
- Value as the specific social form of labour allocation in commodity-producing society
- The difference from Ricardo: Marx reveals the internal nexus between labour and value
- Famous Marx quote (p. 174 of Capital): political economy never asked "why labour is expressed in value"
- Value as a social relation, not a natural category

### Key Concepts/Findings

1. **Critique of Political Economy** - not merely pointing out capitalism's historicity, but making the historically specific relations themselves the central object of investigation

2. **Natural vs. Social Forms** - political economy's fundamental error in treating capitalist categories as natural/eternal rather than historically specific

3. **Production in General vs. Mode of Production** - the dual analysis of capitalism as both continuation of universal human productive activity and historically specific form

4. **Value as Social Relation** - value is not a property of things but the necessary form that social labour takes under conditions of:
   - Advanced division of labour
   - Private property in means of production
   - Decentralized/uncoordinated production decisions

5. **Internal vs. External Nexus** - Ricardo treats labour-value relationship as external postulate; Marx reveals internal/necessary connection

6. **Materialist Conception of History** - provides Marx the theoretical foundation to view capitalism from "outside in" with critical distance

7. **Laws of Motion** - Marx's focus on historical dynamics rather than static quantitative relationships
