# Figure 3: Classification of Wage Labourers Under Capitalism

**Source**: Savran & Tonak 2024, Page 117 (chunk page 3)
**Figure Type**: Hierarchical flowchart/decision tree
**Caption**: "Classification of wage labourers under capitalism"

## Description

This is a hierarchical flowchart showing how wage labour is classified into productive and unproductive categories under capitalism.

## Structure

### Top Level
- **Wage-Labour** (main category at top)

### Second Level - Three branches:

1. **Left Branch**: Wage-Labour paid out of revenue
   - Examples in italics: (domestic servants, cooks, gardeners, chauffeurs, body-guards, etc.)
   - Classification: **Unproductive**

2. **Middle Branch**: State Employees
   - Further subdivided into three categories:
     - **Reproduction of the Social Order**: Unproductive
     - **Social Services**: Unproductive (with note: "may be productive if organized as commodity production")
     - **State Enterprises in Production and transportation**: Productive

3. **Right Branch**: Wage-Labour employed by private capital
   - Two subcategories:
     - **Workers employed in circulation**: (banks, insurance and mortgage companies, wholesale and retail trade, etc.) - **Unproductive**
     - **Workers employed in the production and transportation of goods and services** - **Productive**

## Key Visual Elements

- Rectangular boxes for main categories
- Arrows showing hierarchical relationships
- Text classifications in bold: "Productive" or "Unproductive"
- Italicized examples in parentheses for each category
- Clear branching structure from top to bottom

## Analytical Notes

The figure demonstrates the Marxist distinction between productive and unproductive labour based on:
1. Source of payment (revenue vs. capital)
2. Nature of activity (production vs. circulation)
3. Employment relationship (private capital vs. state vs. revenue-based)

The classification emphasizes that only workers employed in production and transportation of goods and services by private capital, plus state enterprises in production/transportation, are classified as productive labour in the Marxist framework.
