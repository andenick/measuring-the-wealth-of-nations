# Figure Descriptions - Chapter 11

## Figure 1: U.S.A. I-O Networks (Page 244)

**Caption**: U.S.A. I-O networks; a and b in the conventional form; c and d in the Marxian form, 1995 and 2005

**Description**:
Network graph visualization showing four panels (a, b, c, d) representing the input-output networks of the US economy:
- Panel (a): 1995 conventional I-O network
- Panel (b): 2005 conventional I-O network
- Panel (c): 1995 Marxian reconstructed I-O network
- Panel (d): 2005 Marxian reconstructed I-O network

The graphs display sectors as vertices (nodes) with size proportional to their centrality measures. Edges represent intermediate input flows between sectors, with only edges representing more than 3% of total outflows shown for visibility. In the conventional framework graphs (a, b), the Health sector appears as the largest node in 1995, while Public Administration dominates in 2005. In the Marxian framework graphs (c, d), Health remains the largest sector in both years, with other productive sectors more prominent.

## Figure 2: Germany I-O Networks (Page 245)

**Caption**: Germany I-O networks; a and b in the conventional form, c and d in the Marxian form, 1995 and 2005

**Description**:
Network graph visualization showing four panels (a, b, c, d) representing the input-output networks of the German economy:
- Panel (a): 1995 conventional I-O network
- Panel (b): 2005 conventional I-O network
- Panel (c): 1995 Marxian reconstructed I-O network
- Panel (d): 2005 Marxian reconstructed I-O network

Sectors are represented as vertices with size proportional to centrality measures. Edges show intermediate input flows exceeding 3% of total outflows. The Motor Vehicles sector appears as the dominant central node in all four panels, reflecting Germany's strong manufacturing base. The similarity between conventional and Marxian frameworks is more pronounced than in the US case, as German central sectors are largely productive and tradeable in both frameworks.

## Figure 3: Spain I-O Networks (Page 246)

**Caption**: Spain I-O networks; a and b in the conventional form, c and d in the Marxian form, 1995 and 2005

**Description**:
Network graph visualization showing four panels (a, b, c, d) representing the input-output networks of the Spanish economy:
- Panel (a): 1995 conventional I-O network
- Panel (b): 2005 conventional I-O network
- Panel (c): 1995 Marxian reconstructed I-O network
- Panel (d): 2005 Marxian reconstructed I-O network

Vertex size is proportional to counting betweenness centrality measures. Edges represent intermediate input flows above 3% threshold. The Construction sector dramatically dominates all four panels, particularly in 2005 where it shows extreme centrality. This reflects the Spanish construction boom that preceded the 2008 financial crisis. The dominance of Construction is consistent across both conventional and Marxian frameworks, highlighting the structural vulnerability of the Spanish economy.
