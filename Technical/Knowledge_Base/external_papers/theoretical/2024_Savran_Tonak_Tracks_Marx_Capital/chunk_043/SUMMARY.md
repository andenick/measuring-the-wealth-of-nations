## Chapter 17: The Negation of "Negative Values" (Conclusion)

### Main Argument
This section concludes Savran's critique of Ian Steedman's allegations that Marx's theory of value produces contradictions in the context of joint production and fixed capital.

### Key Points

1. **On Simultaneous Equations and Joint Production**
   - Steedman's "negative values" arise from forcible equalization of distinct and unequal magnitudes
   - Steedman applies a theory of value different from Marx's
   - Marx's method of simultaneous equations is appropriate only when: (a) each commodity is produced by a single method, or (b) individual values have already been reduced to social values
   - When multiple production methods exist, simultaneous equations cannot determine social values
   - Steedman's method is both theoretically flawed (assumes equal individual values where only unequal magnitudes exist) and formally flawed (creates indeterminate systems)
   - The problems of "negative values" and "negative surplus-value" are pseudo-problems from mistaking mathematical relations for real relations

2. **Internal Inconsistency of Steedman's Method**
   - Steedman contradicts himself by assuming different individual values to be equal
   - According to Steedman's own premises, value depends on labor embodied, which depends on production method
   - Different production methods should yield different values, yet Steedman assumes them equal

3. **On Fixed Capital**
   - Steedman claims Marx's treatment of fixed capital leads to contradictions
   - Steedman's "correct" method treats fixed capital as joint product using simultaneous equations
   - This produces negative depreciation quotas or negative fixed capital values
   - **Savran's Rebuttal**:
     - Steedman's method again relies on forcible equalization of unequal individual values
     - Marx's conception: linear depreciation with respect to use-value (not time)
     - Means of production transfer value equally to each unit produced during their lifespan
     - Individual values fluctuate above/below social value depending on efficiency variations
     - Over the instrument's entire lifespan, rate of profit equals the general rate
     - Three different values obtained by Marx's method represent individual values (2) and social value (1)—this is consistent, not contradictory

4. **Conclusion**
   - Steedman's anomalous results have no bearing on Marx's theory
   - They derive from misrepresentation of Marx's theory
   - "Negative values" are non-existent in theory, representing "wasted intellectual labour in practice"
   - The rebuttal removes another foundation of post-Sraffian critique of Marxist value theory

## Chapter 18: On the Theoretical Consistency of Sraffa's Economics

### Introduction

**Purpose**: To demonstrate that Sraffa's economics is internally inconsistent—it fails to provide logically and theoretically tenable solutions to its own problems within its own framework.

**Scope and Limitations**:
- Not about adequacy to global analysis of capitalism
- Not a Marxist critique of Sraffa's epistemology
- Focuses solely on Sraffa's own work, not later Sraffians
- Methodological principle: study object in isolation from secondary modifying effects

**Context**:
- Nearly two decades since *Production of Commodities by Means of Commodities*
- Little critical analysis of internal theoretical coherence
- Commentators have assumed internal consistency
- Previous Marxist critiques focused on epistemological/methodological bases

### The Problem

**Sraffa's Fundamental Purpose**:
- Investigate relationship between distribution and prices
- Isolate from output structure variations and technical changes
- System of equations based on technological requirements ("conditions of production")

**Double Requirement for Prices of Production**:
1. Provide for system reproduction by redistributing commodities in original proportions
2. Distribute surplus to ensure equal rate of profit in each branch

**Key Issue**:
- Number of unknowns exceeds equations by one
- One distributional variable must be "independent variable"
- Must be "determined from outside the system of production"
- Theory of "exogenous distribution"

### The Choice of the Independent Variable

**Three Logical Alternatives**:
1. Wage as aggregate of commodities
2. Wage as a price
3. Rate of profit as independent variable

#### (1) The Wage as an Aggregate of Commodities

**Savran's Argument**: This method cannot be used in Sraffa's framework

**Reasoning**:
- Contradicts construction of the standard commodity
- Requires inclusion of all wage commodities as inputs
- Every wage change alters entire input structure
- Different technological matrix for each wage level
- Each technological matrix has unique standard commodity
- Standard commodity changes with distribution changes—contradicts its purpose (to remain constant)

**Importance of Standard Commodity**:
- Part I: Isolates movements of particular prices
- Part II (joint production): Essential for rigorous analysis of both prices and distribution
- Without it under joint production:
  - Wage and profit rate may fall simultaneously (no inverse relationship)
  - No unique relationship between wage level and profit rate
- Absolutely indispensable to Sraffa's analysis
- Rejecting it means discarding analysis of joint production, fixed capital, and rent

**Note**: Sraffa himself never attempts to fix wage as aggregate of commodities

#### (2) The Wage as a Price

**Two Problems Identified**:

**First Problem**: Nature of necessaries in relation to rate of profit
- Wage has two components: subsistence + surplus
- Necessaries (subsistence) are essentially basic commodities by nature
- Abstract, post factum wage excludes them from basic commodities set
- They no longer appear as inputs in production equations
- Sraffa's own quote: "The drawback of this course is that it involves relegating the necessaries of consumption to the limbo of non-basic products"
- Improvements in production of necessaries would no longer directly affect profit rate and prices
- "Necessaries however are essentially basic" (Sraffa)

## Theoretical Significance

1. **For Marx Scholarship**: Defends Marx's value theory against Steedman's critique by showing the critique misrepresents Marx's method

2. **For Sraffian Economics**: Begins systematic internal critique of Sraffa's theoretical consistency, focusing on fundamental problems with the independent variable

3. **Methodological Contribution**: Demonstrates importance of distinguishing individual values from social values in value theory

4. **Debate Context**: Part of the Marxist response to the post-Sraffian critique that emerged following Sraffa's work
