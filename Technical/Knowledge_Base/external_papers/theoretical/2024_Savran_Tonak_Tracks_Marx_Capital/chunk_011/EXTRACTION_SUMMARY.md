## Key Theoretical Contributions

### Chapter 4 - Core Arguments
1. **Triple Critique**: Marx's work operates on three levels:
   - Critique of political economy (scientific)
   - Critique of bourgeois ideology (ideological)
   - Critique of capitalist reality itself (material)

2. **Commodity and Capital Fetishism**:
   - Under capitalism, social relations appear as relations between things
   - "Inverted reality" - products dominate producers (not vice versa)
   - This inversion is objective, not merely psychological
   - Capitalist reality itself generates bourgeois ideology
   - Capital appears as natural/eternal rather than as social relation

3. **Why Political Economy Failed**:
   - Remained "within its bourgeois skin"
   - Avoided fetishism at commodity level but succumbed to it with capital-form
   - Scientific rigor (Ricardo) insufficient without critical perspective

### Chapter 5 - Opening Arguments
1. **Importance of Productive/Unproductive Labour Distinction**:
   - "Vital for accumulation" - only productive labour produces surplus-value
   - Unproductive labour is "positive restraint on capital accumulation"
   - Wages of unproductive workers paid from surplus-value of productive workers

2. **Impact on Key Variables**:
   - Variable capital = wages of productive workers only (not total wage bill)
   - Surplus-value = capitalist revenues + unproductive worker wages
   - Rate of surplus-value determined by productive/unproductive division

3. **Methodological Approach**:
   - Systematic reconstruction (not polemic)
   - Twin goals: clarification AND classification
   - Based on labour theory of value framework

## Major Concepts Defined

### From Chapter 4
- **Commodity Fetishism**: Social relations taking form of relations between things
- **Inverted Reality**: Subject-object reversal in capitalism
- **Capital Fetishism**: Advanced fetishism where capital appears natural
- **Valorization Process**: Capital self-expansion through surplus-value creation
- **Dual Character of Production**: Labour process + valorization process unity

### From Chapter 5
- **Productive Labour**: Labour that produces surplus-value for capital
- **Unproductive Labour**: Labour that does not create surplus-value
- **Variable Capital**: Wages of productive workers only
- **Reconversion of Surplus-Value**: Process requiring productive labour exchange
