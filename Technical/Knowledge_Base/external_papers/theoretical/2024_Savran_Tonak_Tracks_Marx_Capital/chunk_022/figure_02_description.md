# Figure 2: Rates of Exploitation of Productive and Unproductive Workers

**Page**: 192 (book page 192)

**Type**: Line graph with time series data

## Visual Description

The figure shows a time series graph covering the period 1988-2006. It displays two trend lines:

1. **Productive workers (ep)** - Solid line
2. **Unproductive workers (eup)** - Dashed line

The y-axis ranges from 0.00 to 3.50 (representing exploitation rates as decimal/percentage values).

The x-axis shows years from 1988 to 2006.

## Key Features

- Both lines show an overall increasing trend throughout the period
- A horizontal dotted reference line appears at approximately 1.80
- There is a notable annotation showing "(eup)/(ep) = 0.51" indicating the ratio reached its lowest point in 1999
- The productive workers' exploitation rate rises more steeply than unproductive workers
- After 1999, both rates show more marked increases
- By 2006, the productive workers' rate reaches approximately 3.00 (300%)
- By 2006, the unproductive workers' rate reaches approximately 2.20-2.30 (220-230%)

## Data Points

### Notable observations:
- **1999 Crisis Point**: The unproductive workers' exploitation rate fell to half the level of productive workers
- **2006 Final Values**:
  - Productive workers: 312%
  - Unproductive workers: 228%

## Interpretation

The graph illustrates the divergence between exploitation rates of productive and unproductive workers in Turkey. The text explains that by 2006, productive workers had 6 hours of their 8-hour workday appropriated by capitalists, while unproductive workers had 5.5 hours appropriated.
