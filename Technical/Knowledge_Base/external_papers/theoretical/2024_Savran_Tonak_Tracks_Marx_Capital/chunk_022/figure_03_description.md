# Figure 3: Rates of Exploitation in Trade, Finance and Public Sectors

**Page**: 192 (book page 192)

**Type**: Multi-line graph with sectoral breakdown

## Visual Description

The figure shows a time series graph covering the period 1988-2006. It displays three trend lines representing different unproductive sectors:

1. **Trade** - Dashed line (upper trajectory)
2. **Finance** - Dot-dash line (middle trajectory)
3. **Public** - Dotted line (lower trajectory)

The y-axis ranges from 0.00 to 6.00 (representing exploitation rates).

The x-axis shows years from 1988 to 2006.

A vertical line appears around 1999-2000, marking the crisis period.

## Key Features

### Trade Sector
- Starts around 3.00-3.50 in 1988
- Shows high volatility throughout the period
- Peak around 1996 at approximately 5.50-5.60
- Sharp drop during 1999-2000 crisis to about 2.50-3.00
- Recovers post-2000 and reaches approximately 4.80-5.00 by 2006
- Displays the highest exploitation rates overall

### Finance Sector
- Starts around 0.70-1.00 in 1988
- Relatively stable until mid-1990s at around 1.00-1.50
- Tracks closely with public sector until 1999-2000 crisis
- **Dramatic increase post-2000**: leaps by 170%
- Reaches approximately 3.10 (310%) by 2006
- Shows sharp divergence from public sector after 2000

### Public Sector
- Starts around 2.80-3.00 in 1988
- Declines through early 1990s
- Relatively stable around 1.00 for most of the period
- Lowest volatility among the three sectors
- Shows 35% rise between 2001-2006
- Reaches 104% (approximately 1.04) by 2006
- **No general rising trend** for the overall period
- Lowest exploitation rate among unproductive sectors after 2000

## Horizontal Reference Line

A dotted horizontal line appears at approximately 1.00-1.20, serving as a reference point.

## Critical Observations

1. **Pre-1999 Pattern**: Finance and public sectors move closely together
2. **1999-2000 Crisis**: Marked by vertical line, serves as major inflection point
3. **Post-2000 Divergence**: Finance sector dramatically separates from public sector
4. **Trade Sector Volatility**: Highest fluctuations, reflecting cyclical sensitivity
5. **Structural Hierarchy** (by 2006):
   - Trade: ~500% (highest)
   - Finance: ~310% (middle)
   - Public: ~104% (lowest)

## Interpretation

The graph demonstrates sectoral differences in exploitation rates within the unproductive sphere. The 1999-2000 crisis marks a turning point, after which:
- Finance sector exploitation accelerates dramatically (170% increase to 310%)
- Trade sector remains highly volatile but elevated
- Public sector remains relatively stable and lowest

This pattern reflects Turkey's neoliberal restructuring, with privatization and IMF-mandated reforms creating differential impacts across sectors.
