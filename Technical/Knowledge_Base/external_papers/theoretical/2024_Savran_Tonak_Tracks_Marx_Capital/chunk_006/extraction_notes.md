## Key Concepts & Findings

### Major Theoretical Contributions

1. **Marx's Four-Book Plan** (finalized by 1866):
   - Book 1: Production Process of Capital
   - Book 2: Circulation Process of Capital
   - Book 3: Form of the Process as a Whole
   - Book 4: Contribution to the History of the Theory
   - These correspond to the three volumes of Capital plus Theories of Surplus-Value

2. **Historicity of Capitalism**:
   - Central critique: Political economy treats historically specific categories as universal
   - Capitalism is "historical and therefore transitory"
   - Example: Robinson Crusoe metaphor reveals inability to see capitalism's specificity

3. **Method of Research vs. Method of Presentation**:
   - Research: method of abstraction (same as natural sciences)
   - Presentation: constructing "artistic whole" from research results
   - Grundrisse shows research in progress; Capital shows final presentation

4. **Economic Spheres**:
   - Production, consumption, distribution, exchange
   - Primacy of production established
   - Redefinition based on relationalities and interdependence

5. **Conceptual Hierarchy**:
   - Must develop: value → exchange-value → money → labor → capital
   - Cannot transition directly from labor to capital
   - Marx quote (p. 27): "To develop the concept of capital it is necessary to begin not with labour but with value"

6. **Commodity Theory**:
   - Commodity as unity of two aspects: use-value and exchange-value
   - Use-value becomes commodity only through specific economic relation
   - Example: wheat has same use-value under different production relations

7. **Capital as Social Relation**:
   - Capital is not solely money
   - Capital is a social relationship reproduced through accumulation
   - Context for developing surplus-value concept

8. **Labor Power**:
   - Specific commodity appearing only under capitalism
   - Historically specific, not universal
   - Prerequisite for conceptualizing surplus-value

9. **Classical vs. Vulgar Political Economy**:
   - Classical (Smith, Ricardo): "naively describe" antagonistic productive relations
   - Vulgar (Bastiat, Carey): characterize capitalism as harmonious

### Historical Context

1. **Marx's Work Periods**:
   - 1857-1861: Preparation for Capital (Grundrisse period)
   - 1861-1863: Work on Theories of Surplus-Value
   - 1864-1865: Work on Capital Volume Three
   - 1867: Publication of Capital Volume One
   - Post-1867: Continued work on all volumes plus translations

2. **Evolution of Plans**:
   - Early Grundrisse index: (I) Value, (II) Money, (III) Capital in General
   - Final four-book plan communicated to Engels (July 31, 1865) and Kugelmann (October 13, 1866)
   - Crisis volume indicated in Grundrisse was abandoned (noted in 1859 Preface)

3. **Textual Development**:
   - 1857 "Introduction" replaced by 1859 "Preface"
   - "Value" subsection became "The Commodity"
   - Grundrisse material developed in Contribution (1859) and Capital (1867)

### Methodological Insights

1. **Grundrisse as Research Notes**:
   - Not written for publication
   - Shows Marx "practically doing his research"
   - Incomplete, with unfinished discussions
   - "Settling of accounts with political economy"

2. **Hegelian Influence**:
   - Philosophical background from Hegel
   - Break from Hegelian problematic completed during Grundrisse period
   - Distinction economics-politics rooted in civil society-state distinction

3. **Incompleteness**:
   - Some subsections "inordinately lengthy," others "skimmed over quickly"
   - Marx began commodity/value discussion at end of Grundrisse but didn't complete it
   - Explains why Grundrisse ended before systematic treatment achieved
