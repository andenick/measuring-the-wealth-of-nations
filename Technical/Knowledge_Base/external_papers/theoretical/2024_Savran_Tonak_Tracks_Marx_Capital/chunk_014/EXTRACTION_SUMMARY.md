## Key Findings

### Theoretical Content

**Main Thesis**: Defense and clarification of Marxist distinction between productive and unproductive labour (PUPL) against common criticisms.

**Core Arguments**:
1. Services can be productive when organized capitalistically (simultaneity of production/consumption, not immateriality)
2. State employees classified into three categories with different productive status
3. Adam Smith and subsequent Marxists (Poulantzas) conflated social relationship with product materiality
4. Four common objections to PUPL distinction are based on conceptual confusions

**Significance**: Establishes theoretical foundation for empirical classification of labour in national accounts.

### Citations Analysis

**Primary Sources**: Marx (3 works, 4 citations)
- Theories of Surplus Value (1963)

- Capital Vol. 1 (1976b)

- Five critics (Gough, Harrison, Hunt, de Vroey, Laibman) - collectively addressed

**Footnote Density**: 7 footnotes across 10 pages (substantive, not just bibliographic)

1. **"Productive Labour in General"** - Transhistorical concept as necessary condition
2. **Services Properly Defined** - Simultaneity of production/consumption
3. **Double Determination** - Productive under capitalism = subset of productive in general
4. **State Employee Taxonomy** - Three-category classification system

### Contemporary Relevance
