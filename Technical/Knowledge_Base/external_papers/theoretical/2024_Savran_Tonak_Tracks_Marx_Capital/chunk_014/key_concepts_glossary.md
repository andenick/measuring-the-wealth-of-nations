# Key Concepts Glossary - Chunk 014
## Productive and Unproductive Labour Theory

## Core Concepts

### Productive Labour (Under Capitalism)
**Definition**: Labour that produces surplus-value for capital; labour employed by capital that contributes to capital's self-valorization.

**Dual Criteria**:
1. Must be productive *in general* (transforms nature to satisfy human needs)
2. Must be employed by capital (wages paid from capital, not revenue)

**Key Point**: Productive labour under capitalism is a **subset** of productive labour in general.

**Example**: Schoolmaster in private school enriching owner = productive; same teacher in free public school = unproductive.

---

### Unproductive Labour (Under Capitalism)
**Definition**: Labour that does NOT produce surplus-value.

**Two Types**:
1. **Unproductive by definition**: Not productive in general (e.g., police, military - reproduce social order, don't transform nature)
2. **Unproductive contingently**: Productive in general but not employed by capital (e.g., public school teachers, domestic servants)

**Key Point**: Can be wage labour without being productive labour.

---

### Productive Labour in General
**Definition**: Labour that transforms nature (or aspects of nature) to produce use-values satisfying human needs, directly or indirectly.

**Transhistorical**: Applies to all modes of production, not specific to capitalism.

**Function**: Necessary but insufficient condition for productive labour under capitalism.

**Non-Examples**:
- Circulation activities (don't transform nature)
- Reproduction of social order (police, military)
- Pure administration

---

### Services
**Traditional (Wrong) Definition**: Immaterial or intangible products; labour vs commodities.

**Correct Definition** (Savran & Tonak): Activities characterized by **simultaneity of production and consumption**; product not separable from act of producing.

**Key Points**:
- Services ARE commodities when capitalistically produced
- Services involve material inputs (scissors, chairs, ingredients)
- Services often have material outputs (haircut, meal, cleaned clothes)
- What's sold is product of labour, NOT labour-power itself

**Examples**:
- Haircuts
- Restaurant meals
- Hotel accommodation
- Private education
- Private healthcare

**Productive When**: Organized by capitalist firm selling service as commodity
**Unproductive When**: Domestic servant, or free public provision

---

### Circulation (Pure)
**Definition**: Activities exclusively concerned with change of form between commodities and money (C-M-C or M-C-M').

**Examples**:
- Buying and selling transactions
- Cashier functions
- Accounting for sales
- Banking operations
- Stock trading
- Advertising
- Marketing

**Key Distinction**: NOT the same as circulation of use-values (movement of goods through economy).

**Why Unproductive**: Cannot increase total social product; only redistributes existing product; doesn't transform nature.

**Confusion to Avoid**: Some activities in commercial enterprises ARE productive (packaging, displaying goods as necessary for consumer access) - only PURE circulation (exchange transactions) is unproductive.

---

### Production (Sphere of)
**Definition**: Activities that transform nature to create use-values.

**Includes**:
- Manufacturing
- Agriculture
- Mining
- Construction
- Transportation (continuation of production)
- Packaging (necessary for consumer access)
- Capitalistically organized services

**Excludes**:
- Pure circulation (buying/selling)
- Reproduction of social order
- Financial transactions

---

### Circulation of Use-Values
**Definition**: Flow of use-values between branches of production and to consumers.

**Universal**: Necessary in all societies with advanced division of labour.

**Not Same As**: Circulation of commodities, money, capital (specific to capitalism).

**Alternative Forms**: Central planning, communal distribution, rationing.

---

### Circulation of Commodities/Money/Capital
**Definition**: Movement of value through forms (commodity → money → commodity; or money → commodity → money').

**Historically Specific**: Peculiar to capitalism and simple commodity production.

**Can Be Eliminated**: Alternative economic systems can organize distribution without commodity exchange.

---

### Revenue
**Definition**: Income not functioning as capital; income consumed, not invested in production.

**Forms**:
- Wages spent on consumption
- Rent spent by landlords
- Interest spent by creditors
- Profits consumed rather than reinvested

**Significance**: Labour purchased with revenue does NOT produce surplus-value, regardless of what it produces.

**Example**: Wealthy person employing tailor on salary (revenue) vs capitalist employing tailor in garment factory (capital).

---

### Capital (As Employer)
**Definition**: Self-valorizing value; money advanced to purchase means of production and labour-power with aim of producing surplus-value.

**Distinguishing Feature**: Purpose is expansion of value, not consumption.

**Employs**: Productive labour (produces surplus-value).

**Two Forms**:
1. **Production capital**: Organizes production process
2. **Circulation capital**: Organizes buying/selling (employs unproductive labour in circulation functions)

---

### Surplus-Value
**Definition**: Value produced by workers beyond value of their labour-power; source of profit, interest, rent.

**Produced By**: Productive labour only.

**Not Produced In**: Circulation sphere (though realized there through sales).

**Relationship to PUPL**: Productive labour = labour that produces surplus-value; unproductive labour = labour that doesn't.

---

### Faux Frais
**Definition**: French term meaning "incidental expenses" or "overhead costs."

**In Capitalism**: Necessary costs that don't contribute to production of value.

**Examples**:
- Wages of police, military, bureaucrats (reproduction of social order)
- Costs of pure circulation activities
- Banking and financial sector costs

**Significance**: These costs are deducted from surplus-value; represent burden on productive economy.

---

### Use-Value
**Definition**: Utility of a thing; capacity to satisfy human needs.

**All Commodities Have**: Both use-value and exchange-value.

**Produced By**: Productive labour in general (transforms nature).

**Services Have**: Use-values even though may not be embodied in durable material object.

**Relevance to PUPL**: Particular type of use-value produced (steel vs haircut) is irrelevant to whether labour is productive.

---

## State-Specific Concepts

### State Enterprises
**Definition**: Firms owned wholly or partially by state that organize commodity production.

**Status**: Capitalist enterprises (produce surplus-value through wage labour exploitation).

**Workers**: Productive labour (identical to private sector workers in production sphere).

**Distinguish From**:
- State administration (unproductive by definition)
- Free social services (unproductive contingently)

---

### Social Services (State-Provided)
**Definition**: Education, healthcare, housing, etc. provided by state.

**Status**: Contingent - depends on organization.

**Unproductive When**:
- Provided free of charge
- Subsidized below cost
- Fees don't approximate market prices

**Productive When**:
- Privatized
- Fees approach market/shadow prices
- Organized as capitalist enterprise

**Empirical Question**: At what threshold of commodification does shift occur?

---

### Reproduction of Social Order
**Definition**: Activities that maintain class society, enforce property relations, manage population.

**Examples**:
- Military operations
- Police enforcement
- Prison system
- Courts and legal system
- Administrative bureaucracy
- Tax collection

**Status**: Unproductive by definition (not productive in general - don't transform nature for human needs).

**Purpose**: Maintain capitalism as system, not satisfy human needs.

---

## Methodological Concepts

### Social Relationship (of Production)
**Definition**: The form in which labour is organized; employer-employee connection.

**Crucial For**: Determining productive vs unproductive status.

**Examples**:
- Capital-wage labour (potentially productive)
- Master-menial servant (unproductive - revenue purchase)
- State-civil servant (varies by category)

**Smith's Error**: Confused social relationship with nature of product.

---

### Materialization of Labour
**Traditional (Smith) Meaning**: Labour embodied in durable physical object.

**Marx's Critique**: Materialization is social category, not physical.

**Correct Understanding**: All commodities (material or immaterial) are "materializations" of social labour in sense of representing socially necessary labour time.

**Implication**: Material vs immaterial distinction is irrelevant to productive labour classification.

---

### Production in General
**Definition**: Human transformation of nature through labour to satisfy needs.

**Transhistorical**: Applies to all human societies.

**Criterion For**: First filter for identifying productive labour under capitalism.

**Test**: Does activity transform nature (or aspect of nature) to create use-value?

---

## Error Types Identified

### Smith's Conflation
**Error**: Treating as identical:
1. Social relationship (servant paid from revenue)
2. Nature of product (immaterial service)

**Consequence**: False conclusion that all services are unproductive.

**Corrected By**: Distinguishing the two dimensions independently.

---

### Poulantzas's Extension
**Error**: Repeated Smith's conflation, then excluded service workers from working class.

**Consequence**: Misidentified class structure of contemporary capitalism.

**Additional Error**: Translation from French obscured argument.

---

### Universality Fallacy
**Error**: "Circulation necessary in all societies → circulation is productive."

**Mistake**: Confuses circulation of use-values (universal) with circulation of commodities (historically specific).

**Corrected By**: Distinguishing material flows from value-form transformations.

---

### Normativity Objection
**Error**: "Calling circulation unproductive is moralistic value judgment."

**Mistake**: Reverses causality - circulation is unproductive as objective fact (doesn't increase total product), therefore capitalism is irrational.

**Corrected By**: Grounding distinction in material fact that only transformation of nature increases social wealth.

---

### Alleged Contradiction
**Error**: "Marx says use-value type irrelevant, but excludes circulation."

**Mistake**: Ignores double determination - productive labour under capitalism must first be productive in general.

**Corrected By**: Productive labour under capitalism = subset of productive labour in general; circulation fails first criterion.

---

## Contemporary Applications

### Tertiarization
**Phenomenon**: Growth of service sector in advanced capitalism.

**Implication**: NOT necessarily growth in unproductive labour (consumer services often productive).

**Exception**: Financial services remain unproductive.

---

### Privatization
**Phenomenon**: Shift from state provision to private sector.

**Implication**: Converts unproductive labour (free social services) to productive labour (commodified services).

**Effect**: Increases productive labour share without necessarily increasing production.

---

### Business Services
**Category**: Services sold to businesses rather than consumers.

**Status Depends On Function**:
- Productive: HR, training, some information services (support production)
- Unproductive: Marketing, advertising, financial consulting (circulation support)

**Empirical Challenge**: Classifying individual firms and occupations.

---

## Abbreviations Used

**PUPL**: Productive and Unproductive Labour (the distinction)

**C-M-C**: Commodity-Money-Commodity (simple circulation)

**M-C-M'**: Money-Commodity-More Money (capital circulation)

---

## Notes on Usage

1. **Context Matters**: Same occupation can be productive or unproductive depending on:
   - Employer type (capital vs revenue, state vs private)
   - Function performed (production vs circulation)
   - Organizational form (commodity vs non-commodity)

2. **Not Moral Categories**: Productive/unproductive is analytical, not evaluative; doesn't imply useful/useless or good/bad.

3. **Precision Required**: Careful distinction between:
   - Production vs circulation
   - Productive in general vs productive for capital
   - Material flows vs value forms
   - Social relationship vs technical function

4. **Empirical Application**: Conceptual clarity established here enables empirical classification of national accounts data (presumed goal of larger work).
