### Chapter 13 Conclusion (Pages 331-336)

**Part-Time Work Analysis** (pp. 311-312):
- US empirical evidence (1976-1990):
  - Part-time jobs: +7% annual growth
  - Full-time jobs: +2% annual growth
  - Voluntary part-time: +50% (1970-1990)
  - **Involuntary part-time: +150%** (1970-1990)
- Cornell study: "demand side of labor market" (i.e., capital) drives growth
- Worker losses: remuneration, promotion chances, health insurance, unionization difficulty

**Opposite of Flexibility** (pp. 312-313):
- Real opposite: Standardized rights from centuries of struggle
- Examples of "lack of flexibility" (i.e., workers' gains):
  - Maximum workweek (40 → 35 → 30 hours)
  - Prohibition of child labor
  - Women's privileges as oppressed section
  - Limitations on employer redundancy powers
- **Flexibility = neoliberal deregulation of industrial relations**
- Standing against flexibility = defending rights, fighting atomization

**Section 8: Conclusion** (pp. 313-315):
Three major points demonstrated:

1. **Lean production/flexibility consistent with capital's nature**:
   - "Most ruthless forms of realization of capitalist class ambitions"
   - NOT radical change or supersession of capitalism
   - "Post-Fordism" concept is "completely senseless"
   - Modern techniques are "logical conclusion and apex of Taylorism"

2. **Class struggle is the essence**:
   - All dimensions bear mark of capital-labor struggle
   - Complementary to neoliberal/globalist policies

3. **Important determinant of future struggles**:
   - Will remain significant despite losing theoretical luster
   - Working-class movement needs to study and recognize these techniques

**Addressing Pessimism** (pp. 314-315):

- Historical context: Conditions have never been easy

  - Past divisions: Labor aristocracy vs subordinated proletariat

- **Marxist view**: Class struggle moves in ebb and flow (NOT linear progress)
- Revolution will triumph "when capitalism reveals in all its clarity that it can no longer serve the advance of humanity"

### CHAPTER 14: "New" Imperialism (Pages 317-320)

**Author**: E. Ahmet Tonak (Smith College, UMass Amherst)

**Opening Thesis** (pp. 317-318):
- Imperialism concept lost theoretical prestige in globalization age
- Recent theories focus on:
  - Political manifestations (wars, invasions)
  - Economic consequences (inequality, poverty)
- **Tonak's focus**: Uneven economic relations North-South as basis of political domination

**Critique of David Harvey** (p. 318):
- Harvey claims: Classical imperialism interpretation no longer explanatory
- Harvey's speculation: Direction of value transfer has reversed
- **Tonak's counter**:
  - Classical interpretations (Bukharin, Luxemburg, Lenin) still relevant
  - Harvey's reversal thesis "unsound theoretically and empirically"
  - **"Imperialism is alive and well"**
  - Continues to constrain autonomous development in South
  - Imperialist countries continue extracting wealth from global South

**Monopoly vs Competition Debate** (pp. 318-320):
- Most conceptualizations since Lenin based on monopolistic firms' power
- Lenin: Capitalism reached final stage = monopoly capitalism = imperialism
- Baran & Sweezy: Monopoly = absence of competition, Marx's value theory inapplicable
- **Tonak's argument**:
  - Competition **intensified** rather than disappeared
  - Centralization/concentration of capital → MORE competition
  - Monopoly theories rely on false notion of "free/pure/perfect competition"
  - **Marx's conception**: Real competition as **WAR** among capitals
  - Competition operates domestically AND internationally

**Real Competition** (p. 319, footnote 4):
Quote from Shaikh's *Capitalism*:
- "Antagonistic by nature and turbulent in operation"
- "As different from perfect competition as war is from ballet"
- Competition within industry → price-setting, cost-cutting
- Cost reduction via: wage cuts, longer/more intense workday, technical change
- "Competition pits seller against seller, seller against buyer, buyer against buyer, capital against capital, capital against labor, and labor against labor"
- **"Bellum omnium contra omnes"** (war of all against all)

**State's Role** (p. 320):
- State role MORE (not less) significant as capitalism expanded globally
- Primary function: Create fertile ground for profit-making (domestic + international)
- Ideological framework: Freedom (= capital mobility) and competition used fetishistically
- David Gordon: "Razor's edge of capitalist competition has sharpened"
- Every country must submit to "dictates of world competition"

**Chapter 14 Structure** (p. 320):
1. Review main forms of value transfer from South (changing relative significance)
2. Focus on Marx's "rate of surplus-value" concept (empirical use)
3. Comment on use/misuse of "super-exploitation" term
4. Suggest unexplored research areas on imperialist relations
