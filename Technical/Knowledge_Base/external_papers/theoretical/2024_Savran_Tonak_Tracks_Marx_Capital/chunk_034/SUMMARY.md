## CHAPTER 13 CONCLUSION: Lean Production and Flexibility

### Core Argument
Flexibility is **flexibility for capital**, not workers. Modern management techniques represent the "apex of Taylorism," not its supersession or a post-Fordist transformation.

### Three Major Conclusions

**1. Consistency with Capital's Nature**
- Lean production/flexibility = most ruthless realization of capitalist ambitions
- "Post-Fordism" concept is "completely senseless"
- Modern techniques are logical conclusion of Taylorism/Fordism

**2. Class Struggle as Essence**
- All dimensions bear mark of capital-labor conflict
- Complementary to neoliberal/globalist policies
- Claims of class harmony have "not an iota of truth"

**3. Future Determinant**
- Will remain important in coming class struggles
- Working-class movement must study and recognize these techniques

### Part-Time Work: Empirical Evidence (US, 1976-1990)

| Metric | Growth Rate |
|--------|-------------|
| Part-time jobs | +7% annually |
| Full-time jobs | +2% annually |
| Voluntary part-time (1970-90) | +50% |
| **Involuntary part-time (1970-90)** | **+150%** |

**Cornell Study Finding**: Growth driven by "demand side of labor market" (i.e., capital), not worker choice.

**Worker Losses from Part-Time**:
- Lower remuneration
- Reduced promotion chances
- Loss of health insurance
- Difficulty unionizing (harms entire collective)

### The Real Opposite of Flexibility

NOT "rigidity" but **standardized workers' rights** from centuries of struggle:
- Maximum workweek reduction (40 → 35 → 30 hours)
- Prohibition of child labor
- Protections for women as oppressed section
- Limitations on employer redundancy powers
- National labor laws and ILO directives

**Key Point**: Flexibility = neoliberal deregulation of industrial relations. Standing against it = defending hard-won rights and fighting working-class atomization.

### Against Pessimism

**Marxist View of History**: Class struggle moves in ebb and flow, NOT linear progress toward "natural" working-class power (the "unrealist dream" of Bernstein and late Kautsky).

Revolution will triumph "when capitalism reveals in all its clarity that it can no longer serve the advance of humanity, that it has exhausted every possibility of meeting the needs of the population of the planet."

**Each Period Has Difficulties**:
- Past: Strong unions controlled by parasitic bureaucracy
- Past: Labor aristocracy vs. subordinated proletariat divisions
- Present: Working class in imperialist countries also suffering
- Present: Division, fragmentation, quasi-atomization

## CHAPTER 14 INTRODUCTION: "New" Imperialism - Moribund Competitive Capitalism

**Author**: E. Ahmet Tonak (Smith College, UMass Amherst)

### Opening Thesis

In the age of globalization, imperialism has lost theoretical prestige. Recent theories focus on political manifestations (wars) or economic consequences (inequality), **not** on underlying mechanisms.

**Tonak's Focus**: Uneven economic relations between North and South as **basis** of political domination. Value transfer mechanisms reproduce unevenness through global capital accumulation.

### Critique of David Harvey

**Harvey's Claims**:
- Classical imperialism interpretations (Bukharin, Luxemburg, Lenin) no longer have explanatory power
- Direction of value transfer has reversed in recent decades

**Tonak's Counter-Argument**:
- Cannot dismiss classical contributions
- Reversal thesis is "unsound theoretically and empirically"
- **"Imperialism is alive and well"**
- Continues to constrain autonomous development in global South
- Imperialist countries continue extracting wealth from South

### The Competition vs. Monopoly Debate

**Dominant View Since Lenin**:
- Capitalism's final stage = monopoly capitalism = imperialism
- Monopoly = absence of competition (Baran & Sweezy)
- Marx's value theory inapplicable to monopoly stage

**Tonak's Argument**:
- Competition **intensified** rather than disappeared
- Centralization/concentration of capital → MORE competition, not less
- Monopoly theories rely on false notion of "free/pure/perfect competition"
- **Marx's real competition** = WAR among capitals (and among workers)

### Real Competition (Shaikh)

"Antagonistic by nature and turbulent in operation... as different from perfect competition as war is from ballet."

**Competition Process**:
- Pits seller vs. seller, seller vs. buyer, buyer vs. buyer
- Capital vs. capital, capital vs. labor, labor vs. labor
- **"Bellum omnium contra omnes"** (war of all against all)
- Forces cost reduction via wage cuts, work intensification, technical change
- Operates domestically AND internationally

### The State's Expanding Role

**Contrary to popular belief**: State role has become **MORE** (not less) significant as capitalism expanded globally.

**Primary Function**: Create fertile ground for profit-making (domestic + international)

**Ideological Framework**: Freedom (= capital mobility) and competition used fetishistically

**David Gordon**: "The razor's edge of capitalist competition has sharpened: local enterprises are increasingly constrained to match the lowest-cost producers in competitive global markets or risk getting their wrists slashed."
