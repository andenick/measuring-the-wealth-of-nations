### Introduction to Neo-Ricardianism (Page 387)

**Definition**:
> "Neo-Ricardianism is the name given to a school in economic theory that was highly influential among the left intelligentsia internationally in the 1970s and 1980s."

**Origins**:
- Owes achievements to **Piero Sraffa** (Italian economist)
- Friend of Antonio Gramsci (younger days)
- Later years: Cambridge, UK
- **Magnum opus**: *Production of Commodities by Means of Commodities* (1960, simultaneous Italian/English publication)

**Two-Stage Attack**:
1. **First**: Used Sraffa's framework to attack neoclassicism (dominant economics school)
2. **Second**: Turned arrows on Marxist critique of political economy

**Result**: International controversy (1970s-1980s), subsided early 1990s

### Section 1: Sraffa, Ricardo and Neoclassical Economics (Pages 388-390)

**Sraffa's *Production of Commodities*** (p. 388):
- **Length**: Dense treatise, merely 100 pages
- **Subtitle**: *Prelude to a Critique of Economic Theory* (Sraffa 1960)
- **Focus**: Relationship between price formation and distribution (wages vs profits)
- **Method**: Very strict assumptions isolating question from other factors (esp. technology changes)

**Ricardo Connection** (p. 388):
- **Goal**: Resuscitate David Ricardo's approach
- **Ricardo's work**: *Principles of Political Economy and Taxation* (1817)
- **Ricardo's problem**: Labour theory of value ran into trouble explaining distribution's effect on values
- **Ricardo's quest**: "Invariable measure of value"
  - Separate value determination by labour from distribution impact
  - Posthumous essay: *Absolute Value and Exchangeable Value* (1823, immediately before death)

**Sraffa's Solution - Standard Commodity** (pp. 388-389):

**Definition**:
- "Average" mix of commodities
- Total value remains **invariant to changing distribution** (static economy, no productivity change)
- Composite (arbitrary) mix of goods
- Shows distribution (profits/wages) along single dimension
- Individual goods' prices irrelevant (though each changes with distribution)

**Limitations**:
- Practical significance limited
- Invariably modified by slightest change in production conditions of any commodity

**Neo-Ricardian Label** (p. 389):
- Effort to revive Ricardian economics
- **BUT**: Followers NOT defenders of labour theory of value
- Considered labour theory "indefensible and hopelessly contradictory" (Ricardian AND Marxist versions)

**Cambridge Controversy** (p. 389):

**Combatants**:
- **Cambridge, MA (USA)**: Neoclassicists
- **Cambridge, UK**: Sraffians

**Neo-Ricardian Critique of Neoclassicism** (p. 389):

**Core Argument**:
> "The assertion of the impossibility of measuring the value or magnitude of capital prior to and independent of the rate of profit"

**Consequences**:
1. Demolishes "production function" concept
2. Destroys "marginal productivity" explanation of distribution
3. Production function holds **ONLY** for single-sector economy

**"Reswitching" Phenomenon** (p. 389):
- **Neoclassical wisdom**: Inverse relationship between capital intensity and profit rate
- **Sraffa's finding**: Once we leave single-good world:
  - More capital-intensive technique can cede to labour-intensive as profit rate rises
  - BUT may again become profitable at even higher profit rates
- **Impact**: Capital reversing severs canonical tie between marginal productivity and profit rate

**Paul Samuelson's Verdict** (p. 389):
> "We are all Sraffians now"

**BUT** (p. 390):
- Neoclassical economics proved resilient
- Continues dominating academic curriculum worldwide
- One-sector production function still used across discipline

### Section 2: The Neo-Ricardian Critique of Marx's Value Theory (Pages 390-391)

**Cambridge Controversy Impact** (p. 390):
- Created urge to examine Sraffa's implications for Marxist political economy
- **Note**: Many neo-Ricardians (including Sraffa) had Marxist background

**Two Stages of Neo-Ricardian Interest** (p. 390):

**Stage 1 (1970s)**:
- **Claim**: Serious deficiencies in Marxist critique
- **BUT**: Can be overcome through Sraffian insights
- **Goal**: Salvage Marxist project
- **Key figures**: Maurice Dobb, Ronald Meek (veteran British Marxist economists)

**Stage 2 (Post-1977)**:
- **Watershed**: Ian Steedman's *Marx After Sraffa* (1977)
- **Claim**: Much Marxist economics must be **discarded** (not buttressed)
- **Goal**: Replace with Sraffianism (for logical consistency)
- **Key figures**:
  - Ian Steedman (Britain)
  - John Eatwell (Britain)
  - Geoff Hodgson (Britain)
  - Alessandro Roncaglia (Italy)
- **Influential but inconspicuous**: Joan Robinson (Britain), Pierangelo Garegnani (Italy), Luigi Pasinetti (Italy)

**Three Major Neo-Ricardian Propositions** (pp. 390-391):

**1. Critical Proposition**:
- Marx's labour theory of value = **inappropriate** for analyzing capitalism
- Based on claims theory is:
  - (a) Fraught with logical inconsistencies, AND/OR
  - (b) Redundant (therefore superfluous)

**2. Affirmative Proposition**:
- Most fundamental questions for capitalism analysis can be treated:
  - Satisfactorily
  - Logically consistently
- Using Sraffa's framework

**3. Theoretical Proposition**:
- Materialist theory of capitalism can be erected on Sraffian basis
- **Independently** of Marx's value theory
- **Without recourse** to basic structure of Marx's value theory

### Section 3: The "Transformation Problem" and Negative Values (Pages 391-392)

**Focus of Controversy** (p. 391):
- "Critical proposition" = most attention devoted
- **Major testing ground**: Transformation of values into prices of production
- Allegedly a "cul-de-sac" for Marxist value theory

**Two Neo-Ricardian Claims** (p. 391):

**Claim 1 - Logical Inconsistency**:
- Marx assumes: profit rate = total surplus-value / total social capital
- BUT: In "price system," profit rate diverges from "value" rate
- This inconsistency allegedly **independent** of transforming input values

**Claim 2 - Redundancy**:
- Irrespective of inconsistency, value system = redundant, superfluous
- Correct price system obtainable through Sraffian system:
  - Based on physical quantities of inputs
  - Exogenous determination of one distribution variable:
    - Either wage rate (usual) OR
    - Rate of profit (Sraffa's preference, via money market interest rate)

**Neo-Ricardian "Solution"** (pp. 391-392):

**Two-Step Process**:

**Step 1**: Sraffa's standard commodity = solution to Ricardo's invariable measure problem

**Step 2**: Marx's transformation problem = modified version of Ricardo's search
- Therefore: Sraffa's framework = also "solution" to Marx's transformation problem

**Marxist Response** (p. 392):

**Response to Inconsistency Claim**:
- Allegation does NOT hold
- Marxists diverged among themselves on transformation
- **BUT all concurred**: Marx's algorithm NOT internally inconsistent
- Divergence of profit rate NOT independent of input transformation assumption
- **Key point**: Marx's idea (profit rate = surplus-value/capital) = **logical consequence** of NOT transforming input values
- **Corroboration**: If input prices NOT transformed → rate of profit in price system does NOT diverge from value rate

**Response to Superfluity Claim**:
- Simple misunderstanding of Marxist value theory's theoretical status
- Theory fulfills **many different functions** in capitalism analysis
- Would NOT be superfluous even if redundant for prices/profit rate determination
- References: Freeman & Carchedi (1996), Mandel & Freeman (1985), Steedman et al. (1981)

**Response to "Sraffa as Solution"** (p. 392):

**Three Misconceptions**:

**Misconception 1 (Relatively neglected in literature)**:
- Sraffa's standard commodity **NOT relevant** to Ricardo's actual problem
- **Ricardo's problem**: Distinguish impact on values of:
  - Change in production conditions ("absolute value")
  - Change in distribution ("exchangeable value")
- **Sraffa's approach**: Assumed first impact away (holds production constant)
- **Therefore**: Standard commodity does NOT (in general case) provide clue whether value/price change derives from:
  - Production conditions change, OR
  - Distribution change
