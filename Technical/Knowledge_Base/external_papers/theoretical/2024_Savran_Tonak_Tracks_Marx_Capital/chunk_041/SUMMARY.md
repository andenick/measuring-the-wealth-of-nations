## Key Themes and Topics

### A. Neo-Ricardianism: Origins and Development

**Piero Sraffa's Contribution:**
- Italian economist, friend of Antonio Gramsci
- Published *Production of Commodities by Means of Commodities* (1960)
- Developed theoretical framework to resuscitate Ricardian economics
- Created concept of "Standard Commodity" as invariable measure of value

**Historical Context:**
- School highly influential among left intelligentsia in 1970s-1980s
- Controversy subsided in early 1990s
- International debate involving UK, USA, Italy, India, France

### B. The Cambridge Controversy

**Neo-Ricardian Critique of Neoclassicism:**
1. **Capital Measurement Problem:**
   - Impossibility of measuring capital value independent of rate of profit
   - Demolishes neoclassical "production function" concept
   - Undermines "marginal productivity" theory of distribution

2. **Reswitching Phenomenon:**
   - More capital-intensive techniques can become profitable again at higher profit rates
   - Contradicts neoclassical inverse relationship between capital intensity and profit rate
   - Severs link between marginal productivity and profit rate

3. **Impact:**
   - Powerful critique, even Paul Samuelson acknowledged: "We are all Sraffians now"
   - Despite critique, neoclassical economics maintained dominance

### C. Neo-Ricardian Critique of Marxism

**Two Stages:**

1. **First Stage (1970s):**
   - Led by Maurice Dobb and Ronald Meek
   - Claimed Marxist theory had deficiencies but could be salvaged through Sraffian insights

2. **Second Stage (Post-1977):**
   - Watershed: Ian Steedman's *Marx After Sraffa* (1977)
   - All-out assault: claimed Marxist economics must be discarded and replaced
   - Key figures: Ian Steedman, John Eatwell, Geoff Hodgson, Alessandro Roncaglia

**Three Major Neo-Ricardian Propositions:**

1. **Critical Proposition:**
   - Marx's labour theory of value inappropriate for analyzing capitalism
   - Claims: (a) logically inconsistent, (b) redundant/superfluous

2. **Affirmative Proposition:**
   - Fundamental questions can be treated satisfactorily using Sraffa's framework
   - Logically consistent alternative to Marx

3. **Theoretical Proposition:**
   - Materialist theory of capitalism can be built on Sraffian theory
   - Independent of Marx's value theory structure

### D. The Transformation Problem Debate

**Neo-Ricardian Charges:**
1. **Logical Inconsistency:**
   - Marx assumes profit rate = surplus-value/capital
   - But in "price system," profit rate diverges from "value" rate
   - Claim: inconsistency independent of input transformation

2. **Redundancy:**
   - Value system superfluous
   - Prices can be obtained from Sraffian system using physical quantities
   - Exogenous determination of wage or profit rate sufficient

**Neo-Ricardian "Solution":**
- Sraffa's standard commodity solves Ricardo's invariable measure problem
- Therefore, also solves Marx's transformation problem
- Two-step argument linking Ricardo's problem to Marx's

**Marxist Response:**

1. **On Inconsistency:**
   - Marx's algorithm NOT internally inconsistent
   - Divergence of profit rates depends on input transformation assumption
   - Marx's equality logical consequence of NOT transforming inputs
   - If inputs not transformed, profit rates don't diverge

2. **On Redundancy:**
   - Misunderstanding of Marxist value theory's theoretical status
   - Theory fulfills many functions beyond price determination
   - Not superfluous even if redundant for prices/profit rate
   - References: Freeman & Carchedi (1996), Mandel & Freeman (1985), Steedman et al. (1981)

3. **On Sraffa's "Solution":**
   - Suffers from three misconceptions
   - **First misconception** (detailed in text):
     - Standard commodity NOT relevant to Ricardo's actual problem
     - Ricardo sought to distinguish production conditions impact ("absolute value") from distribution impact ("exchangeable value")
     - Sraffa assumed away production changes (static framework)
     - Standard commodity doesn't address general case of value changes

## Key Concepts Introduced

1. **Neo-Ricardianism:** School reviving Ricardian economics through Sraffa's framework
2. **Standard Commodity:** Sraffa's "average" mix of commodities invariant to distribution changes
3. **Production Function:** Neoclassical concept demolished by neo-Ricardian critique
4. **Reswitching:** Phenomenon where technique profitability reverses with profit rate changes
5. **Cambridge Controversy:** Debate between Cambridge UK (Sraffians) and Cambridge MA (neoclassicists)
6. **Transformation Problem:** Conversion of values to prices of production
7. **Invariable Measure of Value:** Ricardo's sought solution to separating value determinants
