## Chapter 7: Marx's Capital in Turkey (Conclusion)

### Marxist Schools of Economic Thought in Turkey

1. **Neo-Ricardians**
   - Main spokesperson: Sencer Divitçioğlu
   - Believed "transformation problem" was the Achilles' heel of Marxist political economy
   - Abandoned Marxism due to perceived failure to solve this problem

2. **Keynesian-Developmentalists**
   - Proponents: Yılmaz Akyüz, Erinç Yeldan, Şevket Pamuk, Çağlar Keyder, Yalçın Küçük (partially)
   - View Marx's political economy as linear development of classical political economy
   - Focus: Distribution of income and economic development as ultimate questions

3. **Value-Theoretical Approach**
   - Proponents: Sungur Savran, Nail Satlıgan, E. Ahmet Tonak, fourth generation scholars
   - Regard Marx's critique as supersession of limitations of classical economy
   - Focus: Mode of production as central, rather than derivative distribution of income
   - Position: Korkut Boratav between categories 2 and 3

### Generations of Turkish Marxist Economists

**Fourth Generation:**
- Came of age during 21st century
- Work on: dialectic, empirical measurement of value categories
- Examples:
  - Benan Eres (differential profitability, Turkish manufacturing)
  - Yiğit Karahanoğulları (Monetary Equivalent of Labour Times - MELTs)

## Chapter 8: Profit on Alienation (Introduction)

### Core Concepts

1. **Profit on Alienation (POA)**
   - Important but generally neglected concept in Marxian political economy
   - Secondary source of profit besides surplus-value
   - Attributed significance by: Anwar Shaikh (1984, 1986, 2016), Costas Lapavitsas (2014)
   - Basis for explaining inequality between total surplus-value and total profit in transformation problem

2. **Surplus-Value**
   - Primary source of profit in capitalist economies
   - Created only through activities of productive capital
   - Not fully retained in productive sectors - flows to unproductive sectors

### Productive vs. Unproductive Labour

3. **Productive Labour**
   - Labour employed in production activities (e.g., industrial)
   - Creates surplus-value
   - "Capitalist production labor which is surplus-value-producing labor, productive labor for capital"

4. **Unproductive Labour**
   - Labour employed in nonproduction activities (e.g., trade, finance)
   - Does not create surplus-value but is still exploited
   - Funded by flows from surplus-value created in productive sectors
   - Can be productive of value or direct-use-value, or forms of social consumption

### Forms of Profit

5. **Industrial Profit**
   - Profit of productive capitalists
   - Subdivides into:
     - **Profit of Enterprise:** Active entrepreneurial profit
     - **Interest:** Passive profit to money-lenders

6. **Nonindustrial Profit**
   - Profit of unproductive capitalists
   - Two main sectors:
     - **Trade:** Profit of enterprise + Interest
     - **Finance:** Profit of enterprise + Interest

7. **Ground Rent**
   - Profit from natural resources
   - Flows to landowners/resource owners

### Theoretical Framework

8. **Exchange of Equivalents**
   - Foundation of Marx's theory of profit
   - Capital pays worker wage equal to value of labour power
   - Appropriates surplus-value produced by worker

9. **Labour Power**
   - Peculiar commodity with unique capacity
   - Produces more value than its own value
   - Use-value is its productive consumption

10. **Transformation Problem**
    - POA helps explain inequality between total surplus-value and total profit
    - Historical debate point for neo-Ricardians

### Exploitation Concepts

11. **Exploitation**
    - Based on appropriation of surplus labour
    - Not exclusive to surplus-value in productive sectors
    - Unproductive workers also exploited through surplus labour appropriation
    - Rate of exploitation measurable for both productive and unproductive workers

12. **Surplus Labour**
    - Labour performed beyond what's needed to reproduce labour power
    - Appropriated by capital in productive sectors as surplus-value
    - Appropriated in unproductive sectors without creating surplus-value

### Merchant Capital and Unequal Exchange

13. **"Law of Commerce"** (Marx quote via Foster & Clark)
    - "To buy cheap so as to sell dearer"
    - "Hence not the exchange of equivalents"
    - When merchant capital dominates: "profit upon expropriation" and "fraud"
    - Constitutes "system of plunder"

### Sectoral Analysis

14. **Production vs. Nonproduction Activities**
    - Key distinction for understanding capitalism
    - Marx's criteria applicable to modern "digital sector"
    - Example: Facebook analysis (Tonak, 2020)

15. **Capitalist vs. Noncapitalist Activities**
    - Within both production and nonproduction labor
    - Only capitalist production labor creates surplus-value
    - POA more significant in developing countries with substantial noncapitalist production spheres

### Methodological Concepts

16. **Operationalization of POA**
    - Chapter focuses on identifying profit types characterizable as POA
    - Distinguished from profit flows deriving from surplus-value
    - Applied to specific sectors: real estate and finance

17. **Empirical Application Areas**
    - **Real estate:** Privatization of public land
    - **Finance:** Bank lending to households
    - Case study: Turkey 2010-2019

### Abstract vs. Concrete Analysis

18. **Levels of Abstraction** (Marx's method)
    - Most abstract: Production of surplus-value assuming exchange of equivalents
    - Less abstract: Concrete manifestations (industrial profit, ground rent, interest, profit of enterprise)
    - Progressive concretization through volumes of *Capital*

### Turkish Context Terms

19. **Turkish Publications and Institutions**
    - *Kapital'in İzinde* [In the Tracks of Capital] (Satlıgan et al., 2012)
    - *Türkiye İktisat Tarihi* [Economic History of Turkey]
    - *Türkiye Üzerine Tezler* [Theses on Turkey]

20. **Sraffa Debate**
    - Major controversy in 1970s Turkey
    - Neo-Ricardian critique of Marxist value theory
    - Defended by value-theoretical school

## Key Theoretical Relationships

1. **Surplus-Value → Industrial Profit** (retained in productive sectors)
2. **Surplus-Value → Nonindustrial Profit** (flows to unproductive sectors)
3. **Surplus-Value → Ground Rent** (flows to resource owners)
4. **All Profits → Interest** (flows to lenders from all sectors)
5. **POA + Surplus-Value = Total Profit** (two sources of profit)
