# Figure 1: Flows of Surplus-Value

**Location:** Page 190 (Book page 166), Chapter 8

**Type:** Hierarchical flowchart/diagram

**Caption:** Fig. 1 Flows of surplus-value

## Full Description

This is a hierarchical flowchart illustrating how surplus-value flows through different sectors and categories within a capitalist economy. The diagram shows the distribution of profit arising from the exploitation of productive labor.

### Structure:

**Top Level (Main Source):**
- "Profit as a Flow of Surplus Value"
- Subtitle: "Profit upon Exploitation"

**Second Level (Three Main Branches):**

1. **Industrial Profit of Productive Capitalists** (Left branch)
   - Subdivides into:
     - Profit of Enterprise
     - Interest

2. **Non-Industrial Profit of Unproductive Capitalists** (Center branch)
   - Subdivides into two sectors:

     a. **Trade**
        - Profit of Enterprise
        - Interest

     b. **Finance**
        - Profit of Enterprise
        - Interest

3. **Ground Rent of Natural Resources** (Right branch)
   - No further subdivisions shown

**Bottom Level:**
- **Lenders** (receives flows from both Trade and Finance branches)

### Visual Characteristics:
- Connected by lines/arrows showing flow relationships
- Hierarchical tree structure
- Multiple levels of branching
- Clear categorization of profit types

### Theoretical Significance:

The diagram illustrates Marx's analysis of how surplus-value, created in productive sectors, flows through the capitalist economy and takes various forms:

1. **Industrial Profit:** Retained by productive capitalists, split between active entrepreneurial profit and passive interest to money-lenders

2. **Non-Industrial Profit:** Flows to unproductive sectors (trade and finance) where it covers their costs and provides profit, also split between profit of enterprise and interest

3. **Ground Rent:** Flows to owners of natural resources

4. **Lenders:** Receive interest payments from both productive and unproductive sectors

The diagram supports the chapter's discussion of how surplus-value is "only created through the activities of productive capital, but it is not fully retained in those sectors" and instead "partially flows out of those sectors to other, unproductive sectors."

## Context in Chapter

This figure appears in Section 1 "Theoretical Background: Surplus-Value and POA" and serves to illustrate the various monetary expressions of surplus-value mentioned in the text, which precede the introduction of the concept of Profit on Alienation (POA) as a distinct, secondary source of profit.
