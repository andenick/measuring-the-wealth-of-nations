# Key Concepts and Terms - Pages 241-250

## Primary Concepts

### Net Social Wage (NSW)
- **Definition**: The difference between public expenditures benefiting the working population and taxes paid by labour
- **Calculation**: NSW = Labour Benefits - Labour Taxes
- **Turkey 1980-2019**: Predominantly negative (average: -1.13% of GDP)
- **Implication**: When negative, indicates state redistribution favors capital over labour

### Labour Benefit Ratio
- Share of public expenditures (cash and in-kind) benefiting working population
- **Turkey average 1980-2019**: 9.95% of GDP
- **As % of labour income**: Peaked at 60.44% in 2009, declined to 46.91% by 2019

### Labour Tax Ratio
- Share of taxes collected from wage and salary earners
- **Turkey average 1980-2019**: 11.08% of GDP
- **As % of labour income**: Rose to 56.82% in 2011, ranged 52.47-55.96% in 2012-2019
- **Trend**: Increased from 35% of total taxes (1980) to 55% (2019)

### Rate of Surplus-Value
- **Standard formula**: s/v (surplus-value / variable capital)
- **Post-NSW formula**: s/(v + NSW)
- **Effect of negative NSW**: Increases the rate of surplus-value, disadvantaging workers
- **Turkey finding**: State intervention favored capitalists and state over productive workers

## Economic Policy Concepts

### Pseudo-Welfare State
- **Characteristics**:
  - Limited welfare provisions
  - Arbitrary and non-universal social protection
  - Restrictive regulations on employee rights
  - State expenditure favors capital over labour
- **Turkey context**: Emerged post-1980 under neoliberal restructuring

### Austerity Policies
- **Pattern during crises** (1991, 1994, 1999, 2001):
  - Cuts to public spending for working population
  - Sustained tax collection from labour
  - Result: Working class "foots the bill"
- **Exception**: 2008-2009 expansionary fiscal policy increased NSW

### Automatic Stabilizers
- **Expected function**: Unemployment benefits, social assistance during downturns
- **Turkey observation**: Opposite pattern - tax burden on labour increases even during crises with rising unemployment

### Fiscal Policy Class Bias
- **Revenue allocation**: ~30% to working people, 70% to capitalists/state absorption
- **Expenditure shift**: Labour share rose from 23% (1980) to 44% (2019)
- **Tax shift**: Labour share rose from 35% (1980) to 55% (2019)
- **Net effect**: Negative NSW throughout period

## Taxation Concepts

### Progressive Income Taxation
- **Post-1980 trend**: Reformed to reduce burden on high-income groups
- **Result**: Over-taxation of labour income

### Tax Amnesty
- **Frequency**: 31 laws enacted between 1980-2019
- **Effect**: Substantially reduced tax burden of businesses

### Direct vs. Indirect Taxes
- **Post-1980 shift**: Emphasis moved from direct to indirect taxes
- **Value-Added Tax (VAT)**: Introduced 1985
- **Special Consumption Taxes**: Added to system
- **Impact on labour**:
  1. Working class spends wages entirely on necessities (high VAT rates)
  2. Low saving propensity increases tax burden
  3. Financialization led to borrowing, increasing consumption and indirect tax payments

## Public Expenditure Issues

### Public-Private Partnership (PPP) Guarantees
- **Mechanism**: State guarantees (rental revenue, patient numbers, vehicle numbers, passengers) to private operators of hospitals, highways, bridges, tunnels, airports
- **Accounting problem**: Classified as health/transportation services but don't benefit labour
- **Effect**: Artificially inflates labour benefit measurements
- **2019 example**: Deducting PPP guarantees decreased labour benefits by 0.66%, increasing negative NSW by 66.26% (from -6.4 to -10.6 million TL)

### Population Growth Effect
- **Issue**: Population growth exceeded public expenditure increases
- **Result**: Per capita public expenditure decreased
- **Implication**: Rise in labour-benefiting expenditures is "exaggerated"

## Labour Organization Concepts

### Unionization Constraints
- **1980 military coup**: Established legal/institutional barriers
- **Post-2002 AKP regime**: "Despotic" orientation
- **Effects**:
  - Workers organizing are laid off
  - Law enforcement and courts side with employers
  - Major strikes banned for nearly a decade

### Class Struggle Impediments
- **Legal**: Regulations impede organization
- **Practical**: Impossible to unionize new private sector workplaces
- **Result**: Workers unable to fight for wage increases or improved conditions

## Crisis Episodes Referenced

### 1991 Gulf Crisis
Net social wage declined into negative zone

### 1994 Economic Crisis
Net social wage declined further negative

### 1999 Earthquake Crisis
Net social wage declined into negative zone

### 2001 Economic Crisis
Net social wage declined into negative zone

### 2008-2009 Financial Crisis
**Exception**: NSW increased into positive zone due to:
- Expansionary fiscal policy
- Unemployment benefit payments

### 2017-2019 Slowdown
- Public expenditures for labour declined
- Tax revenues also declined (unemployment, revenue decrease)
- NSW varied between -0.05 and -0.51
- "Scissors" between benefits and taxes tended to close

## Methodological Concepts

### Data Sources (Post-2003 Law 5018)
- **1980-2003**: Consolidated Budget, Local Governments, Social Security Institutions, Unemployment Insurance Fund
- **2004-2019**: General Government Budget (Central + Local + Social Security + Employment Agency)

### Conservative Estimates
- PPP accounting exaggerates labour benefits
- Exchange rate increases exaggerate amounts paid
- Actual negative NSW likely larger than measured

## Political-Economic Context

### Neoliberal Model (Post-1980)
- Export-oriented industrialization
- Workers' movement suppression
- Collective consumption expenditure reduction
- Tax burden transfer from capital to wage earners
- Limited, arbitrary social protection

### AKP (Justice and Development Party)
- **Power period**: November 3, 2002 onward
- **Claim**: Pro-poor, pro-labour, Islamic values
- **Finding**: Failed to raise working class living standards through welfare mechanisms
- **Conclusion**: Myth of pro-labour stance demolished by NSW evidence

## Theoretical Extensions

### Net Social Profit
- **Need**: Complement to net social wage
- **Purpose**: Full dimensions of state redistribution to capital

### State Economic Functions
- Production activities via state enterprises
- Beyond redistribution
- Based on general circuits of capital

## Social Insurance Reforms

### Retirement Restrictions
- **1992**: Increased days/age requirements
- **1999**: Further restrictions
- **2008**: Additional restrictions
- **Overall effect**: More restrictive employee rights
