## Figure 8: Benefit ratio, tax ratio and net social wage ratio (as percentage of labour income), 1980-2019

**Type**: Line graph with three series and dual y-axes

**Description**: This figure displays three trend lines for the same period:
- **Blue line**: Benefit Ratio (% of Labor Income) - left axis (0.00 to 70.00)
- **Orange line**: Tax Ratio (% of Labor Income) - left axis (0.00 to 70.00)
- **Gray line**: Net Social Wage Ratio (% of Labor Income) - right axis (-14.00 to 10.00)

The graph shows significant volatility, particularly in the net social wage ratio. The benefit ratio reached its peak of 60.44% in 2009, while the tax ratio peaked at 56.82% in 2011. Both ratios show an increasing trend from 1980s to early 2010s, followed by declining trends.

**Data observations from text**:
- Tax ratio rose from 2009 on, reaching 56.82% in 2011
- Tax ratio declined to vary between 55.96% and 52.47% between 2012-2019
- Benefit ratio reached highest level of 60.44% in 2009
- Benefit ratio fell from 55.17% to 46.91% between 2010-2019
- Net contribution to labour income varied between 0.01% and 7.36%
- Income-reducing effect varied between 0.18% and 12.48%

## Figure 9: The relationship between the net social wage and the growth rate in Turkey, 1980-2019

**Type**: Line graph with two series and dual y-axes

**Description**: This figure shows the relationship between:
- **Blue line**: Net Social Wage Ratio (left axis, -4.00 to 3.00)
- **Orange line**: Growth Rate (right axis, -8.00 to 12.00)

The graph illustrates how the net social wage responds to economic growth patterns and crisis periods:
- During crisis periods (1991 Gulf crisis, 1994 economic crisis, 1999 earthquake crisis, 2001 economic crisis), the net social wage declines further into negative territory
- In 2008-2009, while growth declined, net social wage increased in the positive zone due to expansionary fiscal policy and unemployment benefit payments
- In 2017-2019 slowdown, net social wage varied between -0.05 and -0.51

**Note**: The net social wage ratio was calculated as NSW/GDP

**Source**: Rates of growth from the Turkish Statistical Agency (2014, 638; 2020c) data

## Figure 10: The net social wage and the rate of unemployment, 2002-2019

**Type**: Line graph with two series and dual y-axes

**Description**: This figure examines the relationship between:
- **Blue line**: Net Social Wage Ratio (as % GDP) - left axis (-3.00 to 3.00)
- **Orange line**: Unemployment Rate - right axis (0.0000 to 16.0000)

The graph shows an observable connection between increase in net social wage and rising unemployment from 2002 onwards. However, contrary to advanced capitalist countries where automatic stabilizers would typically increase the net social wage during unemployment rises, the Turkish state increased the tax burden on the working class even during economic crises with rising unemployment.

**Source**: Unemployment rates for 2002-2019 from the Turkish Statistical Agency (2020d) data

## Figure 11: The impact of the net social wage on the rate of surplus-value in Turkey, 1988-2006

**Type**: Line graph with two series

**Description**: This figure compares:
- **Blue line**: Rate of Surplus Value (s/v) - y-axis range (0.00 to 4.00)
- **Red line**: Corrected Rate of Surplus Value (s/v+N) - y-axis range (0.00 to 4.00)

The graph demonstrates the effect of net social wage on the rate of surplus-value over the period 1988-2006. The two lines track closely together, with the corrected rate (accounting for net social wage) being slightly higher than the standard rate for most years, reflecting the negative net social wage that increases the effective rate of surplus-value to the disadvantage of the working class.

**Key observation**: Since net social wage was predominantly negative (except for a small positive value in 1997), the corrected rate of surplus-value is generally higher, showing that state intervention favored capitalists and the state itself over productive workers.

**Formula notes**:
- Rate of surplus-value = surplus-value (s) / variable capital (v)
- Post-NSW rate of surplus-value = surplus-value (s) / [variable capital (v) + net social wage (NSW)]

**Source**: The data for variable capital (v) and surplus-value (s) have been taken from Karahanoğulları (2009, 174, Table 11)
