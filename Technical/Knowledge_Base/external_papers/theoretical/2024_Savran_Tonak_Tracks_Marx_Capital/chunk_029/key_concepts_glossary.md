## Chapter 12: Digital Sector and Surplus-Value

### Core Concepts

**Petty Commodity Producer**
- The Facebook user who produces content without receiving wages
- Contributes labor that enriches the platform
- Payment received "in kind" through access to social interaction environment

**Targetable Audience**
- Facebook's actual end product sold to advertisers
- People in social interaction who produce a marketable environment
- Result of combining platform infrastructure with user-generated content

**Modified Commodity**
- User content transforms the initial product (bare social platform) into enhanced product
- Analogized to "diamond on gold ring" - the content defines the platform's value
- Serves as means of production for Stage 2 of Facebook's production process

**User Content as Means of Production**
- User-generated content becomes constant capital for Facebook
- Raw material input for producing targetable audiences
- Has tangible existence in electromagnetic form

**Immaterial Labour**
- Labor producing digital, non-physical outputs
- Chapter argues this is actually material (electromagnetic existence)
- Falls within labor theory of value, not outside it

**Productive vs. Unproductive Labour (in digital context)**
- Productive: Engineers, developers creating platform and algorithms
- Unproductive: Management, marketing, administrative functions
- Surplus-value from productive labor funds both profits and unproductive wages

### Marxist Categories Applied to Digital Economy

**Constant Capital (C)**
- Buildings, servers, networking equipment
- Computing infrastructure
- Platform software as fixed asset

**Variable Capital (V)**
- Wages of engineers, technicians, janitors, repairmen
- Labor power of employees creating platform

**Surplus-Value (S)**
- Generated by productive workers
- Source of profits and unproductive worker wages
- Realized through advertising sales

## Chapter 13: Lean Production and Flexibility

### Contemporary Production Terminology

**Post-Fordism**
- Claimed break from mass production/standardization model
- Emphasis on flexibility and customization
- Used to argue for fundamental transformation of capitalism

**Flexible Specialisation**
- Production adapted to market variations
- Claimed to reduce worker alienation
- Presented as benefiting all classes

**Lean Production**
- Toyota Production System adapted globally
- Minimizing waste, maximizing efficiency
- Central to chapter's critical analysis

**Toyotism**
- Japanese management system
- Just-in-time production, quality circles
- Alternative name for lean production methods

**Total Quality Control/Management (TQC/TQM)**
- Quality management involving all workers
- Used to create appearance of worker empowerment
- Actually intensifies capitalist control

**Team Work**
- Collaborative work arrangements
- Claimed to eliminate hierarchy
- Chapter argues it's new form of domination

**Human Resources Management**
- Rebranding of personnel management
- Emphasis on "human capital"
- Ideological cover for intensified exploitation

**Flat Organisation**
- Reduced hierarchical layers
- Claimed to empower workers
- Actually fragments collective worker power

**Non-Standard Work Arrangements**
- On-call work, temporary contracts, gig economy
- Flexibility serving capital, not labor
- Weakens worker solidarity and power

### Fundamental Marxist Categories

**Labour Process**
- Bringing together human labor with means of production
- Common to all modes of production
- Form changes based on productive forces and class relations
- In capitalism: subordinated to valorisation process

**Valorisation Process (Self-Expansion of Capital)**
- Process of capital expansion through surplus-value extraction
- Peculiar to capitalist mode of production
- Dialectically united with labor process in production process of capital

**Production Process of Capital**
- Dialectical unity of valorisation and labor process
- Heart of capitalist mode of production
- Site of class struggle over control and exploitation

**Formal Subsumption of Labour Under Capital**
- Early stage of capitalist development
- Capital takes over existing labor process without fundamental change
- Domination limited to social relations (wage labor vs. capital)
- Capital shapes process "from outside"
- Form: social relations changed, but not labor process itself

**Real Subsumption of Labour Under Capital**
- Advanced stage of capitalist development
- Capital reshapes labor process according to its own needs
- Goes beyond social relations to subordinate labor "in its relation to nature"
- Capital controls process "from within"
- Real: both social relations AND labor process transformed
- Capitalism "standing on its own feet"

**Specifically Capitalist Mode of Production**
- Emerges with real subsumption
- Labor process branded with logic of valorisation
- Production process directly controlled by capital
- Productive forces developed according to capital's needs

### Historical Context Terms

**Long Post-War Boom**
- Economic expansion from post-WWII to mid-1970s
- Era of relative class compromise (Fordism)
- Ended with crisis of profitability

**Long-Term Depressive Crisis (mid-1970s onward)**
- Structural crisis beginning circa 1973-1975
- Falling rate of profit
- Context for capital's offensive and new management techniques

**Capital's Grand Offensive (1980s onward)**
- Systematic attack on working class power
- Neoliberalism, deregulation, union-busting
- New management techniques part of this offensive
- Lean production, flexibility as weapons in class struggle

### Class Struggle Concepts

**Deskilling**
- Reduction of workers' skill requirements
- Part of capital's control strategy
- Claimed to be reversed by new methods (chapter will dispute this)

**Detail Work**
- Repetitive, fragmented tasks (e.g., "turning same screw")
- Associated with Fordism/Taylorism
- Claimed to be eliminated by new methods

**Division of Mental and Manual Labour**
- Separation of conception from execution
- Key to capitalist control of labor process
- Claimed to be overcome by team work, etc.

**Workplace Hierarchy**
- Management layers controlling workers
- Claimed to be flattened by new organization
- Chapter argues hierarchy remains, takes new forms

**Worker Alienation**
- Estrangement from product, process, species-being
- Fundamental to capitalism
- Cannot be eliminated without ending capitalism

**Power of Working Class vis-à-vis Capital**
- Collective strength to resist exploitation
- Concern that new methods have "shaken it to its very roots"
- Chapter aims to show worries are overstated

**Classical Methods of Class Struggle**
- Strikes, unions, collective bargaining
- Some believe these rendered obsolete
- Chapter argues they remain relevant

## Theoretical/Methodological Terms

**Dialectical Unity**
- Two processes that are distinct yet inseparable
- E.g., labor process and valorisation process
- Contradiction and mutual determination

**Class Character**
- Whose interests a practice/relation serves
- Chapter aims to reveal class character of "new" methods
- Hidden by bourgeois ideology

**Bourgeois Theories/Thinking**
- Intellectual production serving capitalist class
- Presents class interest as universal interest
- Claims harmony replacing conflict

**Marxist Method/Theory**
- Historical materialism applied to capitalism
- Categories: surplus-value, productive/unproductive labor, etc.
- Chapter argues these remain adequate for analysis

**Ideological Laden Concepts**
- Terms that carry political assumptions
- Often "defy definition for their protagonists"
- Require critical examination

## Problems Identified by Chapter

### For Workers and Left

1. **Lack of Theoretical Compass:** Cannot situate dynamics of new trends
2. **Appearance of Radical Change:** Hard to explain with existing frameworks
3. **Impression of Weakened Power:** Belief that classical struggle methods obsolete
4. **Despair and Demoralisation:** Loss of confidence in collective action
5. **Confusion:** "Like finding way in city where street signs removed"

### Bourgeois Claims (to be refuted)

1. **Fundamental Transformation:** Capitalism essentially changed
2. **Class Harmony:** New order benefits all classes
3. **Obsolete Categories:** Marxist concepts no longer applicable
4. **End of Alienation:** New methods overcome basic problems
5. **Elimination of Contradictions:** Class conflict being transcended

## Analytical Framework Summary

The chunk establishes a theoretical framework arguing that:

1. **Continuity not rupture:** New workplace forms represent deepening, not transcendence of capitalist relations
2. **Real subsumption advancing:** Lean production = higher stage of capital's control over labor process
3. **Class offensive:** New methods are weapons in capital's war on labor since 1980s
4. **Marxist categories sufficient:** No need for new theory; Marx's concepts adequately explain contemporary developments
5. **Ideology vs. reality:** Bourgeois claims of harmony mask intensified exploitation and class struggle

The framework promises to show:
- How new forms connect systematically
- Their class character
- Why workers' extreme worries are unfounded while also showing real threats
- That changes are part of capitalist offensive, not mutual benefit
