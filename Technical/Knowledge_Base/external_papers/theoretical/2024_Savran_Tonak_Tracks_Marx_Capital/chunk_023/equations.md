## Circuit of Capital Equations

### Equation 1: Surplus-Value Definition (Page 204)
**Location**: Page 204, Figure 2 discussion

```latex
s = M' - (M_P + M_S)
```

Where:
- $s$ = Surplus-value (money form)
- $M'$ = Money value of newly produced commodities
- $M_P$ = Money capital invested in private sector
- $M_S$ = Money capital invested in state sector

**Description**: Surplus-value is obtained by subtracting the sum of private and state sector investments from total sales revenue.

---

### Equation 2: Gross Product (Page 204)
**Location**: Page 204, Figure 2 discussion

```latex
\text{Gross Product} = M_P + M_S
```

**Description**: The sum of private and state sector money capital investments corresponds to the gross product of the "mixed economy."

---

### Equation 3: Total Money Value (Page 204)
**Location**: Page 204, Figure 2 discussion

```latex
M' = M'_P + M'_S
```

Where:
- $M'_P$ = Money value from private sector production
- $M'_S$ = Money value from state sector production

---

### Equation 4: Circuit of Capital - General Form (Implicit on Page 203-204)

```latex
M - C \begin{cases} LP \\ MP \end{cases} \ldots P \ldots C' - M'
```

Where:
- $M$ = Money capital
- $C$ = Commodities
- $LP$ = Labour Power
- $MP$ = Means of Production
- $P$ = Production Process
- $C'$ = New commodities (commodity capital)
- $M'$ = Money value of new commodities

---

## Surplus-Value Distribution Equations

### Equation 5: Division of Surplus-Value (Page 205)
**Location**: Page 205, discussion of productive and unproductive activities

```latex
s = s_{up} + s_p
```

Alternatively:
```latex
s_p = s - s_{up}
```

Where:
- $s$ = Total surplus-value
- $s_{up}$ = Surplus-value transferred to unproductive sectors
- $s_p$ = Surplus-value remaining in productive sectors

**Description**: Total surplus-value is divided between productive and unproductive sectors.

## Net Social Wage Equations

### Equation 10: Net Social Wage - Unproductive Workers (Page 206)
**Location**: Page 206, Figure 6

```latex
NSW_{upw} = B_{upw} - T_{up}
```

Where:
- $NSW_{upw}$ = Net Social Wage for unproductive workers
- $B_{upw}$ = Benefits/public expenditures to unproductive workers
- $T_{up}$ = Taxes paid by unproductive workers

---

### Equation 11: Net Social Wage - Productive Workers (Page 206-207)
**Location**: Page 206-207, Figure 6

```latex
NSW_v = B_v - T_v
```

Where:
- $NSW_v$ = Net Social Wage for productive workers
- $B_v$ = Benefits/public expenditures to productive workers
- $T_v$ = Taxes paid by productive workers

---

### Equation 12: General Net Social Wage Formula (Page 207)
**Location**: Page 207, text discussion

```latex
NSW = B - T
```

Where:
- $NSW$ = Net Social Wage
- $B$ = Public expenditures/transfers benefiting working population
- $T$ = Taxes paid by working class

**Description**: The Net Social Wage is calculated by taking into consideration both expenditures/transfers benefiting the working population and taxes paid by this same class.

## Rate of Surplus-Value Equations

### Equation 13: Nominal Rate of Surplus-Value (Page 207)
**Location**: Page 207, text discussion

```latex
\text{Rate of surplus-value} = \frac{s}{v}
```

Where:
- $s$ = Money form of surplus-value
- $v$ = Money form of variable capital

**Description**: This calculation assumes a high level of abstraction where state intervention in income distribution is absent.

---

### Equation 14: Impact of Net Social Wage on Rate of Surplus-Value (Page 207)
**Location**: Page 207, text discussion

**When NSW < 0:**
```latex
v_{eff} < v \implies s > s_{nominal}
```

Therefore:
```latex
\frac{s_{eff}}{v_{eff}} > \frac{s}{v}
```

**Interpretation**: When the net social wage is negative, effective variable capital is smaller than nominal value, surplus-value is larger than nominal value, and the rate of surplus-value increases.

**When NSW > 0:**
```latex
v_{eff} > v
```

Therefore:
```latex
\frac{s_{eff}}{v_{eff}} < \frac{s}{v}
```

**Interpretation**: When net social wage is positive, the rate of surplus-value decreases.

---

## Notes on Equation Methodology

1. All equations use money forms of Marxist value categories
2. Subscript notation:
   - P = Private sector
   - S = State sector
   - up = Unproductive sector
   - p = Productive sector
   - eff = Effective (post-tax and transfer)
   - π = Profits

3. The prime notation (') indicates new commodities or money value after production process

4. The analysis operates at different levels of abstraction, progressively incorporating state intervention
