# Figure 4: Capitalist Productive and Unproductive Activities

**Location**: Page 205 (book page 205)

**Type**: Circuit diagram showing surplus-value distribution

**Description**:
This figure illustrates the division of total surplus-value between productive and unproductive sectors and the internal structure of unproductive activities.

**Diagram Structure**:

```
                                           ┌─ c_up
                    ┌─→ LP_up              │
s ──→ s_up ──→ c_up ┤           ... up activity ... s_up ──┤─ w_up
                    └─→ MP_up              │
                                           └─ s_upπ

    └─→ Productive Sector (p)
        s - s_up = s_p ────────────────────────────────────→ s_p
```

**Symbol Definitions**:
- s = Total surplus-value produced in the economy
- s_up = Surplus-value transferred to unproductive sectors
- s_p = Surplus-value remaining in productive sectors (s - s_up)
- c_up = Constant capital in unproductive sector
- LP_up = Labour power in unproductive sector
- MP_up = Means of production in unproductive sector
- w_up = Wages in unproductive sector
- s_upπ = Profits in unproductive sector

**Key Relationships**:
- Total surplus-value splits: s = s_up + s_p
- Unproductive sector costs: c_up + w_up (minimized to maximize s_upπ)
- Productive sector retains: s_p = s - s_up

**Caption**: "Capitalist productive and unproductive activities"

**Theoretical Significance**:
Demonstrates Marx's distinction between productive and unproductive activities. The existence and relative increase of unproductive activities (trade, finance) is conditional on the transfer of part of surplus-value produced in productive sectors. The goal of unproductive capitalist activities is to minimize costs (c_up + w_up) to maximize profits (s_upπ).
