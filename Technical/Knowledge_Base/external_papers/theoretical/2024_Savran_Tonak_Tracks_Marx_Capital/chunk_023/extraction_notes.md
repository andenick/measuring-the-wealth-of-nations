### Theoretical Framework

The chapter presents a sophisticated theoretical framework for understanding the Net Social Wage (NSW) concept:

1. **Literature Review**:
   - Traces the rise and decline of "social wage" concept in academic discourse (1975-1985 peak)
   - Reviews Gough (1979), Bowles & Gintis (1982) approaches
   - Critiques existing literature for focusing only on expenditures, not net effects

2. **Conceptual Innovation**:
   - Redefines social wage as NET social wage (NSW = B - T)
   - B = Benefits/expenditures to workers
   - T = Taxes paid by workers
   - Emphasizes necessity of analyzing both taxation AND expenditures together

3. **Circuit of Capital Framework**:
   - Progressively develops circuits of capital incorporating state intervention
   - Distinguishes productive vs. unproductive activities
   - Shows state intervention in both capital income and labour income
   - Links NSW to rate of surplus-value

4. **Key Theoretical Claim**:
   - When NSW < 0: State intervention increases rate of surplus-value (pro-capital)
   - When NSW > 0: State intervention decreases rate of surplus-value (pro-labour)
   - Challenges Bowles & Gintis claim that social wage is inherently pro-labour

### Methodological Approach

**Marxist Labour Theory of Value Framework**:
- Uses Marx's categories (c, v, s)
- Circuit of capital M-C-P-C'-M' analysis
- Productive/unproductive labour distinction
- Rate of surplus-value (s/v) calculations

**Levels of Abstraction**:
1. Pure capitalist economy (private sector only)
2. "Mixed economy" (private + state sectors)
3. Mixed economy + taxation
4. Mixed economy + taxation + productive/unproductive distinction
5. Mixed economy + state intervention in both capital and labour income
