## Chapter 18 Conclusion (Pages 414-420)

### Main Argument

Savran presents a systematic critique of Sraffa's system of prices of production, demonstrating that none of the three alternative methods for fixing the independent variable (wage or rate of profit) can consistently satisfy Sraffa's own requirements.

### Three Alternative Methods Critiqued

1. **Wage as aggregate of commodities**: Incompatible with the standard commodity
2. **Wage as abstract price**: Cannot guarantee subsistence wage for workers; fails to ensure reproduction
3. **Rate of profit from outside the system**: Cannot guarantee consistency with subsistence wage requirements

### Key Theoretical Claims

**Reproduction Requirement**: Sraffa's system must ensure:
- Restoration of original distribution of gross product to branches
- Every commodity used in production must reappear in same quantity
- Subsistence commodities are part of input structure (basic commodities)

**The Contradiction**: When wage is given as abstract price or rate of profit is determined externally:
- No guarantee that workers receive subsistence wage
- Necessaries (basic goods) may not influence rate of profit as Sraffa's theory requires
- System can provide for surplus distribution but NOT for reproduction

**Sraffa's Own Position**:
- Initially used wage as independent variable
- Later switched to rate of profit as independent variable
- Believed both methods were logically possible (just preference for one)
- Savran argues both are logically impossible within Sraffa's framework

### Ultimate Conclusion

"The system of prices of production constructed by Sraffa...faces a double task: it has to make possible both the reproduction of the system of production and the distribution of the surplus according to the rule of equal profitability for different capitals. However, it fails to meet these requirements simultaneously and consistently."

The paper concludes that "logical consistency requires that Sraffa's theory itself be abandoned."

## Chapter 19 Beginning (Pages 421-423)

### Context and Critics

Savran's 1979 paper drew criticism from:
1. **Steedman (1979)**: Claims Savran's argument is formally incorrect at every link
2. **Eatwell (1980)**: Agrees argument is "trivially correct" formally but criticizes failure to situate theory historically; defends Sraffa using Marx
3. **McLachlan et al. (1980)**: Suggests shortcomings can be rectified through reformulation

### Reply Structure

Savran organizes his reply to:
1. Address formal/logical correctness questions
2. Discuss theoretical implications of Sraffa's internal inconsistency
3. Examine Marx's theory of value and surplus-value in this context

### Concept of Theoretical Consistency

**Two Forms of Theoretical Criticism**:

1. **External criticism**: Judges theory by adequacy in representing real world; uses criteria external to theory
2. **Internal criticism**: Examines mutual consistency of component parts; judges theory by its own standards

**Theoretical Consistency Defined**:
- Not merely mathematical or logical consistency
- "The capacity of a theory to offer acceptable solutions to the very tasks it sets itself"
- Requires "harmony between its questions and its answers"

**Savran's Approach**: His 1979 paper used internal criticism - showing Sraffa's system fails to solve problems it formulates for itself.

### Beginning of Detailed Reply

**Section 1: Steedman on Three Alternative Methods**

Steedman's claims:
1. Savran's objections to all three methods are unfounded
2. There exists a fourth alternative that avoids these objections

**Counter-argument on Standard Commodity**:
- Steedman claims Standard Commodity "not essential to any part of Sraffa's argument"
- Savran argues it IS essential for examining how wages, profits, and prices vary together
- Under joint production, inverse wage-profit relationship only holds with standard commodity as measure
- Without standard commodity, same wage variation could appear as rise OR fall depending on measure used

## Key Concepts and Terms

- **Basic commodities**: Commodities that enter into production of all other commodities (including necessaries/subsistence goods)
- **Prices of production**: Sraffa's system for determining prices that ensure both reproduction and equal profitability
- **Standard commodity**: Sraffa's construct for measuring value and ensuring inverse wage-profit relationship
- **Subsistence wage**: Minimum wage necessary for worker reproduction; part of input structure
- **Necessaries**: Commodities required for subsistence of workers
- **Independent variable**: The distributional variable (wage or profit rate) taken as given
- **Reproduction**: Restoration of original distribution of commodities to production process

## Theoretical Significance

1. **Immanent critique**: Demonstrates internal contradictions within Sraffa's own theoretical framework
2. **Methodological contribution**: Distinguishes internal vs. external theoretical criticism
3. **Debate context**: Addresses Sraffian-Marxist controversy over logical consistency
4. **Challenge to Sraffian orthodoxy**: Questions myth of Sraffa's "perfect internal coherence"
