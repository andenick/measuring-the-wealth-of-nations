## Key Concepts and Findings

### Historical Insights

1. **Cyclical Nature of Revolutionary Periods**: Different generations experience opposite trajectories (hope-to-despair vs. despair-to-hope)

2. **Global Synchronization**: Despite national differences, capitalist imperialism creates "similar life trajectories for people of entirely different kinds of countries"

3. **1970s as Peak**: Unprecedented flourishing of Marxist economics with institutional infrastructure (journals, conferences, publishing houses)

4. **1980s Watershed**: Neoliberal counter-revolution + military coups (Turkey, Latin America) + later Soviet collapse created profound crisis for Marxist thought

5. **Institutional Memory**: Detailed documentation of Marxist economics infrastructure provides valuable historical record

### Theoretical Contributions

1. **Measurement of Marxist Categories**: Shaikh & Tonak (1994) development of "reverse transformation" method going from market prices to "direct prices" or exchange-values

2. **State Theory**: Tonak (1984) work on economic functions of capitalist state

3. **Value Theory Defense**: Savran (1977) engagement with Sraffa-based (neo-Ricardian) critique

4. **Internationalism**: Emphasis on global perspective rather than national-specific analysis

### Contemporary Relevance

The authors identify three main challenges to Marxism today:
1. **Left-wing liberalism**: Accommodation with neoliberal policies
2. **Postmodernism**: Emphasis on identity over class, micro-power over state power
3. **Derivative schools**: Various fragmentations of left thought
