## Main Topics

### 1. Value Transfer and Unequal Exchange (Pages 321-322)
This section discusses mechanisms of value transfer from the Global South to the Global North in the context of imperialism:

- **Unequal Exchange Theory:** Reviews Arghiri Emmanuel's theory challenging Sweezy's view on capital immobility, arguing that capital mobility leads to profit rate equalization, resulting in value transfer from South to North due to lower organic composition of capital
- **Forms of Value Transfer:**
  - Portfolio investments and speculative capital gains
  - International debt interest payments
  - Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) profit repatriation
- **Empirical Finding:** Recent work suggests value outflow from South ranges around 10-20% (Ricci, 2019)

### 2. Foreign Direct Investment Patterns (Pages 322-324)
Analysis of recent FDI trends and factors influencing investment decisions:

- **Historical Context:** FDI as leading characteristic of imperialism since Lenin; shift from commodity capital to productive capital
- **2018 Anomaly:** First time FDI to developing economies (54%, including China) exceeded developed economies, but UNCTAD considers this anomalous due to funds repatriations from Europe
- **Challenge to Left Assumptions:** Low wages NOT the primary driver of FDI
- **Key FDI Factors (World Bank 2018 survey of 754 MNC executives):**
  1. Political stability and security (87%)
  2. Legal and regulatory environment (86%)
  3. Large domestic market size (80%)
  4. Macroeconomic stability (78%)
  5. Available talent and skill (73%)
  6. Good infrastructure (71%)
  7. Low tax rates (58%)
  8. **Low labour costs (53%) - ranked LAST**

### 3. US Foreign Profits Analysis (Pages 325-326)
Quantitative assessment of foreign profit significance:

- **Foreign profits as % of total US profits:** Fluctuated 20-50% (1998-2018, excluding 2007-2009 crisis)
- **Profits from South only:** About 50% of foreign profits, thus 10-25% of total US profits
- **Challenge to "Labour Aristocracy" Thesis:** Repatriated profits from South represent only ~6-7% of total US wages (assuming unrealistic full allocation to workers)

### 4. Rate of Surplus-Value Theory (Page 326)
Theoretical foundation for exploitation measurement:

- **Definition:** Ratio of surplus-value (S) to variable capital (V), or surplus labour time to necessary labour time
- **Significance:** Quantitative expression of worker exploitation; basis for income distribution and profit rate
- **Key Insight:** Rate increases even with higher wages due to mechanization and labor intensity increases
- **Political Dimension:** Enables workers to see appropriation of value and make case for alternative production organization

### 5. Empirical Estimation of Surplus-Value Rates (Pages 327-330)
Comparative analysis challenging super-exploitation theory:

**United States:**
- 1948: 170% → 1989: 244% (Shaikh & Tonak, 1994)
- 1980: 200% → 2001: 300% (Mohun, 2005)
- Increase linked to Reagan-era labor repression

**Turkey:**
- 1950: 376% → 1975: 352% (Tonak, 1980)
- 1988: 254% → 2006: 312% (Karahanoğulları, 2009)
- Average 1988-2006: 239%
- FDI-intensive firms: 2-6 times higher profit-wage ratios than average

**Greece:**
- 1970: 150% → 2006: 200% (Tsoulfidis & Tsaliki, 2014)

**Amsden's Manufacturing Comparison (1969-1977):**
- **Unexpected Finding:** South's rates EXCEEDED North's rates
- **South:** South Korea (465%), Panama (402%), Turkey (388%), Bolivia (346%)
- **North:** Germany (259%), USA (249%), Sweden (220%), Canada (202%)
- **Pattern:** Mid-income South countries showed higher rates than low-income South countries

### 6. Super-Exploitation Debate (Page 330)
Critical engagement with contemporary imperialism theory:

- **Historical Origins:** Roy Mauro Marini's original contribution
- **Contemporary Proponents:** Andy Higginbottom (2010), John Smith (2016)
- **Points of Agreement:** 
  - Global value chains are important and recent phenomenon
  - Requires more theoretical and empirical work using labor theory of value
  - Firms in value chains in South impose higher surplus-value rates
- **Critical Perspective:** Super-exploitationist approach belongs to underdevelopment school, focusing on external causes and implying passivity of working classes in both North and South

## Key Arguments

1. **Value transfer exists but should not be interpreted as the CAUSE of uneven development** - capital accumulation has been uneven and integrated from the beginning

2. **Low wages are NOT the primary factor driving FDI** - institutional, political, and market factors dominate investment decisions

3. **Foreign profits' contribution to US capital and workers is often exaggerated** - especially regarding the "labour aristocracy" thesis

4. **Rates of surplus-value in the South do NOT systematically exceed those in the North** - challenges super-exploitation theory's empirical basis

5. **Marx's prediction confirmed:** Rate of surplus-value necessarily increases as capitalism develops (US: nearly twofold in 50 years)
