### Section 1: Forms of Value Transfer (Pages 321-326)

**Theoretical Context** (p. 321):
> "The existence of value transfer should not be interpreted as the cause of the unevenness between the regions of the world."

- Capital accumulation + global expansion = inherently uneven AND integrated
- Value transfer South → North perpetuates (not creates) imperialist relations
- Direction and amount of NET value transfer "rather complicated"

**Form 1: Unequal Exchange** (p. 321):
- **Arghiri Emmanuel's theory**: Most prominent Marxist formulation
- Challenges Sweezy: Profit rate equalization IS possible globally
- Key mechanism:
  - Capital mobile → equalizes profit rates across globe
  - Labor relatively immobile → persistent international wage differences
  - Process → South profit rate LOWER than North
  - Reason: Transfer of profits (surplus-value) due to lower organic composition in South
- Extended by: Iguiñiz (1979), Bettelheim, Amin (1977), Mandel, Shaikh, Satlıgan (2014)
- **Empirical estimate**: Value outflow 10-20% for South (Ricci 2019)

**Form 2: Portfolio Investments & International Indebtedness** (pp. 321-322):
- **Portfolio**: Repatriation of speculative profits
  - Money capital in stocks, bonds, foreign exchange, other financial instruments
- **Debt**: Exorbitant interest payments imposed on South debtor countries

**Form 3: Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)** (pp. 322-326):

**Historical Significance**:
- Since Lenin: FDI = "leading characteristic of modern capitalist imperialism"
- Evolution: Commodity capital primacy → Productive capital (FDI)
  - Commodity capital: Appropriating natural resources/raw materials + selling finished goods
  - Productive capital: Foreign direct investment

**Recent Pattern - 2018 ANOMALY** (UNCTAD 2019):
- **First time ever**: FDI to developing economies exceeded developed (54%, including China)
- **Excluding China**: FDI to developed still larger (44% to developing)
- Reason for anomaly:
  - FDI to developed: Lowest since 2004, declined 27%
  - Europe: Halved to <$200B (negative inflows in large hosts, UK drop)
  - United States: Declined 9% to $252B
  - Developing: Stable, rose 2%

### Section 2: The Rate of Surplus-Value and Imperialism (Pages 326-330)

**Conceptual Foundation** (p. 326):

> "The rate of exploitation is one of the most important concepts in Marx's theory of value."

**Significance**:
- Basis of income distribution → basis of rate of profit
- Central to understanding specific nature of capitalist society
- Measurement: **S/V** (surplus-value / variable capital = wages of productive workers)
- Shows: How much worker contributes to value increase + how value-added shared

**Alternative expression**:
- **surplus labor time / necessary labor time**

**Radical Politics** (p. 326):
> "Because the rate expresses quantitatively the contradictory interests of the capitalists and the workers, there is an implied radical politics in the analysis of the rate of exploitation. It enables workers to see how much of the share of the value produced is appropriated from them by capitalists, and therefore to make the case for a different way to organise production and end exploitation."

**Mechanisms of Increase**:
- Even if worker paid MORE, rate STILL increases via:
  1. **Mechanization** → cheapening of consumption goods
  2. **Efficient production management** → raising intensity of labor

**Necessary vs Surplus Labor Time** (pp. 326-327):
- **Necessary labor time**: Fraction of workday to produce commodities covering own wages
  - "Necessary" = workers must reproduce depleted labor power continuously
  - Varies across epochs and countries (different consumption baskets, standards of living)
  - Lower standard of living → shorter necessary labor time → lower wages
- **Surplus labor time**: Remainder of workday after necessary labor
  - Time producing commodities above/beyond what needed for wage bill

**US Empirical Evidence** (p. 327):

**Shaikh & Tonak (1994)**:
- Method: Step-by-step estimation for US economy 1948-1989
- Finding: Rate of surplus-value increased **over 40%** during post-war period
  - **170% (1948) → 244% (1989)**

**Mohun (2005) - Updated estimates**:
- **200% (1980) → 300% (2001)**
- Incorporated corrections + newer data sets

**Marx's Foresight Confirmed**:
1. As capitalism develops, rate of surplus-value necessarily goes up → **CONFIRMED** (almost twofold in 50 years)
2. Capitalists use variety of means to increase rate, especially during falling profit rates → **CONFIRMED**

**Reagan Era Impact** (p. 328):
> "It is now a well-established fact that 'the repression directed against labour beginning in the Reagan era had the clear effect of reversing the post-war pattern of profitability' (Shaikh, 2016), as manifested in the increase of the rate of surplus-value from 200% in the 1980s to 300% in the 2000s."

### Super-Exploitation Debate (Page 330)

**Historical Context**:
- Difference in rates North-South = central idea for "super-exploitation" concept
- Used to develop theory of imperialism
- Originates: Roy Mauro Marini
- Recent proponents: Andy Higginbottom (2010), John Smith (2016)
- Smith's book: Award-winning, generated substantial debate (see M. Roberts blog 2019)

**Tonak's Position**:

**Points of Agreement with Smith**:
1. Global expansion of productive capital circuit (value chains) = recent, important phenomenon
2. Requires more theoretical + empirical work using labor theory of value
3. Firms integral to South value chains impose higher rates of surplus-value

**Critique** (Footnote 10):
> "From our point of view, super-exploitationists in the last analysis belong to the underdevelopment school because, for them, the issue has been how to identify the source of the 'development of underdevelopment' within the realm of the so-called external causes, i.e., imperialism."

**Problems with super-exploitation approach**:
1. Underdevelopment of South = product of development of North (intertwined processes)
2. **Implies passivity** of working classes in BOTH South AND North
3. Focuses on "external causes" rather than internal dynamics
