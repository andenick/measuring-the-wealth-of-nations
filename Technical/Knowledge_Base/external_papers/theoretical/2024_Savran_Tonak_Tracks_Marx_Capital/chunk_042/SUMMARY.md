## Chapter 16: The Controversy Between the Neo-Ricardians (Conclusion)

### Main Arguments

#### 1. Sraffa's Standard Commodity Cannot Replace Marx's Transformation (Pages 393)
- **Argument:** Sraffa's standard commodity cannot serve to isolate changes in value from changes in production conditions because every production matrix has its unique standard commodity
- **Key Point:** Even if Sraffa's standard commodity solved Ricardo's problem, it wouldn't solve Marx's transformation procedure
- **Reason:** Marx's transformation itself solves Ricardo's unresolved problem by separating "absolute value" from "exchangeable value" through different levels of abstraction

#### 2. Two Levels of Abstraction in Marx's Work (Page 393)
- **Volume I (Capital in General):** Value/exchange-value at the level of capital-wage labour relationship
- **Volume III (Competition):** Price of production incorporating competition and distribution
- **Implication:** This distinction allows separate determination of:
  - Impact of production condition changes (value)
  - Impact of distribution changes (price of production)
- **Result:** No need for an invariable measure of value

#### 3. Sraffa's Framework Eliminates the Transformation Problem (Page 393)
- **Observation:** Sraffa's framework involves only prices, not values
- **Consequence:** Adopting the Sraffian framework eliminates values, removing the necessity of transformation
- **Conclusion:** Sraffa's theory cannot be a solution to the "transformation problem"

#### 4. The "Negative Values" Allegation (Pages 393-394)
- **Claim:** When Marx's value concept is applied to joint production and fixed capital, negative values result
- **Neo-Ricardian conclusion:** This proves Marx's value theory is logically inconsistent
- **Counter-argument:** Negative values result from misrepresenting Marx's theory, specifically:
  - The equation system disregards Marx's distinction between individual values and social value
  - Marx's method: Different techniques → distinct individual values → equalization → social value
  - Steedman's system: Assumes identity (not difference) between individual values and social value
- **Verdict:** Contradictions result from a "disfigured concept of value," not Marx's theory (Mandel & Freeman, 1985, 211-19)

#### 5. Sraffa's Framework Has Internal Contradictions (Page 394)
- **Issue:** Exogenous determination of distribution (setting wage rate or profit rate independently)
- **Problem:** No guarantee that resulting real wage will suffice for bare worker survival
- **Contradiction:** Worker survival is a presupposition of Sraffa's system
- **Conclusion:** "The system simply does not respond to its own requirements" (Savran, 1979)

#### 6. Materialist Theory Requires Value Theory (Pages 394-395)
- **Neo-Ricardian claim:** Materialist theory of capitalism can be built on Sraffian economics without value theory
- **Savran's response:** This misreads Marx's purpose in Capital

**Three reasons value theory is indispensable:**

**a) Historical Specificity of Capitalist Relations (Page 395)**
- Value theory enables discovery of historical specificity of capitalist social relations
- Analysis of value form leads to commodity fetishism
- Shows that under capitalism, relations between people appear as relations between things
- This occurs in "real" relations of production, not just ideology

**b) Transformation as Theoretical Superiority (Page 395)**
- Transformation of values into prices and surplus-value into profit is not a "nuisance"
- It's a source of superiority over competing theories
- Demonstrates that under capitalism, form of appearance diverges from essence, even concealing it

**c) Exploitation Requires Value Theory (Page 395)**
- Exploitation means appropriation of surplus labour by capitalist
- Must prove that profit, interest, commercial profit have their source in surplus labour
- Without labour theory of value, this becomes impossible

**d) Laws of Motion of Capital (Page 395)**
- Marx's aim: Explain laws of motion, not just decipher capitalist relations
- Requires level of abstraction isolating capital-labour relationship from competition
- Volume I (where values are embedded) enables Marx to:
  - Analyze capitalism as mode of production (not just distribution)
  - Explain dynamics of production and reproduction of capital-wage labour relation
  - Develop concept of subsumption of labour under capital (formal and real)

#### 7. Conclusion of the Controversy (Page 396)
- **Verdict:** Neo-Ricardian assault proved to be "a passing storm"
- **Result:** All neo-Ricardian allegations rebutted effectively
- **Benefit:** Controversy spurred Marxists to sharpen tools and elaborate concepts
- **Status:** Capital's theoretical framework remains "indispensable basis" for understanding capitalism

#### 8. Legacy of Sraffa and Neo-Ricardianism (Page 396)
- **Six decades after Sraffa:** Not much remains
- **Critique of neoclassicism:** Relegated to oblivion
- **Critique of Marxism:** Proved unfounded
- **Current alliance:** Neo-Ricardianism with post-Keynesianism
  - Post-Keynesianism rejects "bastard Keynesianism" (Joan Robinson's term)
  - Rejects "grand synthesis" between Keynesian macro and neoclassical micro
  - Comforted by existence of alternative value theory traced to Sraffa
- **Irony noted:** Keynes set out to demolish Ricardianism in The General Theory, but now owes independence from neoclassicism to neo-Ricardianism

## Chapter 17: The Negation of "Negative Values"

### Introduction (Page 399)

#### The Negative Values Debate
- **Initiator:** Ian Steedman (1975)
- **Central to:** Steedman's critique of labour values (1977, 150-162)
- **Claims:** Extension of Marxist value theory to joint production and fixed capital yields absurd results (negative values, negative surplus-value)
- **Apparent implication:** Something fundamentally wrong with Marx's theory
- **Perceived strength:** "Impeccable nature of Steedman's mathematical argumentation"

#### Savran's Counter-Thesis
- **Core argument:** Mathematical argumentation based on "fallacious reconstruction" of Marx's concept of value
- **Method:** Demonstration rests on "inacceptable replacement" of Marx's concept with alien concept
- **Result:** Anomalies falsely presented as resulting from Marx's own theory
- **Purpose:** Show anomalous results "have no relationship to Marx's theory of value"

### Section 1: Social Values (Pages 400-401)

#### Reducing Steedman's Situation to True Dimensions (Page 400)
- **Steedman's claim:** Joint production pure and simple
- **Reality:** Each of two commodities produced using two different processes
- **Implication:** Two different production methods for each commodity exist in society
- **Requirement:** Must follow Marx's own method for dealing with such situations

#### Marx's Method in Capital Volume I (Page 400)
- **Level of abstraction:** Commodity and "capital in general" (capital in sole relationship to wage labour)
- **Abstraction from:** Relations among different producers of same commodity
- **Requirement:** Labour embodied must be socially necessary, no more, no less
- **Definition:** "The conditions of production normal for a given society" (Marx, 1976, 129)
- **Nature:** Abstract definition - doesn't explain how "normal" is determined concretely
- **Indifference:** To various conditions of production (average, high, or low productivity)

#### Marx's Method in Capital Volume III (Pages 400-401)
- **Inclusion:** Relations between various capitals
- **Cannot abstract from:** Competition among producers within single branch
- **Result:** Analysis of situations where different capitals produce same commodity with differing productivity

#### Social Value Determination (Page 401)
- **New determinations:** Socially necessary labour made concrete
- **Formula:** Social (market) value per unit = Total labour expended in branch ÷ Total mass of commodities produced in branch
- **Key insight:** Commodities under different conditions have distinct individual values
- **Process:** Individual values equalized through a process into social value

#### Conditions Determining Social Value (Page 401)
- **Variability:** Different conditions of productivity may influence/determine social value in different circumstances
- **Determinants:** Can be lowest, highest, or average productivity levels
- **Steedman's error:** Claims Marx "repeatedly asserted" value defined by average conditions only (Steedman, 1976, 607)
- **Truth:** Marx recognizes all three possibilities

#### Critical Distinction (Page 401)
- **Key fact:** For Marx, same commodity produced with different methods has multiplicity of individual values
- **General principle:** None of individual values equals social value
- **Social value:** Comes about only through equalization process

### Section 2: The Equalisation Process (Pages 402-403)

#### Preliminary Points (Page 402)

**Point 1: Negative surplus-value contingent on negative values**
- Once negative values concept eliminated, negative surplus-value problem disappears

**Point 2: Steedman's example features productivity difference**
- Two commodities produced jointly by two different processes
- One process represents higher productivity of labour

**The Wolfstetter Condition (Page 402)**
- **Table 1 data:**
  - Process 1: net product contains 1 unit of each commodity
  - Process 2: net product contains 3 units commodity 1, 2 units commodity 2
- **Significance:** Not coincidental
- **Proof:** Negative values can only arise under such conditions
- **Necessary condition:** Absolute difference of productivity between processes (Wolfstetter, 1976)
- **Implication:** If Steedman's method shown inappropriate for this case, negative values question disappears totally

#### Individual Values in Joint Production (Pages 402-403)

**One-product processes:**
- Given values of other commodities, individual values can be "read off" from individual processes

**Joint production:**
- Not possible to "read off" individual values from individual processes
- At least some processes produce more than one commodity

#### Critical Theorem (Page 403)
**Where absolute productivity difference exists:**
- Individual values of both commodities cannot be simultaneously equal for both processes
- If individual values of first commodity are equal in two processes, those of second are necessarily unequal, and vice versa
- Probability: Individual values of both commodities different for two processes
- Special case: One commodity may have identical individual values in both processes
- Impossibility: This cannot be true for both commodities simultaneously

#### Proof of the Theorem (Page 403)
1. **Assumption:** Pick commodity at random, assume its individual values in two processes are equal
2. **Deduction for first commodity:** After allocating value to first commodity
3. **Remaining labour:** Quantity remaining for second commodity in first process < that in second process
   - Reason: More of first commodity produced in second process
   - Whatever value allocated to first commodity in first process, more must be allocated in second
4. **Production of second commodity:** More produced in second process than first
5. **Value determination:** For second commodity:
   - Second process: Smaller labour amount ÷ Larger quantity of units
   - First process: Larger labour amount ÷ Smaller quantity of units
6. **Conclusion:** Individual value of second commodity necessarily smaller in second process than first
7. **Result:** Equality of individual values not possible for both commodities simultaneously

#### Steedman's Fundamental Error (Page 403)
- **Marx's method:** Process of equalisation between distinct individual values
- **Steedman's method:**
  - Ignores difference between individual values (which has been conclusively proved)
  - Uses simultaneous equations to declare their equality
  - Totally sets aside Marx's own analysis of social value determination
- **Critique:** Assumes equality where Marx describes equalization process
- **Consequence:** Individual values "lose their quality of distinctness only to be assumed equal"

## Key Theoretical Contributions

### 1. Distinction Between Individual and Social Value
The chunk emphasizes Marx's crucial distinction between:
- **Individual values:** Arising from specific production processes/techniques
- **Social value:** Result of equalization process among individual values
- **Importance:** This distinction is what Steedman's formalization ignores

### 2. Levels of Abstraction Methodology
Two levels identified in Marx's Capital:
- **Abstract level (Volume I):** Capital in general, capital-wage labour relation
- **Concrete level (Volume III):** Competition, distribution, multiple capitals
- **Function:** Allows separation of different types of effects on value

### 3. Transformation as Solution, Not Problem
- Marx's transformation solves Ricardo's unresolved problem
- Separates concepts that Ricardo couldn't distinguish
- Not a "nuisance" but a theoretical superiority
- Shows divergence between essence and appearance

### 4. Value Theory as Foundation for Historical Analysis
Value theory enables:
- Discovery of historical specificity of capitalist relations
- Analysis of commodity fetishism
- Understanding of exploitation as surplus labour appropriation
- Analysis of capitalism as mode of production (not just distribution)
- Development of subsumption concept

### 5. Critique of Formalization Without Theory
- Mathematical formalization must respect theoretical content
- Steedman's "impeccable mathematics" based on "fallacious reconstruction"
- Anomalies result from misrepresentation, not from Marx's theory
- Demonstrates danger of equation systems that contradict their theoretical basis

## Critical Terms and Concepts

### Economic Theory Terms
- **Absolute value vs. Exchangeable value** (Ricardian terminology)
- **Capital in general:** Abstraction focusing on capital-wage labour relation
- **Commodity fetishism:** Relations between people appearing as relations between things
- **Exogenous determination:** Setting variables outside/independent of equation system
- **Fixed capital:** Special case of joint production (output + older machinery)
- **Individual values:** Values resulting from specific production techniques
- **Invariable measure of value:** Ricardo's sought-after concept
- **Joint production:** More than one commodity produced through same process
- **Laws of motion of capital:** Dynamic tendencies of capitalist accumulation
- **Negative values:** Anomalous result of applying certain equation systems
- **Negative surplus-value:** Anomalous result contingent on negative values
- **Net product:** Sraffa's concept (output minus input)
- **Price of production:** More concrete category than value, incorporating competition
- **Social (market) value:** Unified value resulting from equalization of individual values
- **Socially necessary labour:** Labour required under normal conditions
- **Standard commodity:** Sraffa's device for invariable measure
- **Subsumption of labour under capital:** Formal and real subsumption
- **Surplus labour:** Part of worker's labour appropriated by capitalist
- **Transformation problem:** Alleged inconsistency in transforming values to prices

### Schools and Approaches
- **Bastard Keynesianism:** Joan Robinson's term for neoclassical-Keynesian synthesis
- **Grand synthesis:** Post-war synthesis of Keynesian macro and neoclassical micro
- **Marxism:** Based on Marx's Capital
- **Neoclassicism:** Mainstream economic theory critiqued by Sraffa
- **Neo-Ricardianism:** School based on Sraffa's work
- **Post-Keynesianism:** Rejects neoclassical bases of mainstream Keynesianism
- **Sraffian economics:** Based on Production of Commodities by Means of Commodities

### Methodological Terms
- **Abstraction (levels of):** Different degrees of concreteness in analysis
- **Equalization process:** Process by which individual values become social value
- **Historical specificity:** Unique characteristics of particular mode of production
- **Mode of distribution:** How social product is distributed
- **Mode of production:** System of producing and reproducing social life
- **Simultaneous equations:** Mathematical method used by Sraffa and Steedman

## Key Arguments Structure

### Neo-Ricardian Critique of Marx (As Presented)
1. Marx's transformation procedure is inconsistent
2. Sraffa's standard commodity provides better solution
3. Negative values prove Marx's theory logically flawed
4. Materialist theory possible without value theory
5. Sraffa's framework is logically consistent

### Marxist Response (Savran's Arguments)
1. **On transformation:**
   - Marx's transformation IS the solution to Ricardo's problem
   - Sraffa's framework eliminates values, thus no transformation needed
   - Different levels of abstraction allow separation of effects

2. **On negative values:**
   - Result from misrepresenting Marx's concept of value
   - Steedman's equations assume equality of individual values
   - Marx's theory requires equalization process, not assumed equality
   - Contradictions result from disfigured concept, not Marx's theory

3. **On Sraffa's consistency:**
   - Exogenous determination creates contradictions
   - No guarantee of subsistence wage
   - System doesn't respond to its own requirements

4. **On value theory's necessity:**
   - Enables discovery of historical specificity
   - Necessary for demonstrating exploitation
   - Required for analyzing laws of motion
   - Foundation for commodity fetishism concept
   - Shows divergence of appearance from essence

### Resolution
- Neo-Ricardian critique was "a passing storm"
- All allegations rebutted effectively
- Capital remains indispensable basis for understanding capitalism
- Controversy benefited Marxism by sharpening concepts

### Table 1: The Wolfstetter Condition
**Process 1:**
- Input: 5 units Commodity 1, 0 units Commodity 2, 1 unit Labor
- Output: 6 units Commodity 1, 6 units Commodity 2
- Net product: 1 unit Commodity 1, 1 unit Commodity 2 (per unit labor)

**Process 2:**
- Input: 0 units Commodity 1, 10 units Commodity 2, 1 unit Labor
- Output: 3 units Commodity 1, 12 units Commodity 2
- Net product: 3 units Commodity 1, 2 units Commodity 2 (per unit labor)

**Productivity comparison:**
- Process 2 produces 3× more net Commodity 1 than Process 1
- Process 2 produces 2× more net Commodity 2 than Process 1
- Both use same labor input (1 unit)
- Demonstrates absolute productivity difference

## Analytical Approach

### Savran's Methodology
1. **Identify the claim:** What neo-Ricardians assert about Marx
2. **Examine the formalization:** How they represent Marx's theory mathematically
3. **Compare to Marx's actual theory:** What Marx actually says
4. **Identify the misrepresentation:** Where formalization deviates from theory
5. **Demonstrate the consequence:** How misrepresentation generates anomalies
6. **Conclude:** Anomalies result from misrepresentation, not from Marx's theory

### Structure of Argument
- **Chapter 16 conclusion:** Overview of entire neo-Ricardian controversy
- **Chapter 17 beginning:** Detailed examination of one specific issue (negative values)
- **Movement:** From general to specific
- **Method:** Both theoretical analysis and mathematical demonstration
