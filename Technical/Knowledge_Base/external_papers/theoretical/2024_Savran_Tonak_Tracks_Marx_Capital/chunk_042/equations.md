## Theoretical Formulations

### 1. Socially Necessary Labour (Page 400)
**Description:** Marx's definition of socially necessary labour from Capital Volume I

**Formulation (prose):** "the conditions of production normal for a given society" (Marx, 1976, 129)

**Context:** This is the abstract definition used in Capital Volume I, which does not specify how "normal" conditions are determined concretely.

---

### 2. Social Value Determination (Page 401)
**Description:** The determination of social (or market) value in branches with different productivity conditions

**Formulation (prose):** "the social (or market) value of a unit of the commodity produced in this branch is determined by the division of the whole quantity of labour expended in the branch to the total mass of commodities produced in the branch"

**Mathematical concept:**
```
Social Value per Unit = Total Labour Expended in Branch / Total Mass of Commodities Produced in Branch
```

**Context:** This represents Marx's concrete determination of value in Capital Volume III, where competition among producers is taken into account.

---

### 3. Individual Values vs. Social Value (Page 401)
**Description:** The relationship between individual values (from different production methods) and social value

**Formulation:** "Commodities under different conditions of production have distinct individual values which are equalized, through a process, in the social value of the commodity"

**Key concept:** Individual values ≠ Social value (in general)

**Context:** This is a central theoretical point distinguishing Marx's approach from Steedman's misrepresentation.

---

### 4. Steedman's Simultaneous Equations (Page 403)
**Description:** Steedman's use of simultaneous equations to determine values in joint production

**Issue identified:** Steedman's equations assume:
- Individual Value (Process 1, Commodity i) = Individual Value (Process 2, Commodity i) = Social Value (Commodity i)

**Marx's actual theory:**
- Individual Value (Process 1, Commodity i) ≠ Individual Value (Process 2, Commodity i)
- Individual Values → equalization process → Social Value

**Context:** This is the core of Savran's critique - Steedman assumes equality of individual values rather than modeling their equalization process.

### Productivity Comparison (Page 403)
**Theoretical relationship:** When comparing two processes with absolute productivity differences:

**Given:**
- Process 1 produces quantities (q₁₁, q₁₂) of commodities (1, 2)
- Process 2 produces quantities (q₂₁, q₂₂) of commodities (1, 2)
- Both use same labor input L

**If individual values v₁ are equal across processes:**
Then necessarily: v₂ (Process 1) ≠ v₂ (Process 2)

**Proof structure (described on page 403):**
1. Assume v₁ (Process 1) = v₁ (Process 2)
2. More of commodity 1 is produced in Process 2, so more total value is allocated to commodity 1 in Process 2
3. Remaining labor for commodity 2 in Process 1 > Remaining labor for commodity 2 in Process 2
4. But more of commodity 2 is produced in Process 2
5. Therefore: (Smaller remaining labor) / (Larger quantity) in Process 2 < (Larger remaining labor) / (Smaller quantity) in Process 1
6. Hence: v₂ (Process 2) < v₂ (Process 1)

## Equation System Concepts Discussed

### Sraffa's Framework
- Involves "only a set of prices" (page 393)
- No values, therefore no transformation problem
- Exogenous determination of distribution (either wage rate or profit rate set independently)

### Marx's Transformation
- From values to prices of production
- Separates "absolute value" from "exchangeable value" (Ricardian terminology)
- Two levels of abstraction: Capital Volume I (value) vs. Capital Volume III (price of production)

**Sraffa's system contradiction:**
- System requires: Real wage ≥ Subsistence wage (for worker survival)

- Result: System "does not respond to its own requirements"
