**Misconception 2** (p. 393):

**Even if Sraffa solved Ricardo, wouldn't solve Marx**:
- Marx's transformation itself = solution to Ricardo's unresolved problem
- Ricardo's problem: Separate "absolute value" from "exchangeable value"

**Two Levels in Marx** (p. 393):
1. **Value** (exchange-value):
   - Level: Capital in general
   - Focus: Relationship between capital and wage labour
   - Location: *Capital* Volume I
2. **Price of production**:

   - Takes into account: Competition, hence distribution
   - Location: *Capital* Volume III

**Footnote 1**: See Chapter 3 for fuller explanation of different abstraction levels

**Result of Distinction**:
> "Once this distinction is established, it is possible to determine separately the impact of a change in production conditions (value) and the impact of a change in distribution (price of production). The need for an invariable measure of value vanishes."

**Misconception 3** (p. 393):

**Common-Sense Oversight**:
- Sraffa's framework: Only a set of **prices**
- Transformation: Between **values** and **prices**
- Adopting Sraffian framework = does away with values
- Removes necessity of transformation

**Conclusion**:
> "So, whatever may be made of Sraffa's theory, it certainly is not a solution to the so-called 'transformation problem'."

**Negative Values Issue** (pp. 393-394):

**Neo-Ricardian Claim**:
- Marx's value concept applied to:
  - Joint production (>1 commodity from same process)
  - Fixed capital value (special case of joint production: output + older machinery)
- Generates anomalous results: **negative values**
- Since no commodity can reasonably be produced through negative labour → value theory logically inconsistent

**Marxist Response** (p. 394):

**Argument shown to be FALSE**:
- Equation system supposedly represents Marx's labour theory
- **Upon closer inspection**: System disregards Marx's distinction (*Capital* Vol. III)
- **Distinction**: Individual values vs social value when different techniques used

**Marx's Method**:
- When >1 production technique exists for single commodity:
  - Each technique yields distinct **individual value**
  - Arrive at **social value** different from individual values
- Equation system generating negative values assumes **identity** (not difference) between:
  - Individual values from different techniques
  - Individual values and final social value

**Conclusion** (p. 394):
> "Hence, it is not Marx's value theory but a disfigured concept of value that generates negative values. So, the contradictions that ensue cannot be attributed to Marx's theory." (Mandel & Freeman 1985:211-19)

### Section 4: Sraffa as a Solution to Marx's Problems? (pp. 394-395)

**Affirmative Proposition Questioned** (p. 394):

**Neo-Ricardian Claim**: Sraffa's framework logically consistent (unlike Marx's)

**Marxist Critique**:
- Exogenous distribution determination → **contradictions** in Sraffian framework
- Exogenous determination can be:
  - Wage rate set externally, OR
  - Rate of profit set externally
- **Problem**: Whichever chosen, no guarantee resulting real wage sufficient for **bare survival**
- Worker survival = presupposition of Sraffa's equation system
- System "simply does not respond to its own requirements" (Savran 1979)

**Theoretical Proposition Examined** (pp. 394-395):

**Neo-Ricardian Claim**: Materialist capitalism theory can be constructed on Sraffian basis (jettisoning value theory)

**Problem**: Misreading Marx's purpose in writing *Capital*, role/function of value theory

**Three Marxist Counter-Arguments**:

**1. Historical Specificity** (p. 395):
- Even if prices/distribution establishable without values
- Discarding value theory eliminates discovering **historical specificity** of capitalist social relations
- Thanks to **value form analysis** → **commodity fetishism**
- Shows: Relations between people appear as relations between things
  - In "real" relations of production (not just ideology)

**Transformation = Superiority, Not Nuisance**:
> "The transformation of values into prices and of surplus-value into profit is not a 'nuisance' for Marx's theoretical edifice, but is a source of superiority over competing theories, because it shows that, under capitalist conditions, the form of appearance of the social relations diverges from their essence, even concealing it."

**2. Exploitation Requires Labour Theory** (p. 395):
- Exploitation = appropriation of part of worker's labour (surplus labour) by capitalist
- Must prove: Profit, interest, commercial profit have source in surplus labour
- Without labour theory of value: Impossible to prove
- Not simply counterpart to "productivity of capital"

**3. Laws of Motion of Capital** (p. 395):
- Marx's aim: Not only decipher capitalist relations BUT explain **laws of motion**
- Requires abstraction level isolating capital-labour relationship from intruding factors (including competition)
- This level (*Capital* Vol. I): Where values logically embedded
- Enables Marx to:
  - Analyze capitalism as **mode of production** (not mode of distribution)
  - Explain dynamics of production/reproduction of capital-wage labour relation
  - Develop concept: **(Formal and real) subsumption of labour under capital**

### Section 1: Social Values (pp. 400-402)

**Core Argument** (p. 400):
> "Steedman reaches negative values and negative surplus-value through the application not of Marx's concept of value but of a totally erroneous and caricatured version of it."

**Steedman's Situation Reduced to True Dimensions** (p. 400):
- **NOT** joint production pure and simple
- **IS**: Each of two commodities produced using **two different processes**
- = Two different production methods for each commodity
- Must follow **Marx's own method** for such questions

**Marx's Method - *Capital* Volume I** (p. 400):

**Level of Abstraction**:
- Investigates: Commodity and "capital in general"
- Capital in sole relationship to wage labour
- Abstracts from: Relations among different producers of same commodity
- Effects excluded by nature of abstraction level

**Socially Necessary Labour** (p. 400):
- Labour embodied in commodity must be socially necessary
- Determined by: "Conditions of production normal for a given society" (Marx 1976:129)
- **First definition** = abstract
- Not explained how "normal" determined concretely
- Indifferent to various actual conditions (average, high, low productivity)

**Footnote 2**: See Marx (1981, Chapter 10)

**Marx's Method - *Capital* Volume III** (p. 401):

**New Level**:
- Relations between various capitals included
- Competition among producers within single branch **cannot** be abstracted from
- Analysis of different capitals producing same commodity with differing productivity methods

**Socially Necessary Labour - Concretized** (p. 401):
- Subject to new determinations beyond Vol. I generality
- Made concrete

**Social (Market) Value Determination**:
> "The social (or market) value of a unit of the commodity produced in this branch is determined by the division of the whole quantity of labour expended in the branch to the total mass of commodities produced in the branch."

**Individual vs Social Values**:
- Commodities under different conditions → **distinct individual values**
- Equalized through process → **social value**

**Marx's Investigation** (p. 401):
- Effect of different production conditions on determining social value
- **Key Point**: In different circumstances, different productivity conditions may influence/determine social value
- Social value can be determined by:
  - Lowest productivity producers, OR
  - Highest productivity producers, OR
  - Average productivity producers

**Steedman's Distortion** (p. 401):

**Steedman's False Claim** (debate with Morishima):
> "In his mature works, Marx repeatedly asserted that... he would define the value of [a] commodity by reference to the average conditions of production and not by reference to the most (or least) favourable conditions." (Steedman 1976:607)

**More Important Fact** (p. 401):
> "For Marx the same commodity, when produced with different methods, has a multiplicity of individual values, none of which is in general equal to the social value of the commodity. The latter comes about only as a result of a process of equalisation."

**Application to Steedman** (pp. 401-402):

**Two Preliminary Points** (p. 402):

**1. Negative Surplus-Value Contingent**:
- Result entirely contingent on negative values existence
- Once negative values concept done away with → no negative surplus-value problem

**2. Higher Productivity in One Process** (p. 402):
- Steedman's example (1977:137-149): Two commodities jointly by two processes

- **Not coincidence**: Proven negative values only arise under such conditions
- Absolute productivity difference = **necessary condition** for negative values (Wolfstetter 1976)

### Section 2: The Equalisation Process (pp. 402-403)

**One-Product vs Joint Production** (p. 402):

**One-product processes**:
- Given values of all other commodities
- Individual values determinable by recourse to individual processes

**Joint production**:
- NOT possible to "read off" individual values from individual processes
- At least some processes produce >1 commodity

**Key Fact with Absolute Productivity Difference** (p. 403):

**Unequivocal Establishment**:
> "Individual values of both commodities cannot be simultaneously equal for both processes."

**Logical Consequences**:
- If individual values of commodity 1 equal in both processes → commodity 2 necessarily unequal, vice versa
- Probably: Individual values of both commodities different for both processes
- Special case: ONE commodity may have identical individual values
- BUT: Cannot be true for **both commodities simultaneously**

**Proof** (p. 403):

**Simple Logic**:
1. Pick commodity at random, assume individual values equal in both processes
2. After deduction for this commodity:
   - Labour remaining for commodity 2 in process 1 < process 2
   - Reason: Process 2 produced more of commodity 1
   - Whatever value allocated to commodity 1 in process 1 → more must be allocated in process 2
3. Process 2 also produced more of commodity 2
4. Therefore: Smaller labour amount divided by larger units in process 2
5. **Result**: Individual value of commodity 2 necessarily smaller in process 2

**Conclusion**:
> "Equality between individual values in the two processes is not possible for commodities simultaneously."

**Critical Implication** (p. 403):

**With Absolute Productivity Difference**:
- Individual values of **at least one commodity** unequal in two processes
- Method consistent with Marx must investigate **equalisation** of distinct individual values

**Steedman's Violation** (p. 403):
> "Steedman claims that he is applying Marx's method to his own special case. Yet what he does is to ignore this difference between the individual values (which has conclusively been proved) and to declare, through the use of simultaneous equations, their equality."

**Fundamental Error**:
- Totally sets aside Marx's analysis of social value determination
- Marx: Process of equalisation between **distinct** individual values
- Steedman: Individual values lose distinctness, assumed equal
