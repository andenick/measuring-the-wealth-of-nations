### Theoretical Focus

This chunk presents a comprehensive historical narrative of the translation, publication, and intellectual reception of Marx's *Capital* in Turkey from the early 20th century through the 21st century. The content is organized chronologically and thematically:

**Key Historical Periods Covered**:
1. **Early Period (1908-1920s)**: First abbreviated translation by Bohor Israel after 1908 revolution
2. **Interwar Period (1930s)**: Multiple summary translations (1933-1937) followed by 1938 ban
3. **Post-War Repression (1940s-1960s)**: Quarter-century of censorship under NATO alignment
4. **Golden Age (1960s-1970s)**: Full translations coinciding with class struggle peak
5. **Institutionalization (1980s-2000s)**: Capital's independent academic/cultural life
6. **21st Century Renaissance (2000s-2010s)**: New German translations and popular resurgence

**Analytical Themes**:
- Relationship between political conditions and translation activity
- Role of non-Muslim minorities in early socialist movements
- Connection between class struggle intensity and theoretical interest
- Publisher and translator rivalries (Sol-Selik-Bilgi controversy)
- Generational succession of Marxist economists
- Popular cultural penetration of Marxist economics

### Historical Significance

The chapter demonstrates how Capital's reception in Turkey was intimately tied to:
- Political repression and liberation cycles
- Multinational character of Ottoman/Turkish socialist movement
- Military coups and their aftermath (1960, 1971, 1980)
- International political alignments (Soviet Union, NATO, etc.)
- Class struggle waves (especially 1960s-70s)

## Translation Controversy

### Sol-Bilgi vs Yordam-Selik/Satlıgan Dispute

**Central Issue**: Whether Selik translations were from German (as claimed) or English (as disputed)

**Sol-Bilgi Position**:
- Bilgi translation (1975-78) is complete and reliable
- Translated from English with French/Russian checking
- First complete three-volume translation
- Selik actually used English not German

**Yordam-Selik/Satlıgan Position**:
- Selik translations were from German original
- Yordam edition superior due to German source
- Satlıgan portions definitely from German
- English used only as control translation

**Unresolved Questions**:
- Source language of Selik's Volume II (discovered posthumously)
- Conclusive evidence for Selik's language competencies
- Whether Volume I was truly from German or English

**Established Facts**:
- Bilgi edition: Completely from English (with French/Russian checks)
- Yordam edition: Partially from German (Satlıgan and Özalp sections certain; Selik sections disputed)
