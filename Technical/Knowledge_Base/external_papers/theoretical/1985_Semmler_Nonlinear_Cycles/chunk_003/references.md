**Key Theoretical Lineages:**

The reference list reveals three main theoretical lineages that converge in this paper:

1. **Ecological Models → Economic Cycles:** Lotka (1925) and Volterra (1926) developed predator-prey models → Goodwin (1967) adapted these to model capital-labor dynamics → Current paper extends to discrete time with additional nonlinearities

2. **Business Cycle Theory:** Samuelson (1939) and Hicks (1950) developed linear models → Goodwin (1951) introduced nonlinearity → Current paper builds on this tradition with discrete time formulation

3. **Chaos Theory:** May (1976) demonstrated chaos in simple nonlinear maps → Current paper applies these insights to economic growth cycles

**Notable Absences:**

Given the 1985 publication date and the paper's focus on nonlinear dynamics and chaos, some notable potential references that are absent include:
- Li and Yorke (1975) - "Period Three Implies Chaos"
- Feigenbaum (1978) - Period doubling cascade
- Lorenz (1963) - Deterministic chaos
- Earlier classical economists (Marx, Ricardo) despite "classical growth theory" framework
- Other contemporary work on chaotic economic dynamics from the early 1980s

The relatively short reference list (7 items) suggests this may be an early contribution to the application of chaos theory to economic growth cycles, building primarily on Goodwin's foundational work and May's demonstration of chaos in simple models.
