## Period Analysis

### Period 1: 1948-1965 ("Golden Age of Accumulation")

**Available only from ST**:

**RSV**: 1.25% growth
- Modest rise in exploitation rate
- Productivity and wages both growing

**y**: 3.60% growth ← **Highest productivity growth of any period**
- Rapid technological progress
- Postwar reconstruction and expansion

**C/v**: 0.76% growth
- Moderate mechanization
- Rising organic composition but gradual

**R**: 0.48% growth
- Rising general profitability
- Healthy accumulation climate

**r**: -0.23% growth
- Slight decline despite rising R
- But levels remain high (~10-11%)

**ΔC/C**: 3.28% accumulation
- Vigorous capital accumulation
- Mass of profits rising despite falling r

**Characteristics**:
- High productivity growth
- Wages and productivity rise together
- High profit rates
- Robust accumulation
- Low unproductive burden

---

### Period 2: 1965-1982 ("Silent Depression")

**All three authors available for most variables**:

**RSV**: 0.26% (ST), 0.49% (PT), 0.09% (SM)
- **Near-stagnant** rate of surplus value
- Productivity slowdown + wage resistance
- Class struggle limits exploitation

**y**: 2.50% (ST), 1.40% (PT), 1.15% (SM)
- **Sharp productivity slowdown**
- PT shows MOST dramatic slowdown (3.60% → 1.40%)
- ST estimate masks severity (3.60% → 2.50%)
- Technological stagnation

**w_P**: 0.20% (PT)
- Real wages barely growing
- Peak in 1977, then decline begins
- Productivity-wage gap opening

**C/v**: 2.41% (ST), 2.69% (PT), 2.60% (SM)
- **Rapid mechanization**
- Fastest C/v growth of any period
- Rising organic composition
- Compensating for stagnant RSV

**R**: -2.14% (ST), -2.20% (PT), -2.50% (SM)
- **Falling general profitability**
- All three authors agree: substantial decline
- Rising C/v overwhelms stagnant RSV

**r**: -6.29% (PT)
- **Catastrophic collapse** of net profitability
- Fastest decline of any period
- From 10.9% (1966) to 3.8% (1982)
- 65% decline in 16 years!

**ΔC/C**: 3.88% (PT)
- **Paradox**: Capital still accumulating despite falling r
- Highest accumulation rate of any period
- Driven by: Expectations, inflation, institutional supports
- Overaccumulation → crisis

**Characteristics**:
- Productivity slowdown
- Profitability collapse
- Mechanization accelerates (desperate attempt to restore profits)
- Continued accumulation despite falling r
- Mass of profits stagnant (Figure 8)
- "Silent" because welfare state cushions blow

---

### Period 3: 1982-1989 ("Early Recovery")

**All three authors available**:

**RSV**: 1.52% (ST), 3.03% (PT), 2.78% (SM)
- **Sharp acceleration**
- PT shows fastest growth (3.03% vs. 1.52% ST)
- Wage suppression + productivity recovery

**y**: 2.42% (ST), 2.59% (PT), 2.29% (SM)
- Productivity recovery
- Information technology effects
- But still below 1948-1965 levels

**w_P**: -0.03% (PT)
- **Real wages actually falling**
- Neoliberal assault on labor
- Union busting (PATCO 1981)
- Deindustrialization

**C/v**: -0.99% (ST), -0.33% (PT), -0.44% (SM)
- **Capital devaluation**
- Crisis writedowns
- Negative growth unique to this period
- Creates space for profitability recovery

**R**: 2.51% (ST), 3.37% (PT), 3.57% (SM)
- **Strong recovery**
- Driven by: Rising RSV + falling C/v
- All authors agree on vigorous rebound

**r**: 6.60% (PT)
- **Fastest growth of any period**
- From 3.8% (1982) to ~6.0% (1989)
- But still far below 1966 peak (10.9%)
- Only to 64% of peak by 1997

**ΔC/C**: 2.36% (PT)
- Slowest accumulation of any period
- Despite rising r, investment cautious
- Neoliberal financialization beginning
- Profits into finance, not production

**Characteristics**:
- Capital devaluation clears ground for recovery
- Wage suppression + productivity recovery → soaring RSV
- Profitability recovers but investment lags
- Beginning of financialization era

---

### Period 4: 1982-2007 ("Extended Recovery" / "New Golden Age"?)

**Only PT estimates available**:

**RSV**: 1.64%
- Continued rise but slower than 1982-1989
- Sustained by stagnant wages
- Reaches record 3.55 by 2007

**y**: 1.96%
- Productivity higher than 1965-1982
- Lower than 1948-1965
- Information technology + globalization

**w_P**: 0.15%
- Essentially stagnant for 25 years
- Never recovers 1977 peak
- Growing inequality

**C/v**: 0.56%
- Resumed rise after 1987
- Renewed mechanization
- From 5.4 (1987) to 7.5 (2007)

**R**: 1.08%
- Modest continued growth
- Approaches 1966 levels by 1997
- Declines after 2004

**r**: 1.67%
- Recovery continues but slowing
- Levels stay far below 1966
- Only 64% of peak even at 1997 maximum
- Begins declining 2004-2007

**ΔC/C**: 2.61%
- Moderate accumulation
- Driven by debt, not profits
- Financialization dominant
- Productive investment weak

**Characteristics**:
- "New golden age" for R but not for r
- Gap = unproductive burden
- Wage stagnation sustains high RSV
- Accumulation increasingly debt-financed
- Setting stage for 2008 crisis

## Key Findings from Table 1

### 1. Productivity Slowdown Was Real

**1948-1965**: 3.60% (ST)
**1965-1982**: 1.40% (PT) ← **61% decline in growth rate**
**1982-2007**: 1.96% (PT) ← Recovery but not to golden age levels

**PT data reveals MORE severe slowdown than ST**

### 2. Wage Stagnation Unprecedented

**1965-1982**: 0.20% real wage growth
**1982-2007**: 0.15% real wage growth
**Entire 1964-2007**: 0.26% real wage growth

**Essentially zero for 43 years!**

### 3. Rate of Surplus Value Explosion

**Growth accelerates over time**:
- 1948-1965: 1.25% (ST)
- 1965-1982: 0.49% (PT) - slowdown
- 1982-1989: 3.03% (PT) - explosion
- 1982-2007: 1.64% (PT) - sustained high

**By 2007**: s/v = 3.55 (highest in 60 years)

### 4. Mechanization Trend Interrupted Only by Crisis

**Long-run trend**: C/v rising ~0.76% to 2.69%
**Only exception**: 1982-1989 (-0.33%) when crisis devalues capital
**Resumed**: 1987-2007 rise from 5.4 to 7.5

### 5. General vs. Net Profit Rates Diverge

**General R (1964-2007)**: -0.22% ← Slight decline
**Net r (1964-2007)**: -1.29% ← **Much steeper decline**

**Gap = rising unproductive burden**

### 6. Accumulation Paradoxes

**1965-1982**: r falling -6.29%, yet g = 3.88% (highest!)
- Overaccumulation → stagnant mass of profits → crisis

**1982-2007**: r rising 1.67%, yet g = only 2.61%
- Underaccumulation despite recovery?
- Profits diverted to finance, not production

### 7. Three Authors Largely Agree

**On trends** (1964-1989 where all overlap):
- All show R declining
- All show C/v rising substantially
- All show productivity slowdown 1965-1982
- All show recovery 1982-1989

**PT innovations**:
- Better data shows MORE severe 1960s-70s crisis
- Extends through 2007 showing partial recovery
- Shows late-2000s downturn beginning

   - Agricultural production
   - Productive because creates use-values (food)

   - Support for agricultural production

   - Extraction industries
   - Produces material inputs

   - Creates fixed capital

9. **Hotels and other lodging places**
   - Accommodation services
   - Productive service

10. **Personal services**
    - Laundries, barbers, etc.
    - Productive when wage labor for capital

11. **Auto repair, services, and parking**
    - Repair services (maintain use-value)
    - Productive

12. **Miscellaneous repair services**
    - Equipment repair, etc.
    - Productive

13. **Motion pictures**
    - Cultural production
    - Productive when capitalist (actors hired by studios)

14. **Amusement and recreation services**
    - Entertainment industry
    - Productive when capitalist

15. **Health services**
    - Medical care
    - Productive service

16. **Educational services**
    - Private education
    - Productive when capitalist

17. **Social Services**
    - Social work, etc.
    - Productive component

18. **Membership organizations**
    - Unions, associations
    - To extent they employ wage labor

19. **Government enterprises (federal)**
    - Postal service, TVA, etc.
    - Productive government operations

20. **Government enterprises (local)**
    - Municipal utilities, transit
    - Productive government operations

#### UNPRODUCTIVE ACTIVITIES (7 sectors)

21. **Wholesale trade**
    - Circulation sphere
    - Changes ownership, not use-value
    - Unproductive despite necessity

22. **Retail trade (except eating & drinking places)**
    - Circulation sphere
    - Selling, not producing
    - **Note**: E&D places removed to productive

23. **Finance, insurance, and real estate (FIRE)**

25. **Legal services**

## Methodological Innovations in Classification

### 1. NAICS vs. SIC Improvement

**Old SIC System** (used by Shaikh & Tonak):
- **Trade sector** (codes 50-59): Lumped together
  - Wholesale
  - Retail
  - **Eating & Drinking** (code 58) hidden
- **Business Services**: Lumped together
  - Management consulting
  - **Computer services** hidden
  - Advertising, etc.

**Problems**:
- Productive activities (E&D, computers) buried in unproductive sectors
- Lack of detailed data forced heroic assumptions
- Moseley (1985): Assumed 50% of trade workers productive (arbitrary)
- S&T: Treated entire trade as unproductive (pragmatic but inaccurate)

**New NAICS System** (used by PT):
- **1,170 industries** vs. 1,004 in SIC
- **Eating & Drinking**: Separate industry
- **Computer services**: Multiple detailed codes (5415, 5112, 5142, 334611)
- **Retrospective data**: Available back to 1960s

### 2. Impact on Productive Employment Estimates

**Text quote** (page 220):
> "It is important to note that the deviation of the two measures of productive employment increases with the passage of time. Thus, the minimum deviation is 6.3 percent observed in 1964, and the maximum deviation is 20.2 percent in 1989."

**PT productive employment vs. ST**:
- **Average**: 13.3% higher
- **Range**: 6.3% (1964) to 20.2% (1989)
- **Why**: E&D and computers reclassified from unproductive to productive

### 3. Impact on Variable Capital (V)

**For productive services sector (1987)**:
> "In the year 1987, for instance, we found that ST's variable capital of the total productive services sector was higher than ours by 43.1 percent, without accounting for the eating and drinking places as well as the computer related industries."

**ST overestimation due to**:
- Assumed uniform wage across productive services
- Reality: Wage heterogeneity substantial
- PT: Industry-level wage data shows lower average V

**Impact on s/v**:
- ST underestimated S (lower MVA)
- ST overestimated V (higher assumed wages)
- Result: ST understated rate of surplus value
- PT corrections: Higher s/v estimates

### 4. Eating & Drinking Places Detail

**Growth as share of trade**:
- 1964: 9.6% of total trade sector value added
- 2007: 13.2% of total trade sector value added
- **38% relative increase** in importance

**Employment impact**:
- Large sector: Millions of workers
- Reclassifying from unproductive → productive significantly changes aggregates
- Lowers unproductive share of total employment

### 5. Computer Services Detail

**Approximation** (page 230-231):
> "Because of the changes in the classifications, the old SIC industry Computer Data and Processing Services is approximated almost 100 percent by the following industries and subindustries in the new system: Computer System Design & Related Services (NAICS code 5415); Software Publishers (NAICS code 5112); Data Processing Services (NAICS code 5142); and the subindustry Software Reproducing (NAICS code 334611)."

**Growth as share of business services**:
- 1977: 10.8% of business/unproductive services GDP
- 2002: 18.1% of reconstituted sector
- **68% relative increase**

**Method**:
- Used benchmark Input-Output tables (1997, 2002)
- Estimated shares from detailed I-O data
- Interpolated and extrapolated for other years

## Theoretical Consistency

### Marx's Criteria

**Productive labor is** (from page 214-215):
1. Employed in sphere of production
2. Creates use-values (new products/services)
3. Hired by capital as wage labor
4. Creates more value than paid in wages

**Unproductive labor is**:
1. Employed in sphere of circulation or social maintenance
2. Changes ownership/protects property, doesn't create use-values
3. Consumes surplus value rather than creating it

### Application to Services

**Not goods vs. services distinction** (page 215):
> "First of all, the definition of productive-unproductive labor has nothing to do with orthodox economics' distinction between goods and services, whose difference is really about the time of consumption or use."

**Examples**:

**Productive services**:
- Transportation (completes production process)
- Eating & Drinking (food preparation)
- Motion pictures (when capitalist)
- Computer programming (creates software)

**Unproductive services**:
- Advertising (circulation)
- Legal services (property protection)
- Financial services (circulation of money capital)

### Military Goods Example

**Not about usefulness or type of product** (page 215):
> "Furthermore, in the classification of productive labor it does not matter what type of goods is produced, that is necessities or luxuries, civilian or military goods, basic or non-basic goods."

**Military production**:
- Manufacturing weapons = PRODUCTIVE
- Using weapons (military) = UNPRODUCTIVE (social maintenance)
- Rationale: Production creates use-values; military consumes them for social order maintenance

## Comparison to Other Studies

### Moseley (1985) Method

**For trade sector**:
> "For example, Moseley (1985) approximated the number of productive employees in Wholesale and Retail Trade by stipulating that fifty percent of non-supervisory employees employed in the trade sector were productive."

**Problems**:
- Arbitrary 50% assumption
- No theoretical or empirical basis
- Simplistic

### Shaikh & Tonak (1994) Method

**For trade sector** (page 229-230):
> "On the other hand, ST in principle would agree that some productive labor is employed in the trade sector and in other generally non-productive sectors of the economy; nevertheless, due to the lack of data and also because the results do not change qualitatively and for all practical purposes, they treated this sector wholly as unproductive."

**Rationale**:
- Theoretically aware of productive component
- But data unavailable (SIC limitations)
- Pragmatic decision: Treat as fully unproductive
- Argued: Doesn't change qualitative results

### Mohun (2005, 2006) Method

**Footnote 4** (page 221):
> "Our differences with Mohun's (2005, 2006) estimates are that we count as part of productive activities the industries Eating and Drinking Places and Computer Data and Processing Services."

**Also**:
- Mohun excludes depreciation and intermediate inputs of trade
- Mohun excludes royalties from productive to FIRE/services
- Result: Mohun's MVA = Net GDP - general govt wages - imputations
- More restrictive definition than PT
