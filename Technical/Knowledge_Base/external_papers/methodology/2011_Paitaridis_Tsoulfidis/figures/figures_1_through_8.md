**Location**: Page 218 (page 6 of PDF)

### Visual Description

**Graph Type**: Dual line graph with Y-axis ranging from 0.42 to 0.67

**Two Lines**:
1. **Share of unproductive to total wages** (upper line, diamond markers)
   - Color: Red/dark (in original)
   - Pattern: Rising persistently throughout period

2. **Share of unproductive to total employment** (lower line, circle markers)
   - Color: Green/lighter (in original)
   - Pattern: Rising until late 1980s, then plateaus

### Data Patterns

**Unproductive Wages Share** (upper line):
- 1963: ~0.51 (51%)
- 1975: ~0.53 (53%)
- 1981: ~0.57 (57%)
- 1990: ~0.62 (62%)
- 2000: ~0.64 (64%)
- 2007: ~0.66 (66%)
- **Overall trend**: Continuous, persistent rise from 51% to 66%

**Unproductive Employment Share** (lower line):
- 1963: ~0.43 (43%)
- 1975: ~0.46 (46%)
- 1987: ~0.48 (48%)
- 1990-2007: Plateaus at ~0.49-0.50 (49-50%)
- **Overall trend**: Rises until late 1980s, then stabilizes

### Key Findings

**Divergence Between Lines**:
- Gap in 1963: ~8 percentage points
- Gap in 2007: ~16 percentage points
- **Interpretation**: Average unproductive wage rose faster than productive wage

**Information Technology Effect** (page 218 text):
> "It seems that the information technologies of the 1980s slowed down the growth rate of unproductive employment but not its wage share, which kept rising, indicating that the average wage of unproductive workers not only is higher than that of productive workers but increased furthermore relative to that of productive workers."

**Critical Thresholds**:
- By 2007: Unproductive wages = 66% of total wages
- By 2007: Unproductive employment = 50% of total employment

### Interpretation

**Structural Shift**:
1. Unproductive activities consume growing share of economy
2. Unproductive workers earn higher average wages than productive workers
3. Technology reduced need for unproductive workers but not their wage premium
4. By 2007, half of all workers are unproductive

**Implications for Growth**:
- Less surplus value available for productive investment
- Growing burden on productive sectors
- Consistent with "phase-change" hypothesis

## Figure 2: Surplus Value, Net Profits, and Unproductive Activities

**Location**: Page 219 (page 7 of PDF)
**Time Period**: 1963-2007
**Variables Plotted**: 3 trend lines (billions of dollars, current prices)

### Visual Description

**Graph Type**: Triple line graph showing absolute values

**Y-Axis**: Billions of dollars (current prices)
- Range: 0 to 9000
- Increments: 1500

**X-Axis**: Years
- Range: 1963 to 2007
- All odd years marked

**Three Lines**:
1. **Surplus value** (top line, diamond markers)
2. **Unproductive activities** (middle line, triangle markers)
3. **Net profits** (bottom line, circle markers)

### Data Values (Approximate)

| Year | Surplus Value | Unproductive Activities | Net Profits |
|------|--------------|------------------------|-------------|
| 1963 | ~500 | ~200 | ~250 |
| 1970 | ~900 | ~400 | ~400 |
| 1980 | ~2000 | ~1000 | ~600 |
| 1990 | ~3500 | ~2000 | ~700 |
| 2000 | ~6000 | ~3500 | ~900 |
| 2007 | ~8500 | ~6000 | ~1000 |

### Trend Analysis

**Surplus Value** (S):
- 1963-2007: Grows from ~$500B to ~$8,500B
- Growth factor: ~17x
- Pattern: Smooth, accelerating growth

**Unproductive Activities**:
- 1963-2007: Grows from ~$200B to ~$6,000B
- Growth factor: ~30x
- Pattern: Accelerating, outpaces surplus value growth
- **Share of S**: Rising from ~40% to ~70%

**Net Profits** (π):
- 1963-2007: Grows from ~$250B to ~$1,000B
- Growth factor: ~4x
- Pattern: Slowest growth of three variables
- **Share of S**: Falling from ~50% to ~12%

### Critical Finding

**Scissors Effect**:
- Surplus value grows rapidly (17x)
- Unproductive activities grow even faster (30x), consuming increasing share
- Net profits grow slowest (4x), leaving less for investment

**Text Quote** (page 219):
> "From an inspection of the graph, we conclude the persistent growth of unproductive activities over a period of 43 years."

**Mathematical Relationship**:
```
Surplus Value (S) = Net Profits (π) + Unproductive Activities
```

As unproductive activities rise, net profits are squeezed even as total surplus value expands.

### Implication

**Squeeze on Accumulation**:
- Despite massive growth in surplus value extraction
- Net profits available for investment grow much more slowly
- Unproductive activities absorb ~70% of surplus value by 2007
- This undermines capital accumulation and growth potential

### Data Patterns

**Productivity (ST estimates)**:

- 1965: ~$80,000 (peak growth period)
- 1982: ~$100,000 (trough)

- Pattern: Rising 1947-1965, slowdown 1965-1982, rising again 1982-1989

**Productivity (PT estimates)**:
- 1964: ~$58,000

- Pattern: Similar to ST but shows MORE pronounced slowdown 1965-1982

**Real Wage** (productive workers):
- 1947: ~$28,000

- 1977: ~$36,500 (highest point)
- 1980-2007: Stagnant at ~$34,000-35,000
- **Never returns to 1977 level**

### Growth Rate Analysis

From Table 1 (page 225):

| Period | Productivity PT | Real Wage PT |
|--------|----------------|--------------|
| 1965-1982 | 1.40% | 0.20% |
| 1982-2007 | 1.96% | 0.15% |
| 1964-2007 | 1.76% | 0.26% |

### Critical Findings

**Productivity-Wage Decoupling**:
- **1947-1972**: Productivity and wages rise together
- **1972-2007**: Productivity rises, wages stagnate
- Gap widens dramatically after 1980

**Text Quote** (page 220):
> "Clearly, productivity increases much faster than the real wage; it is important to note that the real wage never returns to the level of the 1970s and the year 1977 is the peak year (slightly higher than the previous peak of 1972) of the entire period."

**1977 Peak Wage**:
- Highest real wage in entire 60-year period
- Never surpassed despite 30 more years of productivity growth
- Represents turning point in wage-productivity relationship

**Productivity Slowdown**:
- **Mid-1960s to early 1980s**: "Silent depression" period
- PT estimates show this more clearly than ST estimates
- AAGR drops from 3.60% (1948-1965) to 1.40% (1965-1982)

**Post-1982 Recovery**:
- Productivity accelerates again: 1.96% AAGR (1982-2007)
- But wages remain stagnant: only 0.15% AAGR
- Growing gap feeds rising rate of surplus value

### Interpretation

**Surplus Value Extraction**:
- Rising productivity + stagnant wages = rising s/v
- Workers produce more value but receive less
- Difference absorbed by capital and unproductive activities

**Comparison ST vs PT**:
- PT estimates average 0.05% lower MVA than ST
- PT estimates 13.3% higher productive employment than ST
- Result: PT productivity estimates slightly lower
- PT estimates show sharper mid-1960s slowdown

### Methodological Differences

**Why PT > ST**:
- ST overestimated variable capital (wages) in productive services sector
- ST assumed uniform wage across productive services (page 223)
- PT uses detailed industry-level wage data
- Result: PT shows lower V, higher S, thus higher s/v

**Example** (page 223):
> "In the year 1987, for instance, we found that ST's variable capital of the total productive services sector was higher than ours by 43.1 percent"

### Interpretation

**Record Exploitation Levels**:
- By 2007, s/v reached ~3.55 - highest in 60-year period
- Workers receive only ~22% of value they produce (1/(1+3.55))
- Capitalists appropriate ~78%

**Driven By**:
1. Rising productivity (Figure 3)
2. Stagnant real wages (Figure 3)
3. Growing unproductive employment with higher wages (Figure 1)

**Text Quote** (page 221):
> "The combination of rapidly growing productivity and the stagnant real wages of productive workers form the ideal conditions for the unprecedented expansion of surplus value, and the rate of surplus value which reached record levels"

### Three Phases Clearly Visible

**Phase 1: 1948-Mid 1960s** (Rising)
- Steady upward slope
- ln(π) rises from 19.1 to 19.8
- Pattern: Smooth growth
- Real profits double over ~18 years

**Phase 2: 1965-Mid 1980s** (STAGNANT)
- **FLAT trend** from ~1965 to ~1984
- ln(π) fluctuates around 19.5-19.8
- Some years: slight rise
- Other years: decline
- **Net effect**: NO growth for ~20 years
- **Text quote** (page 227):
  > "We observe that the mass of real net profits in the U.S. economy stagnated during the period between the mid-1960s to the mid-1980s."

**Phase 3: Mid-1980s to 2007** (Renewed Growth)
- ln(π) rises from ~19.5 (1984) to ~20.6 (2007)
- Pattern: Steady growth resumed
- Real profits triple over ~23 years

### The "Silent Depression"

**Characteristics** (page 227):
> "This time period is known as the 'silent depression' in the sense that the effects of a depression (such as high unemployment and business bankruptcy rates) were not fully unfolded. The reason is that the presence of institutions and, in general, government intervention moderated the adverse effects of the disruption of the accumulation process on employment."

**Why "Silent"**:
- Stagnant profits normally → severe depression
- But: Welfare state institutions cushioned blow
- Result: High unemployment but not 1930s-level catastrophe
- Inflation instead of deflation
- "Stagflation" = stagnant growth + inflation

### Crisis Condition: Stagnant Mass of Profits

**Theoretical Framework** (pages 226-227):

Starting from: π = rC

Taking differences: Δπ = rΔC + CΔr

Dividing by ΔC:
```
Δπ/ΔC = r + (C/ΔC)Δr = r(1 + (Δr/Δ C)(C/r))
```

**Crisis condition**: Δπ/ΔC = 0 (stagnant mass of profits)

This requires:
```
(Δr/Δr)(C/r) = -1
```

Or equivalently:
```
(Δr/r) / (ΔC/C) = -1
```

**Interpretation**:
- Percentage fall in r exactly offsets percentage rise in C
- Additional investment yields NO additional profits
- Investment becomes pointless
- Accumulation grinds to halt

**Text quote** (page 227):
> "The necessary condition is attained when the elasticity of the rate of profit, that is the term (ΔrC/ΔCr), is equal to –1, which is equivalent to saying that the percentage change in the fall in the rate of profit (Δr/r) is exactly matched by a rise in capital accumulation (ΔC/C), a condition requiring a persistently and sufficiently falling rate of profit."

### Empirical Verification

**Figure 8 shows**:
- 1965-1984: Mass of profits stagnant (flat ln trend)
- Crisis condition approximately met for ~20 years
- Economy in prolonged structural crisis
- Only institutional supports prevent collapse

### Post-1984 Pattern

**Renewed Profit Growth**:
- ln(π) rises steadily 1984-2007
- But: Driven by different factors than 1948-1965
- 1948-1965: High r + growing C
- 1984-2007: Low r + rapidly growing C + rising s/v

**Late 2000s Inflection**:
- Growth rate slowing again by 2004-2007
- Suggests onset of new crisis
- Presages 2008 collapse

### Comparison Across Three Depressions

**Text discussion** (pages 228-229):

**1930s Depression**:
- Philosophy: No intervention
- Result: 25% unemployment, severe deflation
- No institutional cushions

**1970s-80s "Silent Depression"**:
- Philosophy: Keynesian welfare state
- Result: High unemployment (~10%) but tolerable, inflation
- Institutional supports prevented full crisis effects
- "Silent" because moderated

**2008- Depression** (implied by late decline):
- Philosophy: Neoliberal (welfare state partially dismantled)
- Stagnant wages, no safety net
- Yet crisis emerges anyway
- Reveals: Problem is systemic, not policy-driven

## Summary: Integration of All Figures

### The Complete Story (1947-2007)

**Structural Transformation**:
1. **Unproductive sector expands** (Figure 1): From 43% to 50% of employment, 51% to 66% of wages
2. **Unproductive activities absorb surplus** (Figure 2): From 40% to 70% of surplus value
3. **Net profits squeezed** (Figure 2): Grow only 4x while S grows 17x

**Labor Exploitation Intensifies**:
4. **Productivity soars** (Figure 3): ~4.5x increase 1947-2007
5. **Real wages stagnate** (Figure 3): Peak in 1977, never recovered
6. **Rate of surplus value explodes** (Figure 4): From ~1.7 to ~3.55

**Capital Accumulation Dynamics**:
7. **Value composition rises** (Figure 5): C/v from ~4.7 to ~7.5 (with 1980s devaluation interruption)
8. **General profit rate cycles** (Figure 6): Peak 1966, trough 1982, recovery to 1997, decline after 2004
9. **Net profit rate collapses** (Figure 7): 10.9% (1966) → 3.8% (1982) → 7.0% (1997) - never recovers
10. **Mass of profits stagnates** (Figure 8): Flat 1965-1984 ("silent depression"), renewed growth 1984-2007

### Phase-Change Pattern

**Golden Age (1948-1965)**:
- High R and r
- Rising productivity and wages together
- Low unproductive burden
- Healthy accumulation

**Silent Depression (1965-1984)**:
- Falling R and catastrophic fall in r
- Productivity slowdown
- Wages stagnate
- Unproductive burden rising
- Mass of profits flat
- Accumulation disrupted

**New Golden Age? (1984-2007)**:
- R recovers to near 1960s levels
- But r stays depressed (only 64% of 1966 peak)
- Productivity accelerates
- But wages completely stagnant
- s/v at record highs
- Unproductive burden at record highs
- Accumulation continues but on weaker foundation

**Onset of New Crisis (2004-2007)**:
- R declining
- r declining
- Productivity growth slowing
- All variables showing patterns similar to mid-1960s
- Presages 2008 collapse

- Longer time series (through 2007 vs. 1989)
- More accurate productive employment estimates
