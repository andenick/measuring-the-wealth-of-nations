## Circuit Formulations from Marx

### 1. Productive Consumption Circuit (Pages 53-54)

**Marx's definition** (Marx, 1976, p. 1047):
> "For instance, if I buy produce from a self-employing worker, artisan, etc., the category does not enter into the discussion because there is not direct exchange between money and labor of any kind, but only between money and produce."

**Circuit for productive labor:**
$$M - C\{LP, MP\} ... P ... C' - M'$$

Where exchange is between **money (as capital)** and **produce (labor-power + means of production)**.

**Circuit for unproductive labor (from revenue):**
$$C - M - C'$$

Where exchange is between **money (as revenue)** and **produce (service or commodity for consumption)**.

**Critical distinction:**
- Productive: $M \rightarrow M'$ (value expansion through surplus-value production)
- Unproductive: $C \rightarrow C'$ (exchange of equivalents, consumption)

### 2. Productive Consumption (PC) - Three Destinations (Page 54)

**Products of productive consumption can enter:**

**a) As means of production (back into PC):**
$$PC \rightarrow \text{Means of production} \rightarrow PC$$

Not 'faux frais de production' - these become **factors of production** and labor is **productive**.

**b) As consumable goods (social unproductive consumption):**
$$PC \rightarrow \text{Social UPC} \rightarrow \text{Social reproduction/maintenance}$$

Subject to unproductive consumption activities - labor becomes **unproductive**.

**c) As personal consumption goods:**
$$PC \rightarrow \text{Personal consumption}$$

No labor activity involved - outside PUPL framework.

### 3. Military Production Classification (Page 55)

**Two distinct classifications:**

**Workers producing weapons:**
> "labor activities in the production of military goods (e.g., weapons) as productive labor activities, since capitalists engaged in such production activities aim for the production and appropriation of surplus-value as much as do other capitalists in other spheres of production."

$$\text{Weapons production} \in \text{Productive labor} \quad \text{(if capitalist production)}$$

$$V_{military\_prod} = \text{Wages of workers producing weapons}$$

$$S_{military\_prod} = \text{Surplus-value from weapons production}$$

**Military personnel consuming weapons:**
> "there should not be any question either in classifying the activities of military personnel who unproductively consume those weapons as unproductive labor activities, since they are outside the circuit of capital and the salaries are paid within the circuit of revenue."

$$\text{Military personnel} \in \text{Unproductive labor}$$

$$W_{military\_personnel} = \text{Part of surplus-value distribution}$$

**Classification equation:**

$$\begin{cases}
\text{Productive} & \text{if: capitalist production of weapons} \\
\text{Unproductive} & \text{if: military consumption of weapons}
\end{cases}$$

### 4. Faux Frais de Production (Pages 54-55)

**Definition:** "False costs of production" - expenditures necessary for capitalism but not creating surplus-value.

**Marx's circuit formulation** (Marx, 1976, p. 1047):
> "For instance, if I buy produce from a self-employing worker, artisan, etc., the category does not enter into the discussion because there is not direct exchange between money and labor of any kind, but only between money and produce."

**When PC products enter social UPC:**
- They are NOT 'faux frais de production'
- They become subject to unproductive labor activities
- Example: State expenditures on weapons (military goods)

**Implication for surplus-value:**

$$S_{available} = S_{total} - \text{Faux frais}$$

Where faux frais include:
- Circulation costs
- State expenditures on unproductive activities
- Military consumption expenditures

### 5. State Expenditures on Military Goods (Page 55)

**Question:** How to classify state purchases of weapons?

**Answer:** Products of PC (productive consumption) but entering social UPC:

$$\text{State military expenditures} = \text{Faux frais de production}$$

**Impact on accumulation:**

$$S_{available\_for\_accumulation} = S - W_{UPL} - \text{State military spending}$$

**Luxury consumption analogy:**
> "In terms of their role in and impact in the accumulation process, such military goods can therefore be viewed as luxury consumption goods."

**Equation:**
$$\text{Military goods consumed by state} \equiv \text{Luxury consumption}$$

Both are:
- Products of productive labor (produced capitalistically)
- Consumed unproductively
- Represent drain on surplus-value available for accumulation

## Critique of "Indirectly Productive Labor" (Pages 56-57)

### Problem with the Concept

**O'Connor, Rowthorn, Gough** propose:
> "state workers (excluding those in the nationalized industries who do not produce an exchange value (a commodity for sale) nor there fore surplus value, may produce crucial elements of constant [capital, for example, transportation facilities] for variable capital, for example health services" (Gough, 1979, p. 161)

**Their classification:**
$$\text{State workers} \in \text{"Reproductive workers"} \quad \text{(because they aid accumulation)}$$

### Tonak's Rejection

**Fundamental objection** (Page 57):
> "Although it is true that some of the state activities are in fact non-production activities, such as distribution and social reproduction and maintenance, the existence of the latter activities hardly justifies the introduction of a new category of 'indirectly productive' labor."

**Why reject this category:**

**The key distinction is surplus-value production** (Page 57):
> "The fundamental significance of the distinction between productive and unproductive labor is its emphasis on 'the dependence of all sectors of the capitalist economy (including the state) upon the production of surplus value'"

**Correct formulation:**

$$\text{All sectors} \Rightarrow \text{Dependent on } S \text{ production}$$

**But dependency ≠ productivity:**

$$\text{Dependent on } S \not\Rightarrow \text{Productive of } S$$

**State workers:**
- Are **dependent** on surplus-value (paid from taxes, which come from S)
- Do NOT **produce** surplus-value
- Therefore: **Unproductive**, not "indirectly productive"

### Impact on Calculations

**If we accepted "indirectly productive":**

$$V = \text{Direct PL wages} + \text{Indirect PL wages} \quad \text{(WRONG)}$$

$$S = \text{Total surplus} - \text{Indirect PL wages} \quad \text{(WRONG)}$$

**Correct formulation (Tonak's position):**

$$V = \text{Only wages of labor producing surplus-value directly}$$

$$S = \text{Total value created} - V = \text{Rent} + \text{Interest} + \text{Profit} + W_{all\_UPL}$$

Where $W_{all\_UPL}$ includes:
- Commercial workers wages
- Financial sector wages
- **All state workers wages**
- Other unproductive workers

### Why This Matters

**Allowing "indirect productivity" would:**

1. **Blur the fundamental distinction**
   - Between creating value and facilitating realization
   - Between production and circulation/maintenance

2. **Inflate variable capital incorrectly**
   $$V_{incorrect} > V_{correct}$$

3. **Understate surplus-value incorrectly**
   $$S_{incorrect} < S_{correct}$$

4. **Distort rate of surplus-value**
   $$e'_{incorrect} = \frac{S_{incorrect}}{V_{incorrect}} < e'_{correct} = \frac{S_{correct}}{V_{correct}}$$

5. **Obscure drain on accumulation**
   - Growing state sector = growing unproductive consumption
   - This should appear as reduction in $S$ available for accumulation
   - Not as increase in $V$ (which would suggest productive investment)
