## KEY THEORETICAL ARGUMENTS

### Two Fundamental Dimensions (Page 30)

**Framework Purpose**: Classification of state revenues and expenditures requires:

1. **Clear criteria for distinguishing PUPL** - Enables classification of wage funds as variable capital vs surplus-value
2. **Situate distributive activities within circuit of capital** - Shows state as subcircuits of general circuit

**Quote** (Page 29):
> "The underlying premises of this thesis is that the classification of the state revenues and expenditures is an essential element of the analysis of the economic activities of the capitalist state."

### A.1. Significance of the Distinction of PUPL (Pages 31-34)

**Core Argument**: PUPL distinction is VITAL for accumulation analysis

**Key Quote** (Page 31, citing Marx 1973, p. 1048):
> "the distinction between PUPL is vital for accumulation since only the exchange for productive labor can satisfy one of the conditions for the reconversion of surplus-value into capital"

**Two Reasons for Significance**:

1. **Theoretical** - Essential for understanding accumulation process:
   - Productive labor produces surplus-value
   - Only exchange of productive labor satisfies necessary (but not sufficient) conditions for reconversion of surplus-value into capital

2. **Practical** - Role in capitalist crisis analysis:
   - Increasing proportion of unproductive labor = reduction in profitable investment
   - Within falling rate of profit framework, **ceteris paribus**, increasing unproductive labor = decreasing effective rate of profit

**Quote from Yaffe (1973, p. 191)**:
> "The fact that increasing a proportion of unproductive labor represents a reduction in profitable investment implies that the distribution of PUPL also plays a major role in the analysis of capitalist crisis."

**Mandel's Position** (Page 32):
> "(a) precise definition of a productive labor under capitalism is not only of theoretical importance. It is also has major implications for social bookkeeping (calculation in value terms of the national income and significantly affects our analysis of social classes and the political conclusions we draw from it" (Mandel, 1981, p. 46)

**State Intervention Analysis** (Page 33):
> "an examination of the limits and contradictory nature of state intervention in capitalist economies requires a conceptual framework based on the PUPL distinction. Only when such a framework is satisfactorily developed, an identification of the specific sources of the state revenues funded from variable capital and surplus-value can be both theoretically and empirically achieved."

### A.2. Reconstruction of the PUPL Distinction (Pages 34-41)

**Problem Statement**: NO general theoretical agreement among Marxists on PUPL distinction

**Confusion Sources** (Page 34):
- Different interpretations of definitional categories (necessary vs unnecessary activities, intrinsic qualities, use-values, physical vs non-physical commodities, goods vs services)
- Conceptual lack of clarity manifests in various suggested criteria
- All presented as "crucial elements for the PUPL distinction"

**Result**: Sets of criteria with "no objective operational content whatsoever"

**Tonak's Position**: Must define PL as:
> "a set of activities within capitalist society which leads to the production of surplus value from the 'standpoint of capital'" (Page 41)

**Critical Methodological Point** (Page 42):

**Two Common Errors** in PUPL literature:

1. **Failure to see Marx's work as continuous process with higher theoretical maturity at each stage** - Instead scholars pick various definitions from different periods (spanning 25+ years) and try to demonstrate contradictions between them, leading to rejection or modernization of Marxist framework

2. **Mistake of perceiving Marx's published and unpublished works as written for publication without distinction in their relative significance** - Leads to treating self-clarification concepts/notes as "almost absolute truths"

**Result**: "various formulations, whose theoretical origins and failures are specifically underlined by Marx, are actually the original versions of the so-called 'original contributions' which have been recently presented in the debate on PUPL" (Page 42)

### Marx's Definitions from Capital

**From Resultat** (Page 45, Marx 1976, p. 1043):
> "Productive labor is exchanged directly for money as capital, i.e. for money which is intrinsically capital, which is destined to function as capital. Thus productive labor is labor which for the worker only reproduces the value of his labor-power as determined beforehand, while as a value-creating activity it valorizes capital and confronts the worker with the values so created and transformed into capital. The specific relationship between objectified and living labor that converts the former into capital also the latter into productive labor."

**From same section** (Page 45, Marx 1976, p. 1038):
> "Since the immediate purpose and the authentic product of capitalist production is surplus-value, labor is only productive, and an exponent of labor-power is only a productive worker, if it or he creates surplus-value directly, i.e. the only productive labor is that which is directly consumed in the course of production for the valorization of capital."

**Definition of UPL** (Page 46, Marx 1976, p. 1041):
> "Whenever labor is purchased to be consumed as a use-value, as a service and not to replace the value of variable capital with its own vitality and be incorporated into the capitalist process of production - whenever that happens, labor is not productive and the wage-laborer is no productive worker."

**Key Insight** (Page 46):
> "these definitions are therefore ... derived ... from the definite social form and the social relations of production within which the labor is realized." (Marx 1963, p. 157)

## TABLE I: Historical Stages of Production (Page 37)

**Transformation of Purpose of Production Across Historical Modes**:

| Historical Stage | Nature of Production Activities |
|-----------------|--------------------------------|
| I. Primitive Communism | Production of Use-Values for Personal and Social Use |
| II. Other Pre-Capitalist Societies | - Production of Use-Values for Personal Use<br>- Production of Values for Exchange |
| III. Capitalism | - Production of Values and Surplus-Values for Exchange<br>- Negligible Production of Use-Values (some farmers, etc.) |

**Key Point** (Page 36):
> "Production, in its more general sense, is the creation or transformation of objective properties of use-values by means of purposeful human activity. Although this is common in all modes of production, the very purpose of production changes over time since 'all production is appropriation of nature on the part of an individual with and through a definite form of society'" (Marx 1973, p. 87)

## TABLE II: Distribution and Social Reproduction (Page 39)

**Nature of Distribution Activities & Social Reproduction & Maintenance Across Historical Stages**:

| Historical Stage | Distribution | Social Reproduction & Maintenance |
|-----------------|-------------|----------------------------------|
| I. Primitive Communism | **Distributions**: There is no considerable distribution activity since there is no production for exchange. | **Social Reproduction and Maintenance**: There is no such activity either since this is a classless and primitive society where "social life" is conflict-free and able to reproduce itself. |
| II. Pre-Capitalist | **Distribution**: There is a distribution of values. Commodities produced for exchange are distributed either by producers themselves or via merchants. | **Social Reproduction and Maintenance**: Reproduction of social relations and their maintenance require institutions such as king, lord, emperor, army, legal system, etc. |
| III. Capitalist Society | **Distribution**: Value distribution, i.e., the distribution of commodities produced and sold by producers (family farming, artisans, etc.)<br><br>**Surplus-value distribution**: The dominant and complex form of distribution | **Social Reproduction and Maintenance**: Army, state, mass transportation, health, education, administration activities, etc. |

## A.3. MARX ON PUPL: THE DEBATED ISSUES (Pages 41-52)

### Adam Smith's Two Definitions

**First Definition** (Page 48, Smith 1976, p. 351) - Emphasizes capitalist social relations:
> "...(T)he labor of a manufacturer adds, generally, to the value of the materials which he works upon, that of his own maintenance, and of his master's profit. The labor of a menial servant, on the contrary, adds to the value of nothing. Though the manufacturer has his wages advanced to him by his master, he, in reality, costs him no expense, the value of those wages being generally restored, together with a profit, in the improved value of the subject upon which his labor is bestowed. But the maintenance of a menial servant never is restored. A man grows rich by employing a multitude of menial servants."

**Second Definition** (Page 48, Smith 1976, pp. 351-2) - Emphasizes intrinsic quality of labor:
> "...(T)he labor of manufacturer fixes and realizes itself in some particular subject of vendible commodity, which lasts for some time at least after that labor is past. It is, as it were, a certain quantity of labor stocked and stored up to be employed, if necessary, upon some other occasion. That subject, or what is the same thing, the price of that subject, can afterwards, if necessary, put into motion a quantity of labor equal to that which had originally produced it. The labor of the menial servant, on the contrary, does not fix or realize itself in any particular subject or vendible commodity. His services generally perish in the very instant of their performance, and seldom leave any trace or value behind them, for which an equal quantity of service could afterwards be procured."

**Marx's Position** (Page 48, Marx 1973, p. 273):
> "Adam Smith was essentially correct with his productive and unproductive labor, correct from the standpoint of bourgeois economy."

**Two Critical Points on Smith's Definitions** (Page 49):

1. **Lack of relationship between intrinsic quality of labor and PUPL distinction** - Second definition focus on material aspects leads to classification of some productive service activities as unproductive

2. **Superfluous attention to material aspect** - Classification of some productive service activities as unproductive

**Marx's Unambiguous Position** (TSV, page 49, Marx 1963, p. 401):
> "...(T)he designation of labor as productive labor has absolutely nothing to do with the determinate content of the labor, its special utility, or the particular use-value in which it manifests itself. The same kind of labor may be productive or unproductive."

### Materiality Issue (Page 49-50)

**Not relevant to PUPL distinction from standpoint of capitalism**

**Marx's Example** (TSV, page 49, Capital Vol I, p. 644):
> "The only worker who is productive is one who produces surplus-value for the capitalist, or in other words contributes towards the self-valorization of capital. If we may take an example from outside the sphere of material production, a schoolmaster is a productive worker when, in addition to belaboring the heads of his pupils, he works himself into the ground to enrich the owner of the school. That the latter has laid out his capital in a teaching factory, instead of a sausage factory, makes no difference to the relation."

**Domestic Labor** (Page 51-53):

**Critical Argument**: PUPL distinction CANNOT properly be employed for analysis of domestic labor without violating Marx's framework

**Reasoning** (Page 52):
- Domestic labor is "production labor in general, but not productive labor from the standpoint of capitalist society"
- No exchange of labor against capital
- No surplus-value produced in household
- Neither necessary conditions for PL satisfied
- Domestic labor uniqueness as commodity (labor-power) exists at particular historical junction (capitalism)
- Nothing to do directly with PUPL division within household or nature of domestic labor activity outside household

**Quote** (Page 52-53):
> "since there is no exchange of labor against capital and no surplus-value is produced in the household, neither of the two necessary conditions for PL is satisfied. Moreover, the uniqueness of labor-power as a commodity which comes into existence at a particular historical junction (capitalism), has nothing to do directly either with the division of labor within the household or with the nature of the domestic labor activity in terms of its position outside the household."

### Commercial and Merchant Activities (Page 50)

**Marx's Position** (1956, p. 134):
> "... If by a division of labor a function, unproductive in itself although a necessary element of reproduction, is transformed from an incidental occupation of many into the exclusive occupation of a few, into their special business, the nature of this function itself has not changed."

**Circulation Sphere Example** (Page 50, Marx 1956, pp. 134-5):
> "(A buying and selling) agent expends his labor-power and labor-time in the operations of C-M and M-C. And he makes his living in that way, just as another does by spinning or by making pills. He performs a necessary function, because the process of reproduction itself includes also unproductive functions. He works as well as another does by spinning or making pills...his labor is involved in the total process that is realized in the product."

### Military Expenditures Classification (Pages 53-55)

**Key Application of PUPL to State Expenditures**

**Three Destinations of Productive Consumption (PC) Output** (Page 54):

1. **Re-enter PC activities** - Expenditures are NOT 'faux frais de production' (false costs of production); labor expended is PL

2. **Enter social unproductive consumption (UPC) activities or personal consumption** - Unproductive; labor activity clearly unproductive (only social UPC involves labor)

3. **Enter unproductive personal consumption** - No labor activity; no classification problems

**Military Goods as "Luxury Consumption"** (Page 55):

**Production of military goods (weapons)**:
- Labor involved = **PRODUCTIVE** (capitalists aim for surplus-value production as in other spheres)

**Personnel consuming weapons (military personnel)**:
- = **UNPRODUCTIVE** (outside circuit of capital; salaries paid within circuit of revenue)

**State expenditure on weapons**:
- = Products of PC activities
- = Part of social UPC activities
- = **faux frais de production** (false costs of production)
- = Reduction in total surplus-value available for productive reinvestment

**Quote** (Page 55):
> "These particular state expenditures are products of PC activities and since they are part of social UPC activities they must be considered as a typical example of faux frais de production. In terms of their role in and impact in the accumulation process, such military goods can therefore be viewed as luxury consumption goods."

- Production = Productive
- Consumption = Unproductive
- Expenditure = Faux frais (reduction of surplus available for reinvestment)

Directly relevant to **NSW government expenditure classification** in current project.

### 3. "Indirectly Productive" Critique

**From Pages 56-57**: Tonak REJECTS O'Connor and Rowthorn's "indirectly productive labor" concept:

> "Although it is true that some of the state activities are in fact non-production activities, such as distribution and social reproduction and maintenance, the existence of the latter activities hardly justifies the introduction of a new category of 'indirectly productive' labor."

**Fundamental significance** (Page 57, citing Fine and Harris 1979, p. 130):
> "The fundamental significance of the distinction between productive and unproductive labor is its emphasis on 'the dependence of all sectors of the capitalist economy (including the state) upon the production of surplus value'"

**Implication**: State non-productive expenditures are "ultimately a drain on surplus value" - emphasizes **impact** rather than creating new intermediate categories.
