## Theoretical Foundation for Future Tables

While no tables appear in this chunk, the literature review establishes the **conceptual framework** that will structure all empirical tables in later chapters.

### 1. Critique of Existing Approaches - Implications for Table Design

#### A. Neo-Ricardian Approach (Page 18)

**Problem identified:**
> "LTV concepts are used, but superficially (for instance, profit-wage ratio is equalized to rate of surplus-value)"

**Implication for tables:**
- Future tables must **distinguish** between:
  - Distribution categories (profit, wages, rent)
  - Value categories (surplus-value, variable capital, constant capital)
- Cannot simply use NIPA profit/wage ratios as proxies for Marxian categories

**Tables needed** (Chapter IV):
- Labor share calculations (distinguishing productive/unproductive)
- Surplus-value calculations (not just profit margins)
- Tax incidence by **class**, not just by income bracket

#### B. Fiscal Crisis Approach (Page 18)

**Problem identified:**
> "contradiction between the state's role in the process of capital accumulation, and its actions towards its own legitimization; however, the limits of the state's economic functions are not discussed effectively"

**Implication for tables:**
- Need time-series data showing **trends** in:
  - State expenditures by function (accumulation vs. legitimization)
  - Tax revenues by source
  - Deficit/debt accumulation
- Must show **limits** empirically through fiscal constraints

**Tables needed** (Chapter IV):
- Government expenditures by economic function (1952-1980)
- Tax revenue trends showing limits to extraction
- Deficit financing patterns

#### C. Capital-Logic School (Page 19)

**Problem identified:**
> "views of the capital-logic school on the limitations of state intervention are also confused...to understand this relation, it was seen to be necessary to analyze the source of the particularization (Besonderung)"

**Implication for tables:**
- Empirical data must ground the **philosophical concept** of Besonderung
- Show concretely how state is both **part of** and **separate from** capital accumulation

**Tables needed:**
- State productive vs. unproductive activities
- State purchases from private sector (integration)
- State's own revenue base (separation)

### 2. Gough's Framework - Table Structure Template

#### Gough's "Political Economy of Welfare State" (Page 20)

Tonak notes that Gough:
> "attempts to...corporate the positive features of each approach"

**Three key elements for tables:**

1. **Role of working-class struggle** (Page 22)
   - Tables must show how state transfers respond to class struggle
   - Need historical context markers (union density, strike activity periods)

2. **Representative democracy features** (Page 22)
   - Electoral cycles may affect spending patterns
   - Tables could include political control variables

3. **Capitalist system constraints** (Page 22)
   - Despite struggles, state remains constrained by accumulation needs
   - Tables must show these constraints (revenue limits, debt ceilings)

**Gough's approach will be critiqued in Chapter V** - but the table structure acknowledges these elements.

### 3. Gintis & Bowles - "Citizen Wage" Concept to Test

#### Two Propositions (Page 23)

**a) State as response to accumulation logic:**
> "the logic of accumulation is set by the character of capitalist social relations alone, the role of the state being simply a response to the pattern of growth and to the crisis generated by the capitalist mode of production"

**Tables needed to test:**
- Correlation between accumulation crises and state intervention
- Response lags in state spending to economic downturns
- Counter-cyclical patterns (or lack thereof)

**b) State as cohesion factor:**
> "the state is a factor of cohesion in the social formation, its primary function being to reproduce the social relations of production"

**Tables needed to test:**
- Net transfer by class (does state reproduce or reduce inequality?)
- Education/training expenditures (reproducing labor force)
- Social insurance (maintaining reserve army)

#### "Citizen Wage" Empirical Test (Page 26)

> "the concept of 'citizen wage' will be critically dealt with in Chapter V"

**Tables needed** (Chapter V will use Chapter IV data):
- All government transfers to individuals
- All taxes paid by individuals
- **Net = Transfers - Taxes** (the "citizen wage" claim)
- Comparison with private wages to test if it's truly a "wage"

### 4. Instrumentalist Critique - Implications for Classification

#### Miliband and Domhoff (Page 24)

Criticized for assuming:
> "political influence of the economic elite which nullifies the poser of a mass electorate"

**Implication for tables:**
- Do **not** simply assume all state activity benefits capital
- Must **empirically calculate** which expenditures benefit labor vs. capital
- Tax incidence must be **calculated**, not assumed based on nominal taxpayer

**Tables needed:**
- Government purchases by sector (who sells to government?)
- Military spending (capital subsidy or labor employment?)
- Infrastructure spending (benefits capital accumulation or workers?)

### 5. Structuralist Critique - Table Methodology

#### Poulantzas and Therborn (Page 24)

Key contribution:
> "independence of investment decision from state control" and "dependence of a popularly elected government on high levels of growth and unemployment for its continuance in power"

**Implication for tables:**
- State expenditures are **constrained** by private investment decisions
- Revenue base depends on **growth** of private accumulation

**Tables needed:**
- State revenue as % of GNP (shows dependency)
- Tax revenue elasticity to growth
- Expenditure composition changes during recessions vs. expansions

## Summary: From Theory to Tables

This chunk's literature review establishes that empirical tables must:

1. **Distinguish** Marxian value categories from conventional distribution categories
2. **Track time series** (1952-1980) to show trends, limits, contradictions
3. **Allocate by class** (labor vs. capital) both taxes and benefits
4. **Calculate net transfers** to test "welfare state" and "social wage" claims
5. **Show constraints** on state autonomy through fiscal data
6. **Ground philosophical concepts** (Besonderung, relative autonomy) in empirical measurements
