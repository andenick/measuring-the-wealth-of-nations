## CRITICAL THEORETICAL CONTRIBUTIONS

### 1. Circuit of Capital Framework (Chapter III.B, Pages 60-62)

**State as Subcircuits**: Tonak presents state distributive activities as subcircuits within general circuit of capital.

**Two Distinct Processes**:

**Private Circuit**:
```
M_p - C_p(LP_p, MP_p) ... P_p ... C_p - M'_p
where M'_s + M'_p = M'
```

**State Circuit**:
```
M_s - C_s(LP_s, MP_s) ... P_s ... C_s - M'_s
```

**Key Notation**:
- (p) = private sector
- (s) = state sector
- M' = total gross money form of value = M'_p + M'_s
- Taxable components: Variable capital (V) and Surplus-value (S)

**Effective Variable Capital** (Page 61):
- State collects taxes (V_t) from nominal variable capital (V_nom)
- State supplies services/transfers (V_st)
- **V_eff = V_nom - (V_t - V_st)**
- Effective variable capital can be smaller OR larger than nominal

**Surplus-Value Circuit** (Page 61):
- Part retained as profit
- Part transferred as (S_up) to distributive sectors
- Diagrammatic representation shows C'_up, W_nom, S'_up flows
- Final equation: S_up - (C'_up + W_nom) = S_upπ

**Net-Tax Definition** (Page 62):
- **Net-tax paid by labor** = (V_t - V_st) + (W_t - W_st)
  - Productive workers: (V_t - V_st)
  - Unproductive workers: (W_t - W_st)
- **Net-tax paid by capitalists** = (S_t - S_st)

### 2. Rejection of "Indirectly Productive" Labor (Pages 56-59)

**O'Connor & Rowthorn's Position**: State activities that reduce circulation time might "indirectly increase production of surplus value"

**Tonak's Critique** (Page 58):
> "Within our framework we consider all expenditures of labor in non-production activities of the state as unproductive labor while some of such activities represent **faux frais de production** (administrative, militaristic, etc.) and others consumption of workers and capitalists."

**Quote from Rose (1977, p. 35)** cited on page 58:
> "since these activities are **necessary** to keep workers alive, they are a component of the value of labor power"

**Tonak's Response**: "The fact that such activities are necessary does not make, contrary to Rowthorn's claim, 'state employees...[having] much in common with productive workers'" (Page 58)

**Fundamental Issue** (Page 59): Marx's focus was on "direct appropriation of the surplus labor by the capitalist class in a value production process" - explicitly excludes activities in sphere of distribution

## CHAPTER IV: CORE EMPIRICAL FRAMEWORK

### Figure I: Net National Product and Income in NIPA (Page 78)

**Six-Phase Derivation of Net-Tax**:

**Phase I**: Net National Product divided into:
- Gross Labor Income (GLI)
- Gross Non-Labor Income (GNLI)

**Phase II**: Taxes Paid by Labor (LT) and Non-Labor (NLT)
- Allocation based on labor share calculations

**Phase III**: After-Tax Income
- GLI - LT = Labor After-Tax Income (LATI)
- GNLI - NLT = Non-Labor After-Tax Income (NLATI)

**Phase IV**: Government Expenditures allocated to labor/non-labor based on content

**Phase V**: Benefits & Income received calculated
- Labor receives portion
- Non-labor receives portion

**Phase VI**: **NET-TAX CONCEPT**
- Net-tax for labor = Taxes - Benefits
- Net-tax for non-labor = Taxes - Benefits

**Property Taxes** (Page 86):

### Labor Share Calculation (Pages 90-94)

**Method** (Page 90): "ratio of total labor income to adjusted personal income, which I term **labor share**, is used to determine the portion of income taxes paid by workers."

**Formula**:
- Labor Share = (Total Labor Income) / (Adjusted Personal Income)
- Adjusted Personal Income = Total Personal Income MINUS Imputed Income

**Table II**: Calculation of Labor Share, 1952-80 (Page 92)
- **1952**: Labor Share = 0.73 (73%)
- **1960**: Labor Share = 0.73
- **1970**: Labor Share = 0.74
- **1980**: Labor Share = 0.71

**Validation** (Table III, Page 93): Comparison with Weisskopf's estimates
- Tonak's estimates "considerably lower than the latter except the first five-year period, 1952-1956"
- **No overestimation** of taxes paid by labor

**Household Income Check** (Page 94):
- 1980 taxable returns: income up to $49,999 = 68.9% of total federal income taxes
- Tonak's figure: 71% based on labor share
- **Conclusion**: "seems perfectly adequate, and certainly does not suggest any overestimation of the tax share paid by the working class"

6. Economic development, regulation, and services - excluded (mainly for small businesses)
7. Labor training and services - entirely received by workers
8. Postal service, Energy, Natural resources, Recreational and cultural activities - "social consumption in general" allocated by labor share

**Marx Quote on Faux Frais** (Page 106):
> "The sum of labor-power and social means of production that is spent in the annual production of gold and silver as instruments of circulation forms a heavy item for **faux frais** for the capitalist mode of production, or more generally for a mode of production based on commodity circulation. It withdraws from social use a corresponding sum of possible additional means of production and consumption, i.e. of real wealth. To the extent that the costs of this expensive machinery of circulation are reduced, with the scale of production remaining the same, i.e. at a given level of its extension, the productive forces of social labor are correspondingly heightened." (Marx, 1893, p. 420)
