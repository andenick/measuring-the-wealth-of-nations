## Methodological Context

While no tables appear in this chunk, the text establishes the **conceptual framework** that will structure the empirical tables in later chapters:

### 1. Classification Categories Established (Page 14)

The dissertation will classify state activities into categories that will become table columns:

**State Revenues:**
- Taxes (by type: income, sales, property, etc.)
- Public debt
- Public credit

**State Expenditures:**
- By government function
- By class beneficiary (labor vs. capital)

**Labor Categories:**
- Productive labor
- Unproductive labor

### 2. Key Empirical Questions (Implicit in Chapter II)

These questions will be answered through tables in Chapter IV:

1. **What portion of taxes are paid by labor vs. capital?**
   - Will require: Tax incidence tables (see Chapter IV, Table II et al.)

2. **What portion of government expenditures benefit labor vs. capital?**
   - Will require: Expenditure allocation tables (see Chapter IV)

3. **What is the net fiscal impact on labor?**
   - Will require: Net tax calculations (Taxes paid - Benefits received)
   - **Key result:** Table X (page 113) - Net Tax Paid by Labor 1952-1980

4. **How does state activity affect the rate of surplus-value?**
   - Will require: Adjusted surplus-value calculations (see Table XIII, page 120)

### 3. Theoretical Debates Requiring Empirical Evidence

**The "Welfare State Myth" Debate** (Referenced throughout):

Future tables will test claims about:
- Whether the welfare state redistributes from capital to labor
- The net fiscal burden on the working class
- The evolution of class tax burden over time (1952-1980)

**The "Social Wage" Concept** (Chapter V focus):

Tables will quantify:
- Government expenditures benefiting labor
- Comparison with taxes paid by labor
- Whether "social wage" reduces surplus-value or not

### 4. Methodological Debates Addressed

**Keynesian vs. Marxist Approaches** (Pages 8-9):

The dissertation critiques Keynesian approaches that ignore:
- Class composition of tax burden
- Class distribution of benefits
- Impact on surplus-value and accumulation

Future tables will operationalize the **Marxist alternative** using labor theory of value categories.

## Significance for Overall Analysis

This chunk establishes that the state's economic role must be analyzed **empirically** using labor theory of value categories, not just **theoretically** or **politically**.

The absence of tables here is deliberate - Tonak first:
1. Critiques existing approaches (Keynesian, political/sociological)
2. Establishes Marx's methodological evolution on the state
3. Reviews Marxist theoretical debates
4. **Then** (in subsequent chapters) provides empirical evidence

This mirrors Marx's own method in Capital: theoretical development before empirical application.
