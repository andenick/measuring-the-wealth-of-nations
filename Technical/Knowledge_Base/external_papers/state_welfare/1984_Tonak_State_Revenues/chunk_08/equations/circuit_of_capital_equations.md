# Circuit of Capital Equations - State Distributive Activities

**Source**: 1984 Tonak Dissertation, Chunk_08, Pages 60-62
**Section**: B. State Distributive Activities As Subcircuits of the Circuit of Capital

## Private Circuit of Capital

```
        LP_p
       /
M_p - C_p        ....P_p.....C_p' - M'_p
       \
        MP_p
                              M'_s + M'_p = M'
```

**Where**:
- `M_p` = Money capital (private sector)
- `C_p` = Commodity capital (private)
- `LP_p` = Labor power (private)
- `MP_p` = Means of production (private)
- `P_p` = Production process (private)
- `C_p'` = Commodity capital after production (private)
- `M'_p` = Expanded money capital (private)

---

## State Circuit of Capital

```
        LP_s
       /
M_s - C_s        ....P_s.....C_s' - M'_s
       \
        MP_s
```

**Where**:
- `M_s` = Money capital (state sector)
- `C_s` = Commodity capital (state)
- `LP_s` = Labor power (state)
- `MP_s` = Means of production (state)
- `P_s` = Production process (state)
- `C_s'` = Commodity capital after production (state)
- `M'_s` = Expanded money capital (state)

---

## Total Money Form of Value

At the aggregate level, the economy's **total gross money form of value M' is given by**:

```latex
M' = M'_p + M'_s
```

**Taxable components** of this total value are only:
- **Variable capital (V)**: Wages of productive workers
- **Surplus-value (S)**: Profit, rent, interest

**Because**: Constant capital (C) is value that has been previously produced, materialized and transferred. It is not part of newly created value subject to taxation.

---

## Fundamental Marxist Decomposition

```latex
M'_p + M'_s = M' = {(C) nom, (V) nom, (S) nom}
```

**Where** `(nom)` denotes nominal value.

**This means**: The total value created can be decomposed into:
- **C (nom)**: Constant capital (value transferred from means of production)
- **V (nom)**: Variable capital (wages paid to productive workers)
- **S (nom)**: Surplus-value (value appropriated by capitalists)

---

## State Taxation and Redistribution

### Stage 1: State Collects Taxes

**At this stage of analysis of the distributive activities of the state**, it is assumed that the state collects some taxes:

- **V_L**: Out of nominal variable capital (V_nom)
- **V_st**: Amount of services and transfer payments (supplied by state)

**Variable Capital Adjustment**:

After incorporating this transaction, **(V_L - V_st)**, **(V_nom)** becomes the **effective variable capital (V_eff)**:

```latex
V_eff = V_nom - (V_L - V_st)
```

**Interpretation**:
- If `(V_L - V_st) > 0`: State is a net tax collector from labor → V_eff < V_nom
- If `(V_L - V_st) < 0`: State is a net transfer provider to labor → V_eff > V_nom
- If `(V_L - V_st) = 0`: No net impact on variable capital

**Key Insight**: Clearly, effective variable capital can be **smaller or larger** than nominal variable capital, depending upon whether `(V_L - V_st)` is larger or smaller than zero.

---

## Surplus-Value Division and Redistribution

### Stage 2: Surplus-Value Partitioning

At the next stage, a part of surplus-value already produced is retained as profit and the rest denoted by **(S_up)** transferred to the distributive sectors.

**Diagrammatic representation** (page 61):

```
             /  C'_up
            /   W_nom
LP_up .....S'_up .....
       \           \
        MP_up       S'_upπ ____
                             S'_up - (C'_up + W_nom) = S'_upπ
(S_nom - S'_up)...............(S_nom - S'_up)
```

**Where**:
- `S'_up` = Surplus-value transferred to unproductive capital
- `C'_up` = Constant capital in unproductive sector
- `W_nom` = Nominal wages of unproductive workers
- `S'_upπ` = Profit in unproductive sector (redistributed surplus-value)
- `(S_nom - S'_up)` = Surplus-value retained in productive sector

**Note**: At the starting point of the unproductive capital circuit, the amount of value was **(S'_up)**, which equals the amount ultimately absorbed in unproductive activity.

**The whole purpose of this particular circuit from the unproductive capitalist point of view is then to appropriate (S_upπ)**, the profit made in these unproductive branches, as redistributed surplus-value.

---

## State Taxation of Surplus-Value

Now the state can again collect taxes out of this final portion of surplus-value which ends up as profits of productive and unproductive sectors:

```latex
S'_up - (C' + W_nom)
```

**State collects**:
- **S_L**: Taxes on profit
- **S_st**: Benefits, subsidies, etc. supplied to capitalists

This in turn transforms **(S'_nom)** into **effective surplus-value**:

```latex
S_eff = S'_nom - (S_L - S_st)
```

**The only component left untaxed at this level of abstraction is** the portion of surplus-value which takes the form of **unproductive workers' wages, W_nom**.

This component will also be taxed and benefit from state expenditures, thus being transformed into **effective wages of unproductive workers**:

```latex
W_eff = W_nom - (W_L - W_st)
```

---

## Net Tax Definition

Based on the above discussion, **net-tax paid by labor can be defined as**:

**Net taxes paid by productive workers**:
```latex
(V_L - V_st)
```

**Plus** those paid by unproductive workers:
```latex
(W_L - W_st)
```

**Total Net Tax Paid by Labor**:
```latex
Net Tax_{Labor} = (V_L - V_st) + (W_L - W_st)
```

**Similarly, net taxes paid by capitalists** (or non-labor segments) become equal to:
```latex
Net Tax_{Capital} = (S_L - S_st)
```

## Significance for NSW Analysis

### Key Insights

1. **Variable Capital Adjustment**: State taxes/benefits directly affect effective variable capital
   - This changes the wage base for calculating exploitation rate
   - Critical for our Phase 1 NSW calculations

2. **Surplus-Value Redistribution**: State participates in redistribution of already-produced surplus-value
   - Not just labor vs. capital
   - Also productive vs. unproductive sectors

3. **Net Tax Concept**: This is the **foundational formula** that becomes Net Social Wage
   - Tonak 1984: `(V_L - V_st) + (W_L - W_st)`
   - Our Phase 1: `(T1 + T2×LS) - (E1 + E2×LS)`
   - Conceptually identical, different notation

4. **Material Cost Escapes Value Added**: Unproductive activities' material costs (C') escape value added
   - While wages of unproductive workers remain within taxable base
   - This asymmetry is important for classifying state expenditures

## Connection to Our NSW Framework

### Mapping Tonak's Notation to Our Notation

| Tonak 1984 | Our Phase 1 | Meaning |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| `V_L` | Portion of T1 | Direct taxes from labor |
| `V_st` | Portion of E1 | Direct benefits to labor |
| `W_L` | Portion of T1 | Taxes from unproductive workers |
| `W_st` | Portion of E1 | Benefits to unproductive workers |
| `(V_L - V_st)` | Net Tax (productive) | NSW for productive workers |
| `(W_L - W_st)` | Net Tax (unproductive) | NSW for unproductive workers |
| Total | `(T1 + T2×LS) - (E1 + E2×LS)` | Total NSW |

**Key Difference**:
- Tonak explicitly separates productive (V) and unproductive (W) workers
- Our Phase 1 aggregates all labor, uses Labor Share (LS) for mixed items
- Both approaches measure same underlying phenomenon
