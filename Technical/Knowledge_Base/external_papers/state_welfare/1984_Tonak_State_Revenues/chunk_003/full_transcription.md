## CHAPTER II: A. 'PLAN PROBLEM' AND SOME METHODOLOGICAL REMARKS

### The Plan Problem and Marx's Treatment of the State (Pages 8-14)

**Context**: Tonak argues that contemporary studies of the capitalist state suffer from confusion about where the state fits within Marxist economic theory. This confusion stems from failure to resolve the **'plan problem'** - the relationship between Marx's various plans and outlines for his critical theory.

**Marx's Pre-Capital State Analysis** (Page 8):
- Marx's writings on state mostly date from **pre-Capital** period
- Grundrisse (1857): Marx's initial association with Hegel's concept of institutionally-conceived civil society
- In this period, Marx characterized state as "political institution of civil society"
- Tonak is NOT examining political dimension of capitalist state in this study
- Has "serious reservations about any modern political analysis of the state which does not take account of the economic concerns of the post-Grundrisse (or the mature) Marx"

**The 'Plan Problem' Defined** (Page 9):
> "The 'plan problem' is the name given to the debate on the relationship between the various plans and outlines Marx drafted at different times and his actual writings on economic theory."

**Importance**: For scholars adopting Marxist paradigm, the debate centers on:
- Contents of Marx's various plans
- Extent to which Marx's finished and unfinished works (especially **Capital**) accomplish tasks set by the plans

**Marx's Plans for the State** (Pages 9-10):

**First Plan** - Contained in draft 'Preface' for Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts (Paris Manuscripts) (Oakley 1983, p. 28; Marx and Engels 1975, p. 232):
- First work in evolution of Marx's critical theory was Deutsch-Französische Jahrbücher article "Critique of Hegel's Philosophy of Right: Introduction"
- Marx's interest in the state precedes "first plan" and goes back to 1843-1844 (Oakley 1983, p. 29)

**"Draft Plan for a Work on the Modern State"** (November 1844) - **Most Detailed Outline**:

```
"1) The history of the origin of the modern state or the French Revolution. The self-conceit of the political sphere - to mistake itself for the ancient state. The attitude of the revolutionaries towards civil society. All elements exist in duplicate form, as civic elements and (those of) the state.

2) The proclamation of the rights of man and the constitution of the state. Individual freedom and public authority. Freedom, equality and unity. Sovereignty of the people.

3) State and civil society.

4) The representative state and the charter. The constitutional representative state, the democratic representative state.

5) Division of power. Legislative and executive power.

6) Legislative power and the legislative bodies. Political clubs.

7) Executive power. Centralization and hierarchy. Centralization and political civilization. Federal system and industrialism. State administration and local government.

8) Judicial power and law.

8") Nationality and the people.

9') The political parties.

9") Suffrage, the fight for the abolition of the state and of bourgeois society." (Marx and Engels 1975, p. 666)
```

**Analysis** (Page 10): "Marx's emphasis was on the history and politics of the modern state within the perspective of civil society and there was no 'economics' whatsoever."

**Grundrisse Plans** (Pages 11-13):

**First Grundrisse Plan** (September 1857) - Included state as third item:
- "Concentration of bourgeois society in the form of the state - viewed in relation to itself. The 'unproductive' classes. Taxes. State debt. Public credit. The population. The colonies. Emigration." (Marx 1973, p. 108)

**Second Grundrisse Plan** (October 1857):
- Contains item about state but no indication of topics Marx was planning to cover
- State treatment "still between the analysis of the 'internal structure of production' (or 'inner structure of bourgeois society')" and "'international relations'" (Marx 1973, p. 227)

**Third Grundrisse Plan** (November 1857) - Essentially same as first, modified version treating state "domestically and then 'the state externally'" in context of world economy
- Section elements: "State and bourgeois society'; 'Taxes or the existence of the unproductive classes'; 'The state debt'; and 'Population'" (Marx 1973, p. 264)
- Letter to Lasalle (February 22, 1858): Marx mentions plans for six books, **sixth exclusively on the state**

**1854 Contribution Preface** (Page 13):

When Marx formulated materialist problematic in 1854, most concise form in the **Contribution**:

> "The general result at which I arrived and which, once won, served as a guiding thread for my studies, can be briefly formulated as follows:
> In the social production of their life, men enter into definite relations that are indispensable and independent of their will, relations of production which correspond to a definite stage of development of their material productive forces. The sum total of these relations of production constitutes the economic structure of society, the real basis, on which rises a legal and political superstructure, and to which correspond definite forms of social consciousness. The mode of production of material life conditions the social, political and intellectual life process in general" (Marx 1969, p. 503).

**Methodological Break** (Pages 13-14):

Tonak argues this represents a **radical break from Hegelian problematic**:
- Distinction between "civil society" and "state" is NO LONGER the main premise
- Marx's new materialistic formulation emphasizes **primacy of economics**
- Even institutions (mainly superstructural) have determining relations with economy
- **Methodological change** in Marx's conception of capitalist society

**Implication**: Treatment of capitalist state perceived by Marx **increasingly as an economic topic**

**Guidelines for State Analysis** (Page 14):
- Mentions elements: 'taxes', 'productive and unproductive labor', 'public debt', 'public credit'
- Clearly indicate need to **conceptualize state's activities by dealing with taxation and various expenditures** using **labor theory of value**
- "This is our starting point in formulation the questions raised in this dissertation"

## B. LITERATURE REVIEW (Pages 15-17)

### Overview (Page 15)

**Two-Part Structure**:
1. **General review** of Marxist theories of capitalist state - situate analysis within ongoing debate
2. **Specific focus** on Ian Gough, Herbert Gintis, Sam Bowles - their "social-wage" (citizen-wage) concept is central to economic role of capitalist state

**Rationale**: Since Tonak's work classifies state revenues/expenditures and criticizes social wage approach (Chapter V), useful to see how Gough, Gintis, Bowles position themselves

**Historical Context**:
- Marx and Engels did NOT systematically develop theory of capitalist state (except critical writings on Hegel, newspaper articles, Engels' studies on state as institution)
- Until 1960s, Marxist theory remained confined to:
  - General judgments on state's role as superstructural institution
  - Repressive apparatus of ruling classes

### The Miliband-Poulantzas Debate (Pages 15-17)

**Starting Point** (Late 1960s): "Miliband-Foulantzas" debate - various schools clustering around **'relative autonomy of the state'** concept

**Schools Developed**:
1. 'Capital-logic' school
2. O'Connor's 'fiscal crisis' school
3. These debates still taking place

**Derivation of 'Relative Autonomy' Concept**:

Can be derived from Marx's political writings, such as **The 18th Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte**. However:

**Tonak's Critique**: These are "conjectural analyses" where state's 'independent' character "has not been evaluated in connection with the material base"

**Key Argument** (Page 16):
> "attributing to Marx the concept of the capitalist state with a relative autonomy of undetermined limitations is to misunderstand or neglect his approach of social totality rooted in the determinism of the economy."

**Marx's 1875 Critique of 'Free State'** (Page 16):

Marx criticizes German Workers' Party concept of 'free state':

> "...instead of treating existing society... as the basis of the existing state, it [Gotha Programme] treats the state rather as an independent entity that possesses its own intellectual, ethical, and libertarian bases." (McLellan 1977, p. 564)

**Engels' Argument**:
> "its function is to regulate the struggle between antagonistic classes through repression and concession and thus to moderate class conflict without undermining the continued domination of the ruling class and the reproduction of the dominant mode of production"

Adopted by Lenin in **State and Revolution**

**Later Official Marxism-Leninism**:
- Dominant motif: state as **class instrument**
- Concept of state's relative autonomy "most definitely underplayed or absent"
- **General shortcoming**: Content and limits of state's **economic interventions** undertaken on behalf of bourgeoisie "quite undetermined"

**Two Main Schools of Thought**:

**First School** (Page 17):
- Miliband-Poulantzas problematic
- Neo-Ricardian approach
- 'Fiscal crisis of state' approach (basically Keynesian, developed form of economic analysis, extended to include legitimization problem)
