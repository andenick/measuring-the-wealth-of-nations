## Figure Structure

This is a **parallel two-column flowchart** showing the six-phase derivation of net-taxes for both **Gross Labor Income (GLI)** and **Gross Non-Labor Income (GNLI)**.

### Layout

The figure consists of two parallel vertical flows:
- **LEFT COLUMN**: Gross Labor Income (GLI) derivation
- **RIGHT COLUMN**: Gross Non-Labor Income (GNLI) derivation

Each column flows through six phases (I through VI), with boxes connected by arrows showing the transformation of income categories as taxes and expenditures are applied.

**Note**: These ratios demonstrate the distributional impact of state fiscal activities in 1964.

---

## Key Features of the Diagram

1. **Parallel Processing**: Both labor and non-labor income streams are processed through identical phases but with different values

2. **Phase Progression**:
   - Phase I: Correction for NIPA's treatment of indirect business taxes
   - Phase II: Allocation of taxes between labor and non-labor
   - Phase III: Calculation of after-tax income
   - Phase IV: Allocation of government expenditures and benefits
   - Phase V: Derivation of net-tax (taxes minus benefits)
   - Phase VI: Final after-net-tax income

3. **Sign Convention**:
   - Labor column: Net tax is **positive** (+10.79), meaning workers pay more than they receive
   - Non-Labor column: Net tax is **positive** (+0.79), meaning capital also pays more than it receives

4. **1964 Benchmark Year**: All values are in billions of dollars for the year 1964, used as a representative year to demonstrate the methodology

---

## Interpretation

### Labor's Net Position (1964)
- Gross Labor Income: $520.09 billion (implied)
- Taxes Paid: $99.50 billion
- Benefits Received: $109.76 billion
- **Net Tax**: +$10.79 billion (workers subsidize state by $10.79B)
- After-Tax Income: $420.59 billion
- After-Net-Tax Income: $430.38 billion

### Non-Labor's Net Position (1964)
- Gross Non-Labor Income: Approximately $697 billion (1.34 × $520B)
- Final After-Net-Tax Income: $340.17 billion

### Key Finding
Despite receiving substantial benefits ($109.76B), workers still paid **more in taxes** ($99.50B base + additional amounts) than they received, resulting in a positive net-tax of $10.79 billion. This supports Tonak's finding that workers subsidize the state rather than the state subsidizing workers.

---

## Methodological Significance

This diagram is the **visual centerpiece** of Tonak's empirical methodology. It shows:

1. How NIPA categories are transformed through successive adjustments
2. The allocation methodology applied at each stage
3. The parallel treatment of labor vs. capital income
4. The quantitative impact of state fiscal activities on distribution

The six-phase structure provides a **systematic, replicable framework** for calculating net-taxes that can be applied to any year of data, not just 1964.
