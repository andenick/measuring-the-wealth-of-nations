## Figure 1: Marx's Hierarchical Structure of Social Spheres (1846)

**Location:** Page 11 (PDF page 4)
**Type:** Hierarchical diagram
**Source:** Based on Marx's December 28, 1846 letter to Annenkov

**Description:**

A simple three-level hierarchical flowchart showing the relationship between economic base and superstructure in Marx's early materialist conception:

```
┌─────────────────────┐
│  Economic Instance  │
└──────────┬──────────┘
           │
           ▼
┌─────────────────────┐
│   Civil Society     │
└──────────┬──────────┘
           │
           ▼
┌─────────────────────┐
│  Political System   │
└─────────────────────┘
```

**Context:**

This diagram illustrates Marx's 1846 conception of the relationship between the economic base, civil society, and the political system (state). Each level is connected by downward arrows indicating determination or foundation.

The diagram appears in the context of Tonak's discussion of Marx's early formulation of historical materialism, where Marx writes to Annenkov:

> "Assume a particular level of development of a man's productive forces and you will get a particular form of commerce and consumption. Assume particular stages of development in production, commerce and consumption and you will have a corresponding social system, a corresponding organization of the family of social estates or of classes in a word, a corresponding civil society. Assume such a civil society and you will get a political system appropriate to it, a system which is only the official expression of civil society."

**Significance:**

1. **Early Base-Superstructure Theory:** Shows Marx's initial formulation of base-superstructure distinction in 1846, before the mature formulation in the 1854 Contribution

2. **Hierarchical Determination:** The downward arrows indicate that the economic instance (mode of production) determines civil society, which in turn determines the political system

3. **Methodological Foundation:** This early hierarchical conception contrasts with Marx's later, more complex materialist formulation where economic structure is distinguished from legal/political superstructure

4. **Evolution of Theory:** Tonak uses this to show how Marx's conception of the state evolved from this simple hierarchical model to a more sophisticated treatment in the Contribution and Capital

5. **Plan Problem Context:** The diagram helps situate the "plan problem" debate - showing that Marx's early plans for analyzing the state were embedded in this hierarchical framework
