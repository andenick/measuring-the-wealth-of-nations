### Key Patterns and Trends

**Absolute Growth**:
- Total taxes: $90.1B (1952) → $839.2B (1980) = **9.3x increase**
- Labor taxes: $34.6B (1952) → $456.4B (1980) = **13.2x increase**
- Non-labor taxes: $55.5B (1952) → $382.8B (1980) = **6.9x increase**

**Proportional Trends**:
- 1952: Labor 38.4%, Non-labor 61.6%
- 1960: Labor 43.0%, Non-labor 57.0%
- 1970: Labor 50.0%, Non-labor 50.0% ← **CROSSOVER POINT**
- 1980: Labor 54.4%, Non-labor 45.6%

**Critical Finding**: **Labor's share of tax burden increased from 38% to 54%** over the 1952-1980 period, despite labor share of income remaining stable around 72-73%. This represents a **regressive shift** in the tax structure, with labor bearing an increasing proportion of the total tax burden.

**1970 Milestone**: First year where labor and non-labor paid approximately equal amounts in absolute taxes ($151.39B vs $151.49B), despite labor earning 74% of income.

### Implications

1. **Increasing Tax Burden on Labor**: Labor's tax payments grew faster (13.2x) than non-labor's (6.9x)

2. **Reflects Payroll Tax Growth**: Growth of Social Insurance contributions (100% allocated to labor) from 10% to 24% of total revenue

3. **Corporate Tax Decline Impact**: Fall of corporate taxes (100% allocated to non-labor) from 22% to 10% reduced non-labor's burden

4. **Sets Stage for NSW Calculation**: This table provides the "T" (taxes) component needed for Net Social Wage = Benefits - Taxes

### Trend Analysis

**Period 1: 1952-1966** (Stability)
- Average: 27% of GNP
- Range: 25-28%
- Interpretation: Post-war stabilization, government sector constant relative to economy

**Period 2: 1967-1980** (Expansion)
- Average: 31-32% of GNP
- Range: 30-35%
- Peak: 1975 (35% - recession year)
- Interpretation: Government sector grew as share of economy

**Overall Trend**: Government expenditures increased from 27% (1952-1966 average) to 32% (1967-1980 average) of GNP, representing a **5 percentage point increase**.

### Key Insights

**Footnote b** (page 99) provides crucial context:
> "According to Paul Mattick, who considers all government expenditures as unproductive expenditures, the ratio of total government expenditures to gross national product excluding government expenditures is a more accurate indicator for the increasing role of the State in capitalist economy."

**Alternative Measure**: Column (2)-(1) shows net GNP
- This ratio increased from 0.37 (1952) to 0.50 (1980)
- Suggests government absorbed an increasing share of *productive* output

### Significance for NSW Analysis

This table contextualizes the expenditure analysis that follows. The growth of government spending created the *potential* for increased benefits to labor, but whether workers actually received these benefits (rather than just paying increased taxes) is the empirical question Tables IX-X will answer.

## Table VII: Government Expenditures by Function

**Location**: Pages 102-103 (referenced on page 101)
**Time Period**: 1952 and 1960 (selected benchmark years)
**Purpose**: Functional classification for benefit allocation

### Expenditure Categories (21 Functions)

**FEDERAL Functions**:

1. **Central executive, legislative, and judicial**: Administration
2. **International Affairs**: Excluded from NSW (non-militaristic foreign aid)
3. **Space**: Excluded from NSW
4. **National Defense**: Excluded from NSW (military)
5. **Civilian safety**: Police, fire, corrections
6. **Education**: Elementary, secondary, higher
7. **Health and hospitals**: Infrastructure-based services
8. **Income support, social security, and welfare**: Direct benefits
9. **Veterans benefits and services**: **Excluded** from NSW
10. **Housing and community services**: Urban renewal, public housing
11. **Recreational and cultural activities**: Parks, libraries

**STATE and LOCAL Functions**:

12. **Transportation**: Highways, air, transit, railroad
13. **Postal service**: Mail delivery
14. **Economic development, regulation**: Business assistance
15. **Labor training and services**: Job programs
16. **Natural resources**: Conservation, land management
17. **Agriculture**: Farm subsidies (**excluded** - goes to capitalist farmers)
18. **Energy**: Power generation
19. **Commercial activities**: Government enterprises
20. **Net interest paid**: Debt service (**excluded**)
21. **Other and unallocable**: Miscellaneous

### Allocation Logic (from page 101)

**Two Types of State Activities**:

**Type 1**: Activities that "directly support reproduction of the working class"
- Education
- Health and hospitals
- Income support
- Housing
- Recreation
→ **Allocated to labor** (E₁ or E₂ × LS)

**Type 2**: Activities that "reproduce capital and capitalist class"
- National Defense
- International Affairs
- Agriculture subsidies
- Net Interest (faux frais for capitalist class)
→ **Excluded from labor benefits or allocated to non-labor**

**Mixed Functions**: Allocated by labor share (LS)
- Transportation (workers and businesses use)
- Education (workers' children and management training)
- Postal service
- etc.

### Exclusions from Labor Benefits

**Explicitly Excluded** (pages 106-107):
1. **Central Executive, Legislative, Judicial**: Administrative costs of state apparatus
2. **International Affairs**: Foreign policy/"protecting persons, property, and social relations" abroad
3. **Space**: Not consumption-related
4. **National Defense**: Military/"represents protection of private property"
5. **Civilian Safety**: Police, prisons = "protection of private property (persons) and social rules"
6. **Veteran Benefits**: "Current costs of the damage and destruction of was" (Shaikh 1980, p. 36)
7. **Agriculture**: Subsidies to **capitalist farmers** (price stabilization, financing)
8. **Net Interest**: Payments to highest income brackets (capitalist class)

**Quote from Marx** (page 106, footnote 22):
> "The sum of labor-power and social means of production that is spent in the annual production of gold and silver as instruments of circulation forms a heavy item for faux frais for the capitalist mode of production, or more generally for a mode of production based on commodity circulation..."

This establishes that certain state expenditures are **faux frais** (false costs) - overhead costs of maintaining the capitalist system rather than benefits to workers.
