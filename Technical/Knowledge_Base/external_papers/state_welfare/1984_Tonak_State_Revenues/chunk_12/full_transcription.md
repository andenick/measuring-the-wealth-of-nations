## The Structure of Government Expenditures

There are different ways of breaking down government expenditures to determine the components that are most responsible for the increased GNP share of the state sector in the U.S. Hyman, for example, has divided "government expenditures into four major categories for the Federal Government:

1. Purchases of goods and services (exhaustive expenditures)

2. Transfer payments to persons

3. Grants-in-aid to state and local governments

4. Net Interest paid "(Hyman, 1983, p. 21)¹⁸

Musgrave has a similar breakdown of 'Purchases versus Transfer Payments' (Musgrave and Musgrave, 1980, p. 145). He has in addition adopted another designation of expenditures, that of 'Defense versus Civilian Expenditures'. The rationale behind the latter was to determine whether the "...expenditure growth is to be explained in terms of expenditures for defense, or whether it applies to civilian expenditures as well." (Musgrave and Musgrave, 1980, p. 144)¹⁹

A third breakdown of government expenditures is known as the functional classification and is better than the previous two in allocating incomes and benefits received from the state to labor and non-labor segments.

### 4. Method of Allocation of Government Expenditures and Related Assumptions

The question to be addressed concerning total Federal, state and local expenditures involves the relationship of such expenditures to labor income and/or the consumption of workers. In other words, all of the 21 categories of government expenditures must be allocated to the labor and non-labor segments of society depending upon their content. At a general level, we can regard state activities expenditures as directed toward securing the conditions of social reproduction. Within this, we can designate two types of activities. One set are those in which the state directly supports reproduction of the working class; a second set of activities reproduces capital and capitalist class. For our purposes we focus on the first set of activities which directly support labor income and consumption though various kinds of government expenditures. ## Central Executive, legislative and judicial activities

are excluded because they represent an absorption of taxes by the costs of central administration, management, tax collection financial management and legislative and Judicial activities. They are neither a form of labor income nor workers' consumption; rather, they are largely the requirements of the preservation of society's superstructural institutions.

## International affairs expenditures are also

excluded because such expenditures directed toward protecting persons, property, and the social relations in a supposedly non-militaristic manner on a world scale.

Space, national defense and civilian safety

expenditures are excluded since the first two do much the same militaristically, while the last, consisting of the police, fire and prison systems, also "represents protection of private property, (persons) and social rules and not consumption of any use-values as such" (Shaikh, 1980, p. 36)

Veteran benefits and service are also excluded from labor income or consumption, since medical and insurance expenses represent "the current costs of the damage and destruction of was" (Shaikh, 1980, p. 36).

Agriculture consists of various forms of subsidies and financing to capitalist farmers, such as stabilization of fram prices and income" and "financing farm ownership and utilities". Hence it cannot be considered a part of labor income or consumption derived from government expenditures.

Net interest paid, and others and unallocables, in the form of net interest paid as such or in the form of general revenue sharing, "largely represent payments to the highest income brackets" (Shaikh, 1980, p. 36) and hence are excluded from labor income and consumption.

### Methodological Framework (Pages 100-107)

**Two Types of State Activities**:
1. Support working class reproduction → Included in benefits
2. Reproduce capital/capitalist class → Excluded from benefits

**9 Categories Excluded from Labor Benefits**:
1. Central executive, legislative, judicial (administration)
2. International affairs (foreign policy)
3. Space (not consumption)
4. National defense (military)
5. Civilian safety (police, prisons)
6. Veteran benefits (war costs)
7. Agriculture (capitalist farmer subsidies)
8. Net interest (payments to wealthy)
9. Other and unallocable

**Rationale**: These are **faux frais** - overhead costs of capitalism, not benefits to workers

### Key Findings

- **Tax Burden Shift**: Labor's share of taxes rose from 38% (1952) to 54% (1980)
- **Government Growth**: Expenditures rose from 27% to 33% of GNP
- **1970 Crossover**: First year labor paid equal absolute taxes to non-labor despite earning 74% of income
