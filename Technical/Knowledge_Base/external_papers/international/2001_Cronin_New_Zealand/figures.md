# Figures from Cronin (2001)

## Figure 1: Relationship between conventional national accounts and classical categories assuming all activity of firms is productive (Page 6)

**Type:** Conceptual diagram

**Description:** Shows the mapping between National Accounts categories and Classical Categories:

**National Accounts:**
- Intermediate Production
- Value Added:
  - i. Depreciation
  - ii. Salaries and Wages
  - iii. Operating Surplus
  - iv. Indirect Taxes
  - v. Subsidies
- Imports
- **Total Output**

**Classical Categories:**
- Constant Capital
- Variable Capital
- Surplus-Value
- **Total Value**

## Figure 2: Derivation of classical production and trade categories (Page 7)

**Type:** Grid diagram

**Description:** Shows the structure dividing:
- **Production** sectors: a-g (agricultural production, manufacturing, productive services, transportation)
- **Trade** sectors: h-k (wholesale trade, retail trade, building and equipment rental)
- Rows: Intermediate Production/Trade, Depreciation, Salaries and Wages, Operating Surplus, Indirect Taxes and Subsidies
- **Total Value** spans production and trade sectors

---

## Figure 3: Value and secondary/fictitious categories (Page 8)

**Type:** Extended grid diagram

**Description:** Expands Figure 2 to include:
- **Production** (a-g)
- **Trade** (h-k)
- **Royalties**

## Figure 4: Components of value (Page 10)

**Type:** Grid diagram with classical categories overlay

**Description:** Maps National Accounts components to classical value categories:
- Intermediate Production/Trade → **Constant Capital**
- Depreciation, Salaries and Wages → **Variable Capital**

## Figure 5: Estimates of rate of surplus-value post-1970 (Page 12)

**Type:** Line graph

**X-axis:** Years 1972-1988

**Y-axis:** Percentage (0%-300%)

**Lines:**
- NZ (Current Study): Rises from ~200% (1972) to ~275% (1988), with dip to ~165% (1975)
- USA (Shaikh & Tonak, 1994): Relatively stable around 200-225%

**Key finding:** New Zealand shows greater volatility and higher final rate than USA

---

## Figure 6: Estimates of value composition of capital post-1970 (Page 12)

**Type:** Line graph

**X-axis:** Years 1972-1988

**Y-axis:** Percentage (0%-450%)

**Lines:**
- NZ (Current Study): Rises from ~300% (1972) to ~410% (1985), then declines to ~360% (1988)
- USA (Shaikh & Tonak, 1994): Gradually rises from ~240% to ~295%

**Key finding:** New Zealand has significantly higher value composition of capital than USA

## Figure 8: Estimates of value composition of capital pre-1970 (Page 13)

**Type:** Line graph

## Figure 9: Rate of surplus-value and profit/wage ratio (Page 14)

**Type:** Line graph

**X-axis:** Years 1972-1993

**Y-axis:** Percentage (0%-300%)

**Lines:**
- s/v (Rate of surplus-value): Varies from ~165% to ~285%
- os/ec (Operating surplus/Employment compensation): Stable around 50-75%

**Key finding:** Conventional profit/wage ratio is poor proxy for rate of surplus-value

**Legend:**
- s/v = Rate of surplus-value
- os = Operating surplus
- ec = Employment compensation

---

## Figure 10: Accumulation of surplus-value (Page 16)

**Type:** Line graph

**X-axis:** Years 1972-1992

**Y-axis:** Percentage (0%-45%)

**Line:** s/TV (Surplus-value/Total Value)

**Pattern:** Declines from ~34% (1972) to ~29% (1975), recovers to ~30-32% (1976-1984), rises to ~38% (1987-1992)

**Key finding:** Shows the accumulation rate declined 1972-1983, recovered 1984-1989, then leveled out

**Legend:**
- s = surplus-value
- TV = total value

---

## Figure 11: Rate of surplus-value and value composition of capital (Page 16)

**Type:** Line graph

**X-axis:** Years 1972-1992

**Y-axis:** Percentage (0%-450%)

**Lines:**
- c/v (Value composition of capital): Rises from ~290% to ~407% (1985), then declines to ~360%
- s/v (Rate of surplus-value): Dips from ~205% to ~169% (1975), then rises to ~280%

**Key finding:** Shows inverse relationship between the two measures during 1985-1989 period

**Legend:**
- c/v = value composition of capital
- s/v = rate of surplus-value

---

## Figure 12: Accumulation rate and rate of surplus-value (Page 17)

**Type:** Indexed line graph (1972=100)

**X-axis:** Years 1972-1993

**Y-axis:** Index (20-160, base 1972=100)

**Lines:**
- s/TV (Accumulation rate): Varies 80-110 index points
- s/v (Rate of surplus-value): Rises from 100 to 145

**Key finding:** Shows tracking of both measures until 1984-87, then divergence

**Legend:**
- s/v = rate of surplus-value
- s/TV = surplus-value/total value or accumulation rate

---

## Figure 13: Classical and conventional measures of output and wages (Page 17)

**Type:** Indexed line graph (1972=100)

**X-axis:** Years 1972-1992

**Y-axis:** Index (100-220, base 1972=100)

**Lines:**
- w'/v (Near-total wages/Variable capital): Rises from ~167 to ~203
- GO/TV (Gross output/Total value): Rises from ~120 to ~132

**Key finding:** Growth of unproductive activity shown by faster growth of total wages vs. productive wages

**Legend:**
- GO = gross output
- TV = total value
- v = variable capital
- w' = near-total wages (employee compensation plus wage & salary equivalent for productive self-employed)

---

## Figure 14: Classical and conventional measures of value added and surplus (Page 18)

**Type:** Indexed line graph (1972=100)

**X-axis:** Years 1972-1992

**Y-axis:** Percentage (0%-140%)

**Lines:**
- GDP/VA* (Gross domestic product/Classical value added): Relatively stable 105-120%
- os/s (Operating surplus/Surplus-value): Declines from ~68% to ~47%, then rises to ~62%

**Key finding:** Conventional GDP can grow faster than classical value-added only when operating surplus grows faster than unproductive activity

**Legend:**
- GDP = gross domestic product
- VA* = classical value added (total value less constant capital)
- os = operating surplus
- s = surplus-value
