# SUMMARY: Cronin (2001) - New Zealand Classical Economic Analysis

## Document Overview
**Title:** Productive and Unproductive Capital: A mapping of the New Zealand system of national accounts to classical economic categories, 1972-95

**Author:** Bruce Cronin (Massey University)

**Publication:** Review of Political Economy, Vol. 13, No. 3, 2001, pp. 309-327

**DOI:** 10.1080/09538250120055168

---

## Research Question
Does the classical Marxian framework reveal different insights about New Zealand's neo-liberal economic reforms (mid-1980s onward) compared to conventional neoclassical accounts?

---

## Methodology
- **Framework:** Shaikh & Tonak (1994) methodology for mapping national accounts to classical categories
- **Data:** New Zealand System of National Accounts (NZSNA) + Inter-Industry Studies
- **Period:** 1972-1995 (24 years)
- **Approach:** Output-side analysis of 25 production groups
- **Key Innovation:** Simplified approach using primarily annual NZSNA data

---

## Main Findings

### 1. Three Distinct Periods

**1972-1983: Profitability Crisis**
- Accumulation rate (s/TV) fell from 34% to 31%
- Value composition of capital (c/v) rose 20%
- Rate of surplus-value (s/v) stable around 200%
- **Interpretation:** Classic falling rate of profit scenario

**1984-1989: Neo-Liberal Recovery**
- Accumulation rate rose to 38%
- Rate of surplus-value jumped 37% (to 280%)
- Value composition of capital fell 12% (recession write-offs)
- **Interpretation:** Labor exploitation-driven recovery

**1990-1995: Stagnation Despite Reforms**
- Accumulation rate stalled at 38%
- Rate of surplus-value continued rising (to 307%)
- Value composition of capital increased again
- **Interpretation:** Rising exploitation couldn't offset rising capital composition

### 2. Growth of Unproductive Activity
- Gross output grew faster than total value (classical measure)
- Unproductive employment expanded significantly
- Financial sector expansion key driver
- **Result:** Appearance of growth masking productive stagnation

### 3. Conventional Measures Misleading
- Operating surplus/wage ratio poor proxy for rate of surplus-value (off by 60-70%)
- GDP growth overstates productive economy health
- "Economic fundamentals" claimed by reformers reflect unproductive sector expansion

### 4. Comparison with USA
- New Zealand shows higher value composition of capital (290-410% vs 240-300%)
- More volatile rate of surplus-value
- Similar patterns but more extreme

## Critical Insights

### The Neo-Liberal Paradox Explained
**Conventional View:** Reforms improved "economic fundamentals" but productivity mysteriously stagnated

**Classical View:**
- Reforms increased labor exploitation (rate of surplus-value up 49%)
- But expanded unproductive sectors (finance, trade, administration)
- Unproductive sector growth created GDP growth illusion
- Productive capacity remained weak, explaining productivity stagnation

### Methodological Contributions

**Problems with Conventional Proxies:**
- Profit/wage ratio ≠ rate of surplus-value (off by factor of 3-4)
- GDP ≠ productive value creation
- Gross output heavily influenced by unproductive activity

**Problems with Earlier Studies (Pearce 1986):**
- Confused productive/unproductive labor distinction
- Used capital stocks instead of flows (depreciation)
- Result: Underestimated exploitation rate by 66%, overestimated capital composition by 600-800%

3. **Labor Market:** Deregulation increased exploitation rate but not productive capacity
4. **Measurement:** Conventional indicators mislead about economic health

## One-Sentence Summary
**Classical analysis reveals that New Zealand's neo-liberal reforms generated apparent growth through expanded unproductive activity and increased labor exploitation rather than genuine productive capacity expansion, explaining persistent productivity stagnation despite "improved economic fundamentals."**

## Key Contextual Notes

### New Zealand as Paradigmatic Case
- **Timeline:** Reforms began mid-1980s under Labour government (Roger Douglas)
- **Scope:** Deregulation, privatization, labor market liberalization, financial sector opening
- **International Significance:** Served as model for neo-liberal reforms elsewhere
- **Political Impact:** 4 Prime Ministers and 3 Finance Ministers in 3 years (early 1990s)

### Persistent Problems (Despite "Right Fundamentals")
- High unemployment
- Highest real interest rates in world
- Volatile nominal interest rates and business confidence
- Deteriorating balance of payments
- Productivity stagnation (the key puzzle)

### The Classical Answer
All these "anomalies" explained by:
- Weak productive sector (low accumulation rate)
- High value composition of capital (capital-intensive production)
- Unproductive sector expansion (draining surplus)
- Exploitation-based rather than productivity-based growth
