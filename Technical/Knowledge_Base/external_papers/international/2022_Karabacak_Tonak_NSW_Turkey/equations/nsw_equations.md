# Equations - 2022 Karabacak & Tonak NSW Turkey Study

**Source**: Karabacak, Z., & Tonak, E. A. (2022). The Net Social Wage in Turkey, 1980–2019. *Review of Radical Political Economics*, 54(4), 577-605.
**Extraction Date**: October 23, 2025
**Section Focus**: Mathematical framework for NSW calculation in Turkey

---

## Core NSW Definition

### Equation 1: Net Social Wage (Basic Formula)

```latex
NSW = B - T
```

**Where**:
- `NSW` = Net Social Wage (net fiscal transfer to labor)
- `B` = Total benefits and transfer payments received by labor from the state
- `T` = Total taxes paid by labor to the state

**Interpretation**:
- If `NSW > 0`: Workers receive more from state than they pay (net beneficiaries)
- If `NSW < 0`: Workers pay more to state than they receive (net contributors/subsidizers)
- If `NSW = 0`: Workers break even (taxes equal benefits)

**Key Finding for Turkey**:
Average NSW = **-1.13% of GDP** (1980-2019)
- Workers subsidize the state
- No evidence of "welfare state" or "social assistance state"

## Circuit of Capital Framework

### Equation 2: Private Circuit of Capital

```latex
M_p - C_p \left\{ \begin{matrix} LP_p \\ MP_p \end{matrix} \right\} ... P_p ... C'_p - M'_p
```

**Where**:
- `M_p` = Money capital (private sector)
- `C_p` = Commodity capital (private)
- `LP_p` = Labor power purchased (private productive workers)
- `MP_p` = Means of production (private)
- `P_p` = Production process (private)
- `C'_p` = Commodity capital after production (private)
- `M'_p` = Expanded money capital (private) = M_p + ΔM_p

**Significance**: Shows how private capital accumulates through exploitation of labor power.

---

### Equation 3: State Circuit of Capital

```latex
M_s - C_s \left\{ \begin{matrix} LP_s \\ MP_s \end{matrix} \right\} ... P_s ... C'_s - M'_s
```

**Where**:
- `M_s` = Money capital (state sector)
- `C_s` = Commodity capital (state)
- `LP_s` = Labor power purchased (state workers)
- `MP_s` = Means of production (state)
- `P_s` = Production process (state)
- `C'_s` = Commodity capital after production (state)
- `M'_s` = Expanded money capital (state)

**Significance**: State operates as a form of capital, purchasing labor power and means of production.

---

### Equation 4: Total Value Creation

```latex
M' = M'_p + M'_s = C + V + S
```

**Where**:
- `M'` = Total money form of newly created value
- `C` = Constant capital (value transferred from means of production)
- `V` = Variable capital (wages of productive workers)
- `S` = Surplus value (unpaid labor appropriated by capital)

**Key Point**: Only `V + S` is newly created value; `C` is transferred value.

---

## Labor Share Calculation

### Equation 5: Labor Share (ls)

```latex
ls = \frac{V}{V + S} = \frac{\text{Wages of Productive Workers}}{\text{Net Domestic Product}}
```

**Where**:
- `ls` = Labor share (ratio of variable capital to value added)
- `V` = Variable capital (productive workers' wages)
- `S` = Surplus value (profits, rent, interest)
- `V + S` = Net Domestic Product (NDP) = newly created value

**Turkey-Specific Calculation**:
Authors calculate `ls` for Turkey 1980-2019 using:
- **Numerator**: Compensation of employees (productive sectors)
- **Denominator**: Net Domestic Product at factor cost

**Significance**: `ls` is used to allocate **mixed expenditures** between labor and capital.

---

### Equation 6: Productive Workers' Wages (V)

```latex
V = \text{Total Compensation of Employees} - \text{Unproductive Workers' Wages}
```

**Unproductive Sectors in Turkey** (excluded from V):
- Wholesale and retail trade
- Finance and insurance
- Real estate activities
- Professional/administrative services
- Public administration and defense
- Education (private schools)
- Health services (private hospitals)

**Productive Sectors** (included in V):
- Agriculture, forestry, fishing
- Mining and quarrying
- Manufacturing
- Utilities
- Construction
- Transportation and storage
- Accommodation and food services
- Information and communication

## Tax Allocation

### Equation 7: Total Taxes Paid by Labor (T)

```latex
T = T_1 + T_2 \times ls
```

**Where**:
- `T_1` = Direct taxes on labor (taxes unambiguously paid by workers)
- `T_2` = Indirect taxes and mixed taxes (allocated by labor share)
- `ls` = Labor share (proportion allocated to labor)

**Turkey-Specific Categories**:

**T_1 (Direct Labor Taxes)**:
- Personal income tax on wages and salaries
- Social security contributions (employee portion)
- Payroll taxes

**T_2 (Indirect/Mixed Taxes)**:
- Value-added tax (VAT) - 18% standard rate in Turkey
- Special consumption taxes (SCT) on fuel, tobacco, alcohol
- Customs duties
- Property taxes (allocated by income share)
- Corporate income tax (allocated by labor share of profits)

---

### Equation 8: Total Taxes Paid by Capital (T_k)

```latex
T_k = T_3 + T_2 \times (1 - ls)
```

**Where**:
- `T_3` = Direct taxes on capital (unambiguously paid by capitalists)
- `T_2 × (1 - ls)` = Capital's share of indirect/mixed taxes
- `(1 - ls)` = Capital share (proportion allocated to capital)

**T_3 (Direct Capital Taxes)**:
- Corporate income tax on retained earnings
- Property taxes on business assets
- Taxes on interest and dividends

---

## Benefit Allocation

### Equation 9: Total Benefits Received by Labor (B)

```latex
B = E_1 + E_2 \times ls
```

**Where**:
- `E_1` = Direct benefits to labor (expenditures unambiguously benefiting workers)
- `E_2` = Mixed expenditures (allocated by labor share)
- `ls` = Labor share (proportion allocated to labor)

**Turkey-Specific Categories**:

**E_1 (Direct Labor Benefits)**:
- Social security pensions (retirement, disability, survivor)
- Unemployment insurance benefits
- Health insurance subsidies for workers
- Education expenditures (public schools, universities)
- Social assistance (cash transfers to poor families)
- Housing subsidies for low-income workers

**E_2 (Mixed Expenditures)**:
- General public services (allocated by ls)
- Defense and public order (allocated by ls)
- Infrastructure (roads, utilities - allocated by ls)
- Environmental protection
- Culture and recreation

---

### Equation 10: Total Benefits Received by Capital (B_k)

```latex
B_k = E_3 + E_2 \times (1 - ls)
```

**Where**:
- `E_3` = Direct benefits to capital (expenditures unambiguously benefiting capitalists)
- `E_2 × (1 - ls)` = Capital's share of mixed expenditures

**E_3 (Direct Capital Benefits)**:
- Export subsidies and incentives
- Investment incentives and tax breaks
- Agricultural subsidies to large farmers
- R&D grants to corporations
- Infrastructure serving business (ports, airports)

---

## NSW Expanded Formula

### Equation 11: NSW (Expanded)

Combining Equations 7 and 9:

```latex
NSW = B - T = (E_1 + E_2 \times ls) - (T_1 + T_2 \times ls)
```

Rearranging:

```latex
NSW = (E_1 - T_1) + ls \times (E_2 - T_2)
```

**Interpretation**:
- `(E_1 - T_1)` = Net impact of clearly-allocated fiscal items
- `ls × (E_2 - T_2)` = Net impact of mixed fiscal items allocated to labor

**Turkey Results** (average 1980-2019):
- `(E_1 - T_1)` ≈ positive but small (direct benefits > direct taxes)
- `ls × (E_2 - T_2)` ≈ large negative (indirect taxes > allocated benefits)
- **Total NSW** ≈ -1.13% of GDP (negative)

## NSW as Ratio

### Equation 12: NSW Ratio

```latex
NSW\_Ratio = \frac{NSW}{GDP} = \frac{B - T}{GDP}
```

**Turkey Time Series** (selected years):
- 1980: -1.02% of GDP
- 1990: -0.75% of GDP
- 2001: -1.05% of GDP (crisis year)
- 2010: -0.85% of GDP (post-crisis recovery)
- 2019: -2.31% of GDP (AKP austerity period)

**Average 1980-2019**: -1.13% of GDP

---

## Decomposition by Component

### Equation 13: Benefit Ratio

```latex
Benefit\_Ratio = \frac{B}{GDP} = \frac{E_1 + E_2 \times ls}{GDP}
```

**Turkey Average 1980-2019**: ~7.5% of GDP

---

### Equation 14: Tax Ratio

```latex
Tax\_Ratio = \frac{T}{GDP} = \frac{T_1 + T_2 \times ls}{GDP}
```

**Turkey Average 1980-2019**: ~8.63% of GDP

---

### Equation 15: NSW Decomposition

```latex
NSW\_Ratio = Benefit\_Ratio - Tax\_Ratio
```

**Turkey Average**:
```
-1.13% = 7.5% - 8.63%
```

**Interpretation**: Workers receive 7.5% of GDP in benefits but pay 8.63% in taxes, resulting in net subsidy to state of 1.13% of GDP.

## Effective Variable Capital

### Equation 16: Adjusted Variable Capital

```latex
V_{eff} = V_{nom} - (V_L - V_{st})
```

**Where**:
- `V_eff` = Effective variable capital (wage after state redistribution)
- `V_nom` = Nominal variable capital (wage before state intervention)
- `V_L` = Taxes paid by productive workers
- `V_st` = Benefits received by productive workers

**Significance**: Shows how state redistribution affects the **real wage** of productive workers.

**Turkey Finding**: V_eff < V_nom for entire period (state reduces real wage)

---

## Effective Surplus Value

### Equation 17: Adjusted Surplus Value

```latex
S_{eff} = S_{nom} + (V_L - V_{st}) + (W_L - W_{st})
```

**Where**:
- `S_eff` = Effective surplus value (after state redistribution)
- `S_nom` = Nominal surplus value (before state intervention)
- `(V_L - V_st)` = Net tax paid by productive workers
- `(W_L - W_st)` = Net tax paid by unproductive workers

**Significance**: State taxation effectively transfers value from labor to capital (via state).

**Turkey Finding**: S_eff > S_nom for entire period (state increases surplus value available to capital)

---

## Rate of Surplus Value

### Equation 18: Rate of Exploitation (Adjusted)

```latex
s/v = \frac{S_{eff}}{V_{eff}}
```

**Where**:
- `s/v` = Rate of surplus value (rate of exploitation)
- `S_eff` = Effective surplus value (after state redistribution)
- `V_eff` = Effective variable capital (after state redistribution)

**Comparison**:

**Without State Adjustment**:
```latex
s/v_{nom} = \frac{S_{nom}}{V_{nom}}
```

**With State Adjustment**:
```latex
s/v_{eff} = \frac{S_{nom} + (V_L - V_{st}) + (W_L - W_{st})}{V_{nom} - (V_L - V_{st})}
```

**Turkey Finding**: `s/v_eff > s/v_nom` for all years

State intervention **increases the rate of exploitation** by reducing effective wages and increasing effective surplus value.

---

## Relationship to GDP Growth

### Equation 19: Growth and NSW (Empirical Test)

The paper tests whether NSW affects GDP growth:

```latex
\Delta GDP_t = f(NSW_{t-1}, X_t)
```

**Where**:
- `ΔGDP_t` = GDP growth rate at time t
- `NSW_{t-1}` = Lagged NSW ratio
- `X_t` = Control variables (investment rate, trade, etc.)

**Finding**: No significant correlation between NSW and GDP growth in Turkey.

**Interpretation**: Contradicts **Keynesian** claim that state redistribution to workers stimulates growth.
