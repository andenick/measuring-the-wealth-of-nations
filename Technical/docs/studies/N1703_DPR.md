## Source Context

Cronin (2001) applies the Shaikh-Tonak (ST) methodology to New Zealand's system of national accounts (NZSNA), mapping 25 production groups to classical Marxian categories over 1972-1995. The value composition of capital (c/v) measures the ratio of constant capital to variable capital — the classical index of capital intensity in production. Key finding: c/v rose 20% during the 1972-1983 profitability crisis, fell 12% in the 1984-1989 neo-liberal recovery (largely due to recession write-offs), then rose again through 1990-1995. New Zealand's c/v (290-410%) is notably higher than comparable US estimates (240-300%), indicating more capital-intensive production. Data quality: 85% (official government statistics, consistent 24-year time series, transparent replicable methodology).
