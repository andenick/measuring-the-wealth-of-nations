## Source Context

Cronin (2001) applies the Shaikh-Tonak (ST) methodology to New Zealand's system of national accounts (NZSNA), mapping 25 production groups to classical Marxian categories over 1972-1995. Total Value (TV) is the classical analogue to GDP: it measures c + v + s — the sum of constant capital consumed, variable capital, and surplus value generated in the productive sector. A key finding is that gross output grew faster than TV over the period, meaning unproductive activity (finance, trade, administration) expanded relative to the productive core. TV grew 1083% from 1972 to 1995 (vs gross output growth of 1196%), quantifying the productive-unproductive divergence. Data quality: 85% (official government statistics, consistent 24-year time series, transparent replicable methodology).
