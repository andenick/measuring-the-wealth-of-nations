## Source Context

Cronin (2001) applies the Shaikh-Tonak (ST) methodology to New Zealand's system of national accounts (NZSNA), mapping 25 production groups to classical Marxian categories over 1972-1995. The rate of surplus value (s/v) measures the ratio of surplus value to variable capital — the key index of labor exploitation in the ST framework. Three distinct phases are identified: a profitability crisis (1972-1983) with s/v stable around 200%; a neo-liberal recovery (1984-1989) in which s/v jumped 37% to 280%; and stagnation (1990-1995) in which s/v continued rising to 307% without translating into productive expansion. Data quality: 85% (official government statistics, consistent 24-year time series, transparent replicable methodology).
