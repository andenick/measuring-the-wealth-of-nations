## Description

Figure 9.3 plots the rates of productive consumption over 1948-1989, expressing productive consumption as a ratio to relevant denominators such as total product or capital stock. These rates measure the intensity of productive activity — how rapidly the economy is using up means of production and labor power relative to its total output or accumulated capital. A declining rate of productive consumption indicates that a growing share of economic activity is devoted to unproductive functions (circulation, administration, finance), which absorb surplus value without contributing to the reproduction of capital. This figure connects the structural shift toward unproductive activity (documented in Chapter 5) to the accumulation dynamics analyzed in Chapter 9.

## Replication Notes

The rate of productive consumption may be defined as productive consumption divided by total product (T501) or by capital stock. T512 contains various Marxian ratio series that likely include this rate. T506 (rate of surplus value) provides context for interpreting whether changes in productive consumption rates reflect changes in exploitation or in the allocation of surplus.
