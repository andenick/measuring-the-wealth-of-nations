## Description

Figure 6.1 compares alternative measures of the real wage over 1952-1989. The conventional real wage deflates nominal wages by a consumer price index, while the Marxian measure relates variable capital (V*) to the labor content of the consumption bundle or to the net social wage framework developed in Chapter 6. The figure demonstrates that different deflation methods and different definitions of the wage (gross vs net of taxes and transfers, inclusive vs exclusive of benefits) yield substantially different pictures of workers' living standards. This is analytically significant because it shows that the apparent postwar rise in real wages may be overstated by conventional measures that ignore the growing tax burden on workers and the distinction between productive and unproductive labor compensation.
