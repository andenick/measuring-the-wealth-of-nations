## Description

Figure 9.4 charts the rates of capital accumulation over 1948-1989, measuring the pace at which surplus value is reinvested to expand the productive capital stock. The Marxian rate of accumulation is the ratio of net investment in productive capital to the existing capital stock, and it is jointly determined by the rate of profit (T513) and the share of profit reinvested (as opposed to consumed unproductively or transferred to unproductive sectors). This figure is central to Chapter 9's argument that the postwar decline in the rate of profit (documented via T513 and T514) translated into a declining rate of accumulation, with consequences for employment, growth, and economic crises. The comparison between Marxian and conventional accumulation rates reveals how orthodox measures can mask the underlying slowdown by including unproductive investment.

## Replication Notes

The rate of accumulation = net productive investment / productive capital stock. This can be decomposed as (rate of profit) x (share of profit reinvested). Plot the accumulation rate over time, potentially comparing Marxian and conventional variants. T513 and T514 provide the profitability context; the accumulation rate itself may be derived from these series combined with investment share data. Verify exact formula against Chapter 9 tables.
