## Description

Figure 9.2 traces productive consumption alongside the capital stock over 1948-1989, linking the flow of value through production to the accumulated stock of means of production. Productive consumption (the using-up of constant and variable capital in commodity production) drives the growth of the capital stock through net investment, while the capital stock in turn determines the scale of productive consumption in subsequent periods. This figure reveals the dynamics of expanded reproduction: whether the productive base of the economy is growing, stagnating, or shrinking relative to total economic activity. The relationship between flow (productive consumption) and stock (capital) is mediated by the rate of profit, which determines how much surplus is reinvested.
