## Description

Figure 5.5 juxtaposes the Marxian rate of profit with the conventional (orthodox) profit rate over 1948-1989. The Marxian profit rate is defined as r* = S*/(C* + V*), measuring surplus value relative to total capital advanced, while the conventional profit rate uses standard national accounts definitions of profits relative to capital stock. This comparison is analytically critical because it demonstrates how the productive/unproductive labor distinction alters the measured trajectory of profitability. Transfers of surplus value to unproductive sectors (trade, finance, real estate) are visible in orthodox accounts as "profits" but are recognized in the Marxian framework as redistributed surplus. The divergence between the two measures reveals the extent to which conventional accounting obscures the underlying dynamics of capitalist accumulation.

## Replication Notes

Plot T513 and T514 on the same axes over 1948-1989. T513 is computed as S*/(C* + V*) using Marxian aggregates, while T514 uses BEA corporate profits divided by BEA fixed asset stocks. The key analytical point is the divergence pattern: whether the Marxian rate falls more steeply, reflecting the inclusion of unproductive labor costs that orthodox measures ignore.
