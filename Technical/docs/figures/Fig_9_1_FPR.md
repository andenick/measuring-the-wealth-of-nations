## Description

Figure 9.1 decomposes total consumption into productive and unproductive components over 1948-1989. In the Marxian framework, productive consumption refers to the use of means of production (constant capital) and labor power (variable capital) in the production of commodities — it is consumption that creates new value. Unproductive consumption encompasses personal consumption by capitalists, state expenditure, and the overhead costs of circulation and finance. This figure shows the relative growth trajectories of both components, revealing whether the economy's surplus is increasingly absorbed by unproductive consumption (a drag on accumulation) or channeled back into productive activity. The rising share of unproductive consumption is a structural feature that Shaikh and Tonak link to the long-run decline in the rate of accumulation.

## Replication Notes

Productive consumption = C* (constant capital used up) + V* (variable capital). Unproductive consumption = total consumption minus productive consumption, derived from the surplus value accounts. Plot both components over time, either as levels or as shares of total product (T501). Cross-reference with Chapter 9 tables for exact definitions of consumption categories.
