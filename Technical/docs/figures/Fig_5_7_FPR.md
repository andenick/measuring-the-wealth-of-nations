## Description

Figure 5.7 presents the long-run trends of total employment, productive labor, and unproductive labor from 1948 to 1988. While total employment grows substantially over this period, the figure reveals that the growth is disproportionately concentrated in unproductive labor — employment in trade, finance, insurance, real estate, government, and supervisory functions. Productive labor (workers directly producing commodities in manufacturing, agriculture, mining, construction, and productive services) grows more slowly or stagnates. This divergence is a key structural transformation of postwar capitalism that underpins the Marxian explanation of declining profitability: a growing mass of workers must be sustained out of surplus value without contributing to its production.
