## Description

Figure 6.3 examines the relationship between the labor content of the workers' consumption bundle and its exchange value (monetary cost) over 1952-1989. In Marxian theory, the value of labor power is determined by the labor time socially necessary to produce the goods workers consume. As productivity rises, the labor content of a given consumption bundle falls even if its monetary price rises. This figure traces both dimensions — showing that the real wage in labor-time terms may behave very differently from the real wage in money terms. The divergence between labor content and exchange value of the wage bundle is a key empirical measure of whether rising living standards come at the expense of capital (falling surplus value) or are compatible with rising exploitation (because productivity gains cheapen the wage bundle in value terms).

## Replication Notes

The labor content of the wage bundle requires converting the monetary wage into labor-time units using the monetary expression of labor time (MELT = GDP / total labor hours, or the Marxian equivalent). Exchange value is V* in nominal or real terms. Plot both series to show their divergence over time. The conversion factor between money and labor time is implicit in the T-series framework.
