## Description

Figure 5.6 compares Marxian and conventional measures of labor productivity over 1948-1989. Conventional productivity is output per worker (or per hour) using GDP and total employment. The Marxian measure restricts both numerator and denominator to the productive sphere: total product (TP*) per unit of productive labor (Lp). Because unproductive labor has grown as a share of total employment while contributing nothing to the value product in Marxian terms, the Marxian productivity measure diverges from the conventional one. This figure illustrates how the secular growth of unproductive activities creates a widening gap between the two productivity concepts, with implications for understanding the postwar productivity slowdown.

## Replication Notes

Marxian productivity = T501 / T515. The conventional productivity measure uses GDP (or gross output) divided by total employment (T511). Plot both on the same axes, indexed or in levels, to show the divergence. The gap between the two series reflects the growing share of unproductive labor in the economy.
