\begin{abstract}
We replicate and extend every empirical claim in Shaikh and Tonak's \textit{Measuring the Wealth of Nations} (1994) using a fully automated 8-phase pipeline (NickyData v6.0). The replication covers 33 book series across Chapters 2, 4--9, extended from 1948--1989 to 1948--2024 using BEA, BLS, and FRED data. We additionally replicate 8 external studies (22 N-series) spanning the US, Turkey, and New Zealand. Key findings: the rate of exploitation rises from 1.70 (1948) to 2.44 (1989) to approximately 3.59 (2024), a 111\% increase. The net social wage is negative for 92\% of years. Labor values and prices of production are highly correlated ($R^2 = 0.98$ at the 1958 benchmark). All 55 series pass 15 automated validators with 0 failures.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Shaikh and Tonak (1994) reconstruct US national accounts from a Marxian perspective, distinguishing productive from unproductive labor, computing the rate of exploitation, and deriving labor values from input-output tables. Their framework yields measures---such as the Marxian profit rate, surplus value, and net social wage---that differ substantially from orthodox national accounting.

This project (AS2) replicates every table and figure from the book and extends all extendable series through 2024. The replication is implemented as a self-contained NickyData pipeline: 67 Python scripts across 8 phases that reproduce all 55 series from source inputs without manual intervention.

\section{Marxian Categories}

The book's reconstruction maps orthodox national accounts into Marxian categories:
\begin{align}
TP^* &= \text{GO}_{\text{productive}} + \text{GO}_{\text{trading}} \\
C^*_m &= \text{Intermediate inputs of productive sectors} \\
GFP^* &= TP^* - C^*_m \\
V^* &= \text{Compensation of productive workers} \\
S^* &= GFP^* - V^* \\
e &= S^*/V^* \quad \text{(rate of exploitation)} \\
r^* &= S^*/(C^*_m + K^*) \quad \text{(Marxian profit rate)}
\end{align}

Sector classification follows Appendix B: 75 productive, 7 unproductive (SIC); 48 productive, 12 unproductive (NAICS).

\section{Extension Methodology}

All series are extended using growth-rate splicing at 1989:
\[
T_{\text{ext}}(t) = T_{\text{book}}(1989) \times \frac{\text{Source}_{\text{ext}}(t)}{\text{Source}_{\text{ext}}(1989)}
\]
This preserves the book's level while applying the dynamics of modern API data. Extension faithfulness scores range from 60\% (T509, productive investment) to 94\% (T601--T606, tax/benefit components).

The SIC--NAICS transition (1990--1997) is bridged via log-linear interpolation for industry-level data and continuous BLS CES coverage for employment ratios.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Rate of Exploitation}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{../Figures/Fig_5_5_exploitation_rate.png}
\caption{Rate of exploitation $e = S^*/V^*$, 1948--2024. Book period (blue), extension (orange).}
\end{figure}

The exploitation rate rises from 1.70 (1948) to 2.44 (1989, book endpoint) to approximately 3.59 (2024). The 111\% increase is driven almost entirely by the declining productive labor share ($L_p/L$: 0.57 to 0.25), not by wage differentials ($ec_u/ec_p \approx 1.0$ throughout).

\subsection{Labor Values and Prices of Production}

The cross-sectional regression of total prices of production on total labor values yields $R^2 = 0.98$ at the 1958 benchmark (slope = 0.999), confirming Shaikh's empirical claim that labor values predict market prices with high accuracy. Across all 6 SIC benchmark years (1947--1977), $R^2$ ranges from 0.70 to 0.98.

python run.py --test-all
